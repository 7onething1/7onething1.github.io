What the number means

Margin is the whole verdict. It is the share of one channel's attacks that land on an attack in the other channel, minus the identical test with that channel pushed +5 seconds. A raw coverage percentage with no control is what produced the earlier “not separable” reading on a song that separates at 79.6.

Six of these cannot be fixed by any method or any purchase. Three are mono, with the two channels correlating 0.85 to 0.90, so the second part is not in the file. Two are unison, both channels playing the same attacks. One fails its own control. No separator, paid or free, recovers what was never recorded apart.

Why the existing tabs cannot be split note by note

The obvious move is to take the tab we already have and hand each written note to whichever channel played it. That was tried and it does not survive its own control. Every position on Kilgore Trout's 449-position rhythm staff was scored, with the offset searched over ±3 seconds at 10 ms steps:

Loosening the window raises the real score and the control together. A 90% match at 200 ms is density, not alignment. There is no tolerance at which this tab's attack times can say who played what, so the tool refuses instead of writing a confident two-staff file out of noise.

The route that works is to split the audio, not the tab. Both channels share one clock, which is exactly what the tab lacks. Write L and R as separate mono files, transcribe each on its own, then put the two results on two staves. Two transcriptions of two channels are two guitar parts by construction, with no ownership call left to get wrong.
python3 ~/.claude/skills/two-guitar-split/split_stem_channels.py \
    --stem "<song>/guitar.mp3" --out-dir <work>/split

The gate runs first and refuses anything that is not SEPARABLE. Verified both ways: Kilgore Trout writes its two channels, Adriatic to Black Sea is refused as mono.

Pitch evidence that alignment cannot break

Every alignment-based pitch measure on this catalogue fails its own control, because a tab whose attacks sit 105 ms off cannot be scored note against note. Pitch-CLASS content needs no alignment at all. What a staff plays across the whole song either matches what that channel plays across the whole song, or it does not. What it still does not settle: which channel. The two channels have nearly identical pitch-class content, 0.9139 against 0.8957 on one staff and 0.9047 against 0.9313 on the other. The two guitarists play the same harmony and differ in their attacks, so a chroma measure cannot assign a staff to a side. Timing is likewise not established: attack recall 24.4%, pre-warp median 104.8 ms. No ties or note lengths are written, so duration agreement has nothing to compare.

Provenance

Zero note ids and zero beat ids in either guitar staff exist in the Songsterr AI tab. Of 529 and 633 written events, 25 and 13 happen to coincide with the AI tab on bar, position, fret, string and pitch, which is 4.7% and 2.1%, and they share no elements. No note content is inherited.

Standing rule

Two guitars, never four. A third staff is never the answer, and the split is decided by who played the note, never by panning or by stem loudness.

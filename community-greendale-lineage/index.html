The argument

NewsRadio is the organizing premise of this project. It started the work, and it holds the strongest cast-level fit on the Community Character Map, where every show is scored by the same declared method.

  • Dave Nelson to Jeff
  • Joe Garrelli to Troy
  • Matthew Brock to Abed
  • Lisa Miller to Annie
  • Beth to Britta
  • Catherine Duke to Shirley
  • Bill McNeal and Jimmy James to Pierce

The map records who descends from whom. This page follows what travels with those characters. A personality can travel on its own. So can a pairing between two characters, a plot machine, or an actor who brings an earlier series onto the set.

The strongest cases carry several of these together. Troy takes Joe's trade and Joe's fire, and his friendship with Abed repeats Joe's partnership with Matthew. Pierce's pill story borrows its intervention test from Saved by the Bell and casts NewsRadio's Matthew as the voice urging more pills. David Cross walks into a series built partly by the directors who shaped Arrested Development, in the season its creator plays Koogler.

Troy grows through three older roles

Troy is the clearest case of developmental ancestry. He enters in one sitcom role, and the series moves him through two more without erasing the first.

1. Slater

Production record

Troy arrives as the former high school football star whose standing comes from sports. The Character Map records Harmon's first picture of the part, a young Woody Harrelson, and his plan to pair Troy with Pierce.

2. Troy and Abed, from Joe and Matthew

PressEpisodeInterpretation

A critic saw the pairing in the first season. Reviewing "Romantic Expressionism" for The A.V. Club on February 5, 2010, Emily St. James compared Community's early casting discoveries to NewsRadio's second season and wrote, "Joe and Matthew was sort of the proto Abed and Troy."

Her sentence pairs the two duos and leaves the individual matches open. The premise map matches Joe with Troy and Matthew with Abed on episode evidence. The review also closes the Character Map's open item on a public source for the pairing.

NewsRadio gives the pair side plots of their own. Matthew begs, "Joe, teach me how to gamble," and the lessons leave him owing $5,120 and then $10,240 ("In Through the Out Door," February 4, 1996). Jimmy books them into an Ultimate Fighting bout, where Joe says, "I don't wanna hurt him" ("4:20," April 29, 1998). Joe teaches Matthew and Max "Joe-jitsu" with a padded suit ("Padded Suit," April 13, 1999).

Community moves that side universe to the center of the show, with Inspector Spacetime (S3E1) and the pillow and blanket war narrated by Keith David (S3E14). Scrubs' J.D. and Turk supply the emotional register of the pair on the Character Map.

3. The repairman

EpisodeInterpretation

The gift starts as a joke. In "English as a Second Language" (S1E24) Troy fixes the bathroom sink, a Good Will Hunting parody urges him to use his gift, and he stays in college because a degree widens his options (TV Tropes recap).

Season 3 turns the joke into prophecy. Vice Dean Laybourne tells him, "You are the true repairman, Troy," and credits him with fixing the men who fix air conditioners [S03E22:L41]. The prophecy reads, "The true repairman will repair man" [S03E22:L44]. When the chaplain reports that Laybourne "inhaled a lethal dose of Freon" [S03E22:L72], Troy doubts the accident, since Laybourne "was the best": "That's kind of a rookie mistake" [S03E22:L101]. He declares himself "the truest repairman" [S03E22:L150] to force the Sun Chamber duel, and when the Annex wants its own frozen punishment for Murray, he insists, "take him to the police. He murdered someone" [S03E22:L191].

NewsRadio's Joe already carried the dangerous side of the gift. In "Led Zeppelin II" (April 28, 1996) the building installs new security cameras, and Joe confesses to Dave, "I was trying to sabotage the security cameras and I screwed up, and I wired them the wrong way." The same episode gives him a theory that the staff are "subjects of some grand social experiment." In "Copy Machine" (April 8, 1998) Ted from Accounting dies after his tie catches in the copier, Joe admits, "I modified the document feeder so it would collate faster," and he starts "flushin' my tools down the toilet."

Both repair stories tie a technical gift to a death by machine and a question of blame. Joe blames his own modification. Troy calls a mentor's fatal accident a rookie mistake, and it proves to be murder.

4. The fire

EpisodeInterpretation

"Intro to Felt Surrogacy" (S4E9) gives Troy a secret from the same bundle. In the puppet confessions he sings, "I caused the Greendale fire of '03. Fifty-five acres went up in ablaze all because I burned an ant hill" [S04E09:L231]. Earlier in the episode Garrett runs in to announce, "There is a fire in the cafeteria!" [S04E09:L185].

Both shows give their practical guy a fire he caused and a scene where he confesses it. Joe's fire comes from a repair he meant as sabotage. Troy's comes from one small childish act that burned 55 acres.

5. The broken thermostatNew find

EpisodeInterpretation

NewsRadio's "Daydream" (November 13, 1996) opens with "the thermostat's broken, but Joe's workin' on it," and the heat sends the staff into movie and television parodies. Joe insists, "I'm fixin' it, all right?" and hears back, "You're just sittin' on your toolbox, staring into space."

Community gives Joe's descendant the climate trade itself. The Air Conditioning Repair Annex plays as a fantasy epic, with a prophecy and a Sun Chamber, so both shows attach genre fantasy to a building's cooling system.

6. The exitNew find

Episode

"Geothermal Escapism" (S5E5) sends Troy around the world on Pierce's boat with LeVar Burton (Character Map). In the series finale Abed turns that exit into a rule for television: "And it needs to be okay for it to get on a boat with Levar Burton and never come back. Because eventually, it all will" [S06E13:L204].

Pierce's pills: the intervention Jeff refuses

Brandon's reading joins a Saved by the Bell leadership test to NewsRadio casting, and it follows Jeff's failure into the story of his father. The cards below carry the evidence for each link.

The older test: “Jessie's Song”

Episode

In Saved by the Bell's "Jessie's Song" (season 2, November 3, 1990), Jessie takes caffeine pills to keep up with midterms and her singing group. Slater tells her, "Jessie, those pills are dangerous," and she snaps, "Mind your own business!" When she breaks down, Zack cancels her performance and calms her down, and Jessie goes to counseling (Saved by the Bell Wiki).

The tiny man

EpisodePersonnel

Pierce breaks both legs double-bouncing on an illegal trampoline in "Aerodynamics of Gender" (S2E7). At the end of the episode the painkillers show him a tiny pilot, played by Andy Dick, who tells him "it's okay to exceed the recommended daily dosage."

Jeff refuses

Episode

In "Early 21st Century Romanticism" (S2E15) Annie wants an intervention (TV Tropes recap). The scene opens with "We gotta do something about those painkillers," and the danger gets waved off with "He's recovering from broken legs" and "He's almost through his prescription." Jeff declines, "Count me out." The group puts it back on him: "He listens to you." Jeff decides, "You know what Pierce probably needs more than anything? Some space," and the talk ends in a fight about the Barenaked Ladies.

The tiny man returns with a harder pitch, "the prescription is almost done," and "if doctors are so smart, why are there millions of them?" The TV Tropes recap sums up the choice he offers, the pills or his friends. Pierce takes the pills and ends the night alone on a park bench, and Jeff texts the group an apology.

Pierce collects

Episode

"Intermediate Documentary Filmmaking" (S2E16) opens on the consequence. Pierce gathers the group "to exact my revenge," and Abed, filming, admits, "I feel bored by Pierce as a subject. But I'm excited about the narrative of the documentary format."

Jeff tries to settle it early: "Pierce, we're sorry we didn't respond quickly enough to your pill addiction. Is there any chance that apology is enough?" Pierce answers with the one subject Jeff claims not to care about: "I found your father."

Jeff insists his father is "not coming," and when asked, "Then why aren't you leaving?" he says, "Because I don't care and I'm not gonna let him think that I care." Pierce later narrates his own arc: "I set out to take revenge on Jeff Winger, to scare him with the ghost of his father. Instead, I took over the role of his father. Helped make him a better man."

What the casting adds

PersonnelPressInterpretation

Andy Dick played Matthew on NewsRadio, and Phil Hartman played Bill. In the premise map Matthew descends to Abed and Bill feeds Pierce. So the actor behind Abed's ancestor appears inside Pierce's head urging more pills, and one episode later Abed films Pierce's crisis and calls him a boring subject.

The public history behind that casting is documented. Brynn Hartman killed Phil Hartman and then herself in May 1998, and the coroner found alcohol, cocaine and the antidepressant Zoloft in her system (Deseret News, CNN). In July 2007 Jon Lovitz said, "Phil told me that they had a Christmas party and Andy was doing cocaine and he gave it to Phil's wife Brynn, who had been sober for 10 years" (CBS News). Dick has denied ever giving her cocaine, and Lovitz later apologized for the comment (Vice).

Critics caught both layers. Reviewing "Aerodynamics of Gender" on November 5, 2010, Emily St. James wrote that Dick's appearance "continues my 'Community is a secret remake of Newsradio' theory, which makes no sense at all." A Pajiba essay carries the title "The Sickest, Most Twisted Meta Joke of the Six-Season Run of 'Community.'" Intent stays open. The accusation was public in 2007, and Dick was cast in 2010.

The leader's test

Interpretation

Zack passes the older test late, canceling the performance and calming Jessie down. Jeff is chosen because Pierce listens to him, refuses, and pays through the one wound he says doesn't matter. The Zack descendant fails Pierce by avoidance, and the Matthew descendant fails him by filming.

David Cross and the Arrested Development line

Cross belongs with the actor-history cases. Arrested Development's directors and its creator both reach Greendale around his arrival.

The directors

PersonnelProduction record

Joe and Anthony Russo directed the Arrested Development pilot and the Community pilot (Looper, via the Character Map). Joe Russo directed "Advanced Dungeons & Dragons" (S2E14, February 3, 2011) and "Debate 109" (S1E9, per its production script).

Cross has described the Russos as the directors "who really created that docu style of directing and shooting," and Hurwitz as a planner who "had ideas for episode 48 that would be in play in episode 4. He had the long game in mind" (Spark and Fire interview).

The creator in the same season

PressPersonnelProduction recordEpisode

Coverage of the November 2013 casting led with his old show: "'Arrested Development's' David Cross to Guest Star on 'Community'" (TheWrap). The same season Mitch Hurwitz plays Koogler in "App Development and Condiments" (S5E8), whose tag announces that "Mitchell D. Hurwitz IS Koogler" (TV Tropes recap). The DVD commentary for that episode lists Yvette Nicole Brown, Mitchell Hurwitz, Harmon, Rob Schrab, Jordan Blum and Parker Deay (Community Wiki).

EpisodeInterpretation

When the group proposes a second game, someone objects, "Was everyone's takeaway from last time that we can use D&D to reprogram brains? Nobody feels that we almost caused a suicide?" and the answer comes back, "We prevented one." Hank, a seasoned player, reads the design at once: "Guys, I don't suppose that this is some sort of contrived paint-by-numbers adventure that's designed to force an emotional bond between me and my emotionally stunted father, is it?" ("Advanced Advanced Dungeons & Dragons," S5E10).

Hank spots the story machine and keeps playing inside it, the same double position Abed's group occupies whenever it builds a narrative for a friend.

Hickey's voice becomes writing

Episode

Hickey promises, "if we lose, I'm gonna punch each of you in the heart," and boasts, "I've punched about a thousand hearts in my life" (S5E10). Abed's cop script appears in "Bondage and Beta Male Sexuality" (S5E7), where the hero is named Police Justice, and the TV Tropes recap for "Intro to Recycled Cinema" (S6E8) credits Hickey with helping on it. There Abed says, "I had a former cop help me with dialogue," and the next line is "I'm gonna punch you in the heart."

Koogler outlives his episodeNew find

Episode

The character returns by name after Season 5. "Preston Koogler" appears on a list in "Basic Crisis Room Decorum" (S6E3, TV Tropes recap), and in "Modern Espionage" (S6E11), "Koogler's thumb drive is actually a hardware encryption key."

The “Huzzah” signal, measured

EpisodeInterpretation

The table answers a speech about fathers and sons with a round of "Huzzah" (S5E10 dialogue file, no speaker names). Across the 53 Arrested Development episodes on disk, "Huzzah!" occurs once, in "Bringing Up Buster" (S1E3), right after Tobias talks his way into directing the school play. The nod is possible, and the evidence for it is one line.

NewsRadio plot machines beyond the pairs

The premise map pairs characters. These cards follow NewsRadio stories whose working parts reappear at Greendale.

The station sale

Episode

In "Station Sale" (January 7, 1996) Jimmy announces, "I'm selling the station," and the staff answer with chants of "No sale!" Greendale faces its own sale to Subway in "Basic Sandwich" (S5E13).

Freaky Friday, with a new partnerNew find

EpisodeInterpretation

NewsRadio's "Freaky Friday" (April 20, 1999) proposes, "Let's switch jobs for a day," and notes, "this is like that movie Freaky Friday." Wikipedia's episode summary gives the switch to Jimmy and Matthew.

In "Basic Human Anatomy" (S4E11) Troy marks "the third anniversary of me and Abed watching Freaky Friday for the first time," and the two stage a body switch. The device stays with Matthew's descendant, and his partner moves from Jimmy to Troy.

What-if episodes that keep the jobs

EpisodeInterpretation

"Space" (May 21, 1997) opens by asking, "What if NewsRadio took place in outer space?" Joe is woken after "Eighty-three years" to "fix the atomic reactor core," and the station ends up on a self-destruct countdown. "Sinking Ship" (May 12, 1998) sets the staff on the Titanic, with Joe vouching that the "radio station/luxury liner is unsinkable" and Matthew assigned to "iceberg watch." "Daydream" runs its parodies off the broken thermostat.

Community keeps its people recognizable through the same kind of transplant, in the timelines of "Remedial Chaos Theory" (S3E4), the Ken Burns frame of "Pillows and Blankets" (S3E14) and Abed's laugh-track happy place in "History 101" (S4E1).

Joe and Matthew as recruits

Episode

In "Zoso" (February 25, 1996) Bill has convinced a date he is British: "Look, she thinks I'm British, okay?" Matthew coaches him that "in England they use pounds, not dollars," and Bill introduces "my mate, Joe." The pair gets pulled into a colleague's scheme as a unit.

Joe's homemade production

EpisodeInterpretation

In "Apartment" (January 12, 1999) Joe launches "the 24-hour break room Internet-cam," the staff start "spicing it up for the camera," and someone recalls, "When Joe first approached me to play the role of Alien, I was skeptical myself, until I read the script."

Casting friends in a homemade production is an Abed habit. Traits cross between the two descendants of the pair, so the Joe line and the Matthew line share material.

Gifts as tests

EpisodeInterpretation

In S2E16 Pierce bequeaths a blank check to Britta, a tiara to Annie, a meeting with LeVar Burton to Troy and a meeting with his father to Jeff (TV Tropes recap). Each gift tests the person who receives it. The Jimmy James side of this habit stays an interpretation until a matching NewsRadio episode is cited.

The rest of the group grows through roles

Troy is the worked example. The same method reads the others, and most of it remains interpretation with episode anchors.

Jeff

EpisodeInterpretation

Abed builds his Jeff impression from "Ten percent Dick Van Dyke, 20 percent Sam Malone, 40 percent Zach Braff in Scrubs, and 30 percent Hilary Swank in Boys Don't Cry," and the one ingredient questioned is "Zach Braff?" (S1E17). The Zack Morris and Sam Malone charm of the early seasons turns toward Dave Nelson's job, managing a group that keeps exceeding his control.

Annie

Interpretation

Annie starts from Jessie Spano's pills and pressure, takes on Lisa Miller's competence as her work widens, and on the Character Map also runs a Buffy line from Cordelia to Willow to Dark Willow.

Britta

Episode

The group's judgment of Britta becomes a verb. "She Britta'd it," and then "You Britta'd 'Britta'd'" (S3E5), and in "Herstory of Dance" (S4E8), "She straight Britta'd this." In that same episode Sophie B. Hawkins arrives thanks to "a wise old man who was the official supplier of moist towelettes to the Lilith Fair between 1997 and 1999," and Jeff hears, "Cut her some slack, Jeff."

Abed

EpisodeInterpretation

Abed casts Annie and himself as Chandler and Phoebe (S1E4) and takes Radar to Jeff's Hawkeye (S1E13), both on the Character Map. He reads his own ancestry out loud, which lets the fiction perform the comparison this project makes from outside.

Chang

Interpretation

Chang changes institutional jobs from season to season, and his constant is the new slot. The map's Kirk comparison from Gilmore Girls is about that function.

Verdict

The notes are right. The grid is wrong. Two separate errors sit in the time signature and tempo layer, and they add up. The tempo is written a beat and a fifth slow, and two bars carry padding that has no audio behind them. Together the tab notates 9.72 seconds more music than the record contains, so it drifts steadily away from the recording and finishes almost ten seconds after the track ends.

Where the padding comes from

The suite's partial bars make sense in place. A 2/8 bar joins the previous section and a 1/8 bar hands off to the next one. Lifting the 109 bars out of the suite to stand alone left those two joints exposed, and they were rounded up into full 3/4 and 4/4 bars. That rounding added grid time with no audio underneath it.

The arithmetic closes

Two independent errors, multiplied, should reproduce the measured overrun. They do.

padding 330.0 / 324.5 quarter beats = +1.695 % tempo 70.20 / 69.00 quarter BPM = +1.739 % combined 1.01695 × 1.01739 − 1 = +3.464 % measured 286.96 s notated / 277.23 s record = +3.507 % residual = 0.04 percentage points
A 0.04 point residual means these two errors account for the whole discrepancy. There is no third problem hiding underneath, and there is nothing wrong with the note content.

The fix, and what it buys

I tested candidate grids by matching the tab's kick and snare positions against onsets detected in the Demucs drum stem, with the offset swept freely so each grid got its best possible showing.

Two edits, nothing touched in the notes. Set the single tempo automation from 69 to 70.2, and change bar 1 from 3/4 to 2/8 and bar 109 from 4/4 to 1/8. That takes the grid error from +3.51% to +0.04%, well inside the 2% tolerance that preflight_import.py already enforces, and it more than doubles the share of the record's kick hits the tab lands on.

Method, and what I could not settle

The record used as the ruler is the video Songsterr syncs this tab to, YouTube 8bXSsh3Z-xE, the official FrankZappaVEVO visualizer, running 277.235 seconds. Drums were isolated with Demucs htdemucs before onset detection, because the full mix has vocals, keyboard, marimba and horns firing into the same onset envelope and it made the pattern matching much noisier.

One thing stayed unresolved. The exact bar-level start offset is ambiguous. A 6/8 groove this uniform autocorrelates strongly at the bar, so alignments separated by a whole bar, about 2.56 seconds, score almost equally. Different fitting passes returned offsets that differ by roughly two bars. The duration and tempo results do not depend on that, since both are measured from the record's own period and total length. Anyone re-checking the start point should do it against a structural landmark such as the two 4/4 bars at 6 and 7, rather than by correlation.

The onset detector also over-triggers on the drum stem, returning 551 kick-band onsets where the tab writes 333 kicks. Over-detection makes matching easier, never harder, so the 26.1% figure for the tab as published is a ceiling and the true match rate is lower.

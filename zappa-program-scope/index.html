0. The plan, corrected 2026-09-06

Phase 1 as stated is already complete, and re-uploading would cause harm

The instruction was to re-upload the ghost restores that had not gone back yet, rebuilding from the original files kept on disk. Checked song by song against the live API on 2026-09-06: all sixteen damaged tabs already carry a restore revision, and every one of them has its pre-sweep baseline on disk. Fifteen restores sit in the moderator queue and one has published. There is nothing left to send.

Sending a second restore on any of these would stack a duplicate revision behind one a moderator has yet to read. That is the failure already visible on Uncle Meat, where three restores queued at once and the third exists only to say which two should be dropped.

The upload rule that separates phase 1 from phase 2

A ghost restore is a repair to damage this account caused, so it belongs on the original tab as a revision. A transcription-matched rebuild is a different reading of the music, so it uses create and edit a copy of the current tab. That keeps another transcriber's work intact and puts our version beside it rather than over it. This is what the whole ghost debacle was caused by not doing.

0d. Disk verification: the restores carry the notation, not just the wording

The revision description is self-reported and is not evidence

Brandon's correction, 2026-09-06: the submit box does not always get filled in, so a description claiming a restore proves nothing. The check that counts is the notation itself. Every .gp in the repair tree was re-counted on 2026-09-06, and the count walks Beat → Notes references rather than the file's Notes pool.

The counting trap that has already produced one wrong verdict

GPIF <Note> elements are shared definitions. Inca Roads holds 14 Note elements carrying <AntiAccent> and those 14 are referenced 174 times across beats. A grep of the raw XML returns 14 and reads as near-absence. That is how reference_inca_roads_baseline_gp_is_post_sweep came to declare a good pre-sweep baseline useless, and it was corrected on 2026-09-06. Never count a marking by grepping the pool.

1. The program in one paragraph

Frank Zappa drum transcriptions on Songsterr are being repaired against professional printed charts and against isolated drum stems. The work has four surfaces. A notation sweep run on 2026-08-29 and 2026-08-30 damaged twenty community tabs and that damage is being undone one song at a time. Separately, each tab is being checked bar by bar against transcriptions by Ryan Brown, Daniel Bédard, Todd Bishop, Pete Sweeney and the Drumnet charts. Separately again, isolated kit stems are being used to find textures the tabs omit entirely. Underneath all three sits a coverage question, which songs have a drum reading of the album take at all.

3. The ghost-note sweep and its damage

What happened, in three sentences

Songsterr draws a ghost note as a notehead inside round brackets, which is the correct rendering. A sweep read those brackets as a defect and submitted revisions removing them across twenty Frank Zappa tabs. Sixteen of those stripping revisions published and went live.

Root cause has three layers. A house rule from stems-to-guitar-pro-drums, written for generated output, was applied to published community transcriptions. GPIF spells a ghost as <AntiAccent>Normal</AntiAccent>, so a search for <Ghost> returned zero and that zero was read as absence. The preservation audit compared pitch, rhythm and position across 120,579 beat events, and articulation sat outside what it measured.

Lane breakdown matters, because a ghost on a snare is unambiguous and a ghost on a ride is a different question. Counted 2026-09-05 by fetching each tab's pre-sweep revision.

Watermelon In Easter Hay s35881, furthest along

Repair 1, foot-pedal hi-hat, confirmed and built

The tab notates six Pedal Hi-Hat events across the whole song. The isolated hat stem carries a steady chick on the quarter. Periodic-subsequence test gives period 1.0800 s against the GP quarter of 1.0842 s at a snare-anchored 55.34 BPM. 178 of 505 grid slots carry a hat onset, 35% against a 26% chance baseline. Brandon's representative chicks at 181.93, 183.03, 184.10 and 185.19 s land in the stem at 181.92, 183.00, 184.09 and 185.17 s. Roughly 172 events are missing. Queue q-2026-09-06-ca2848.

Repair 2, snare rebound texture, confirmed

The tab carries 193 snare events with zero ghost flags. Brandon measured 202 principal snare attacks, 196 carrying at least one substantially quieter follow-up inside 250 ms and 178 carrying two, the first near 92 ms and the second near 156 ms. Use his counts. My own run over-detected principals at 411. Queue q-2026-09-06-8ce0a9.

The alignment anchor, settled

193 notated snare hits against 615 detected snare onsets gives offset 37.60 s, scale 1.012, effective 55.34 BPM, matching 90 of 193 within 80 ms. That is 47% against an 18% chance baseline, real at 2.6 times chance. Every audio measurement on this song uses that alignment.

9. Findings withdrawn, never cite again

  • The Watermelon alignment at offset 33.00 s and scale 1.020. It scored 64% within 50 ms against a 58% chance baseline, so it established nothing.
  • The Mann-Whitney z = -0.40 no-dynamic-difference result, which rested on that alignment.
  • The claim that the 1,316 Watermelon ride flags are a proven intentional accent grid.
  • The rebound-coincidence line. Cymbal-lane ghosts hit rebound times at 7.7% to 9.9% against chance baselines of 11.1% to 20.7%, below chance at every picker setting tested.
  • The claim that a 23.3 s duration gap is a normal lead-in or tail on a YouTube upload. That was invented and the video was never inspected.

Closed by measurement, do not reopen

The 193 post-snare ride ghosts on Watermelon are not misplaced snare rebounds. The tab places them at a rigid 271 ms after the snare, one GP sixteenth, with the 10th and 90th percentiles both at 271 ms. The audio rebounds land at 70 to 92 ms and 134 to 156 ms.

11. The gate stack

One song at a time survives. One song per chat is retired.

The fourth amendment, written 2026-09-06, measured 77 of 164 open queue items sitting in the Zappa lane, 47% of the backlog, with four fresh chats promised for deferred songs and zero opened. A finished song now re-locks inside the same chat. The lock governs attention and carries no authority to stop.

  1. Precedence. The completion contract wins. The 24-hour or 400-turn ceiling wins next, as a handover naming the successor id. The single-song lock applies underneath both.
  2. The lock keys on the song id. Write it as one line and treat it as the only legal target. Fetch api/meta/<songId> and read title and artist back before editing.
  3. Branch three. When an outside holder blocks the upload, the chat keeps working evidence on that same song. The evidence list is finite and has five items, so the branch has a bottom.
  4. A queue item is never authorization to switch songs.
  5. The ceiling hands over. It never empties the queue and never converts open work into something that needs its own chat.

Measured drift that produced these rules: session c1e4ffee ran 113.2 hours with 3,208 assistant turns against 82 typed turns, a ratio of 39 to 1, and carried three song ids inside a chat allowed one.

1The YouTube is the same performance

The hypothesis was worth testing and it does not survive measurement. Onset envelopes from the fresh YouTube pull were cross-correlated against the Moises source mix in seventeen twenty-second probes spread across the whole song. Sixteen of the seventeen lock onto one offset.

FileDurationReads asRelease
Moises source mix545.27 s9:05Joe's Garage, with the Central Scrutinizer megaphone intro
YouTube pull522.44 s8:42Plays the Music of Frank Zappa, intro snipped

The measured offset is −23.34 s, with a standard deviation of 3.9 milliseconds over a 450-second span. A speed difference of even one part in ten thousand would show up as 45 ms of walk across that span. Nothing walks. The two files carry one performance with a different head trim, and the 8:42 figure matches the cited runtime exactly.

Why this matters more than it sounds

Nine days of stem measurement were made against the 9:05 master while the tab is synced to the 8:42 edit. Had the two been different takes, every one of those measurements would be void. They are the same audio, so the stem evidence stands, and the only correction needed is a constant on the time origin.

3Songsterr floors the tempo, and it cost the first fix

The first revision, r8973389, wrote 105 automations spanning 53.57 to 58.37 bpm with two decimals. Reading the published data back showed integers. The rounding is not to nearest.

Flooring every bar makes every bar a little slow, and the error accumulates in one direction. The notated length grew from 506.68 s to 511.28 s, and the match that should have reached 0.782 came out at 0.492. The revision was honest work and its own description overstated what landed.

Read by eye, and no outlier drives it

This started as a number comparison, so it was rendered and read. The served step function sits at or below the sent value at all 105 bars and never above. Per bar, every difference lands in [0, 1) and every one is positive. Rounding to nearest would put about half of them negative.

CheckResult

A mean of 0.500 is what uniform truncation predicts for a discarded fractional part, and the accumulated lag is a monotonic staircase with no step down, ending at +4.61 s. The population carries no outlier to discount, so unlike the bar-1 claims elsewhere on this page this one rests on all 105 values at once.

The fix that survives flooring

Integers are all the platform keeps, so the integers were chosen by error diffusion. At each bar the integer picked is the one that lands the next barline closest to the fitted metronome time, and whatever error remains carries forward into the next choice. Bar durations then alternate between neighbouring values and average out to the right tempo.

Worst barline error against the fitted grid: 0.049 s for error diffusion, against 4.57 s for choosing each bar's integer on its own.

5The crash cymbals: two detectors, both refused

The tab writes 51 crash notes, one at the top of each phrase from bar 4 to bar 96. Two detectors were built and rendered, and the render is what settled both.

The written crashes are real, and this is the measurement that shows it

A crash should out-peak an ordinary ride stroke in the same stem. The 51 written crash positions were compared against the 1,501 written ride strokes that do not coincide with them.

An AUC of 0.894 says that picking one written crash and one ride stroke at random, the crash is louder 89 times in 100. The original transcriber's crash placement is supported. An earlier version of this page rested that on a weaker claim, 42 of 51 detected, and the render showed the detector was firing on every ride stroke, so hitting a crash position proved only that a cymbal was struck. That claim is withdrawn and replaced by the table above.

The second detector clears its control comfortably and still cannot ship. 310 additions across 105 bars is 2.95 crashes per bar, six times the author's own 0.49, sustained for nine minutes. No drummer plays that. The floor sits at only 1.73x the ride median, so roughly a tenth of the 1,501 ride strokes clear it by themselves.

The 310, read by eye

Density was the arithmetic reason. Rendering the 310 marks against the tab's own ride notation gives the cause. 148 of them, 48%, sit within 70 ms of a stroke the tab already notates as ride. They are not missing notes. They are existing notes the detector cannot tell apart from a crash, and the rest land on the same rise-and-decay sawtooth. The written crashes sit on visibly larger events, which is the 0.894 AUC seen rather than computed, and the guitar stem visibly crosses the 0.157 floor around bars 78 to 80.

The blocker is the separation: the lead guitar stem, which contains no cymbals at all, still fires 82 and 89 times under these floors, so the Moises cymbal stem carries guitar bleed at crash amplitude.

It could not be done in this session because both available features stop short: decay separates at AUC 0.606, and peak energy separates well enough to confirm known crashes while returning a density no human plays. 6The video sync points, and a defect claim I withdrew

Withdrawn: "bar 1 is 0.818 s early"

I measured the sync points two ways and both said bar 1 sat 0.81 s off. A stop gate then demanded that an outlier in a width check be re-read by eye before being called a defect. Rendering the 8:42 master killed the claim.

Neither candidate lands on a musical event. The tab's bar 1 sits in a sparse solo-guitar passage and the band enters about five bars later. My "two independent confirmations" both rested on the same 36.870 s metromap anchor, so they were one measurement stated twice.

What the eye added that the arithmetic missed

Interval 1 runs long in both videos by nearly the same amount, +0.79 s on the primary and +0.82 s on the alt, while every later residual hugs zero. Two independently synced videos placing bar 1 the same way relative to bar 2 points at the tab's notated bar-1 length rather than at one video's sync being wrong. That strengthens the withdrawal above: 12.710 s is likely what both syncers intended.

What this means for the tempo fix

For video-synced playback the sync points carry the timing, so r8973454 changes little across the body. It fixes Songsterr's own MIDI playback and the within-bar interpolation. A peer session reached the same shape independently, placing the residual error at the opening rather than through the song.

The route exists, and nothing here is blocked by access

Scanning the five page bundles, 2,693,991 characters: videoSync appears 31 times, video/sync twice, plus POST /api/video-points/process taking {autoSyncRequestId}, an event video/syncPointsPublished, and the editor command commands/editor:pointsReplace. OPTIONS on the read endpoint answers 404 and GET answers 200, so the write path is the editor command, the same class of route that carried the Guitar Pro import. Two browser bridges are live on this Mac.

No pointsReplace was fired. The reason is evidence rather than access: after the eye check there is no verified replacement value for bar 1, and writing an unverified number into published sync data is worse than leaving it.

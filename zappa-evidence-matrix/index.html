The rhythm red zone rule Every passage carrying quintuplets, sextuplets, septuplets, octuplets, nonuplets, ten-note groupings, simultaneous meters, nested tuplets or rapidly changing odd meters is marked before any hit-level comparison. The same marking applies wherever the structural transcription shows drums reinforcing a melody accent or an ensemble attack. No automated repair inside a red zone from neighbouring drum context alone. Establish the exact bar duration from the album book or academic score, then the fractional attack grid from the written ensemble parts including documented ratios such as seven against six, then the kit assignment from a direct drum transcription, performance footage or drummer source. Compare Songsterr against that using rational beat positions rather than a fixed sixteenth grid.

03The three critical red zones

These are the passages where an ordinary sixteenth-note comparison would convert a legitimate drum attack into an apparent error.

Inca Roads

drums play SEPTUPLETS against bass sextuplets, then a QUINTUPLET against marimba sextuplets, in the same bar, over a sustained 4/4 pulse

Don't You Ever Wash That Thing?

opening figures 4/16, 5/16, 9/16, 11/16 with equal 5/8 percussion passages between. 11/16 groups as 3+3+5, 9/16 as 4+5.

Be-Bop Tango

beat 1 a QUINTUPLET, beat 3 a SEPTUPLET subdivided 2+2+3, both against the continuing pulse

Zomby Woof — high

bar 2 barred 10/16 or 5/16+5/16 depending on source; 15/16 x4; 5/4 quintuplet fill

Montana — high

intro rolls measured as 64th notes, not 32nds; rolls start on the offbeat and run 2.5 and 3.5 beats

Echidna's Arf (Of You) — high

melody accents against a 4/4 accompaniment, accents reinforced by bells and later by BASS DRUM. Ensemble doubling polices kick placement.

04Inca Roads is the calibration target

Why it beats every other candidate

Benjamin Wall's thesis carries a complete study score transcribed from the record, explicitly including drum kit, percussion, timpani, bass, marimba, keyboards, guitar and vocals, written because no suitable complete score existed. That gives real drum-lane evidence alongside the surrounding ensemble parts, so a suspicious drum event can be tested for whether it independently follows the groove or copies a composed ensemble attack. It is free.

The difficult material is exactly what the validator has to survive: drums playing septuplets against bass sextuplets, then a quintuplet against marimba sextuplets, in the same bar, over a sustained 4/4 pulse. The One Size Fits All book independently supplies the interlude meters, 11/16, 8/16, 7/16, 5/4, 2/4 and 4/4. Meter, ensemble rhythm and drum orchestration can all be triangulated rather than trusting one Guitar Pro file.

06What each book is for

Over-Nite Sensation and One Size Fits All — the bar skeleton source

Note-for-note across every track on their albums. They supply section boundaries, time signatures, bar lengths, tempo changes, written tuplets, rests and exact attack positions wherever the kit clearly doubles an ensemble figure. Extract that into a machine-readable rhythm map before the next large correction pass.

The 1982 Vai guitar book — notation language only

None of these 26 tracks are in it. It carries Zappa's high-complexity rhythmic vocabulary and transcription conventions, so it is precedent for how sixes, sevens, nines and conflicting subdivisions get represented rather than flattened into ordinary sixteenths. It is never a source for these songs.

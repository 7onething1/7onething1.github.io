CORRECTION, added after a controlled experiment. An earlier version of this page reported that the stem-derived source c-tied.gp was the weakest transcription in the set, with Rhythm pitch support at 24.7%. That was a metadata defect, not a bad transcription.

Its Rhythm track carries CapoFret 2 while every Note's stored Midi was written for capo 0, so the capo-computed pitch exceeds the stored Midi by exactly 2 on all 891 notes. The file means two things at once, and this audit read that staff two semitones sharp. Changing only the capo, with every note, onset, duration, string and fret preserved and verified identical, moves Rhythm pitch support from 24.7% to 63.4%, ownership from 45.6% to 71.9%, and puts the staff in the correct stem. The corrected source sits within 1.5 points of the promoted build.

The gate now hard-fails this defect as CAPO_MIDI_MISMATCH. It fires 891 times on c-tied.gp and is clean on the corrected twin and on the promoted build.

The thirteen promotions were checked against the corrected source and are unaffected. Run the gate on chain62 with c-tied.gp and again with c-tied-CAPO0.gp and both return preservation PASS and position PASS, identically. The reason is mechanical: the gate's pitch census prefers each Note's stored Midi, and position compares written string and fret. Both are capo independent. Receipt 11f6b0f89211bb9f binds chain62 to the corrected baseline.

That divergence is exactly how the defect hid. The gate resolved pitch from stored Midi and this audit resolved it from tuning plus capo plus fret, so the gate saw nothing while the audit read two semitones sharp. The audit now counts and prints the disagreement too, and the gate fails on it, so neither tool can pass it over again.

1. The finding

Every transcription derived from the stems beats the Songsterr AI tab, and they beat it by a lot on the Rhythm staff. Read with its own note data, c-tied.gp scores 63.4% Rhythm pitch support against Songsterr's 41.1%, and the promoted build reaches 65.0%. Against a measured ceiling of 77.5%.

The working assumption of the project holds after all: the stem-derived source is a much better reference than the Songsterr AI tab. It took a controlled experiment to see it, because a capo set on one track without rewriting its frets or its Midi had been reading as forty points of transcription error.

Being stem-derived is still a statement about how a file was produced rather than proof its notes are right. The lesson that survives is narrower and sharper: check a file's internal consistency before measuring it against anything.

2. The control comes first

A detector that answers yes everywhere proves nothing. The first run on the real stems separated a true pitch from a tritone only 70.5% of the time, which is ambiguous by itself: it can mean the tab is wrong, and it can equally mean the detector cannot tell pitches apart. Those call for opposite work, so the ambiguity was resolved before anything was graded.

The tab was rendered to audio, Lead into the left stem and Rhythm into the right, at the same beat-to-seconds map. Every note in that render is correct by construction, and the identical detector was run over it.

At 99.1% separation with a 25.5 dB median margin, and with Lead and Rhythm recovered into their correct stems at 21.8 and 19.3 dB, the detector works. The weak numbers on the real stems are therefore evidence about the transcriptions.

The control also refuted one of my own metrics. The first composite pitch score reached only 41% on a signal where every note is correct. A note whose control could not be measured was being dropped from the numerator and kept in the denominator. Fixed, the ceiling reads 84.0% and 77.5%, and every number on this page is computed after that fix.

4. The Rhythm staff, and the experiment that explained it

The Lead staff is close across every file, 54.9% to 67.0%. Rhythm appeared to spread from 24.7% to 64.9%, and that one staff carried the whole apparent result. The structural difference sat in the same place: c-tied.gp writes Rhythm with capo 2 and the promoted chain writes it with capo 0.

Rather than re-voice a hundred chords on the strength of a correlation, the variable was tested on its own. Only the CapoFret value changed. Every note, onset, duration, string and fret was walked before and after and required to be identical on all five tracks.

The capo explains the entire accuracy gap and none of the playability numbers. That is the expected signature and it is worth stating, because it separates the two cleanly: hand skip and spans are read from written frets, which the capo does not touch, while every sounding pitch shifts by two semitones. Any account that blamed the transcription for the accuracy gap was wrong.

5. The BEST filename carried authority it never earned

Hashing the two files settles the argument that started this work.

s6389251-BEST.gp     sha256  0e7cf090f141ccba...
s6389251-iter48.gp   sha256  0e7cf090f141ccba...   identical bytes

BEST is a byte-for-byte copy of iter48, the blanket refret. An open-string regression was called against that file because of its name. Its 310 and 405 open notes are the outlier in the set, and the stem-derived source sits near 29 and 25.

The validator now records a comparison_basis on every promotion, rejection and receipt: stem source, parent, checkpoint, Songsterr reference, beat map, every audio file, the transformation, the authorized edit scope and the exact changed note IDs, all hashed. Promotion refuses without a declared stem source and at least one audio file, because a build compared only to other transcriptions has never been tested for accuracy.

7. The repair the audio licensed

The physical gate held one IMPOSSIBLE_SPAN, at bar 62.938 on the Rhythm staff: s0f11 s1f4, sounding 47 and 49, a 7-fret span against a 4-fret hand at 128 bpm. The gate's own alternative search reported that no complete voicing of those two pitches is narrower, so no refret could fix it. The gate said the shape or the transcription is wrong, and the audit is what told the two apart.

Pitch 47 passes all three controls in the Rhythm's stem. Pitch 49 fails the wrong-time control by 18 dB in both stems, meaning a randomly chosen instant carries more energy at that pitch than the written moment does, and it is louder in the Lead's stem. The Lead staff writes pitch 49 at 246.750 and again at 248.250, one beat either side. This is the documented failure of the pipeline: two guitars written onto one staff.

One note removed, Note id 2407, Rhythm staff, beat 247.750. Span 7 becomes span 0. Collateral drift across all five tracks: zero beats changed. Pitch 49 remains written at 240 other positions, so no pitch left the music and Tier 1 preservation passes.

Gate verdict moves FAIL to REVIEW and the hard profile is clean: same-string 0, tie-inconsistent 0, impossible spans 0, octave artifacts 0. Re-measured against the stems, the removal also nudged accuracy up: Rhythm pitch support 64.9% to 65.8%, wrong-time separation 75.5% to 76.4%.

Reassignment to the Lead staff was rejected. Pitch 49 fails its wrong-time control in the Lead's stem too, so moving the attack across would keep an unsupported attack rather than resolve one.

8. Working it to convergence

The gate does not fail an interior-string gap or a suspect ownership. It says the shape "needs positive audio evidence of fingerpicking or hybrid picking". That is a request for evidence, and nothing in the pipeline could answer it before, because every check compared one transcription to another. The audit can answer it.

Dropping the panning cue changed the answer

The adjudication above originally decided whether a chord was two guitars by comparing how loud each note was in each stem. That is the cue this recording does not carry. Re-run deciding a note only on whether some stem explains its pitch, the SPLIT class disappears entirely:

RuleEvery note realSplit across guitars Unsupported
with panning, as first run15 of 36813
pitch only, no panning26 of 36010

Eight chords had been called two guitars on 2 to 3 dB of noise. Under the pitch-only rule every note in them is explained by the audio, and the finding evaporates. That correction is the reason the last five promotions were reachable at all.

The evidence was re-derived from the audio after every round rather than reused, so the list shrank as the tab improved: 43 flagged findings became 37, then 36, 35, and finally 33. The run stopped because the rule found zero remaining candidates, not because a budget ran out. Thirteen promotions, each one a real gate decision against the build before it.

Accuracy rose while notes came out. Lead pitch support 65.5% to 67.0% and Rhythm 64.9% to 65.0%, against ceilings of 84.0% and 77.5%. Thirteen notes left the tab across thirteen promotions, each licensed by its own measurement and each leaving its pitch elsewhere in the music. 25 of the 33 chords the gate asked about now carry positive evidence that every note in them is real.

9. Why PASS is out of reach for this artifact

The gate holds at REVIEW rather than PASS, and the reason is a single diagnostic tier. Reading the gate's own thresholds, PASS needs hand skip at or under 40%. Both staves sit above it.

StaffSkipping chordsHand skip Same pitches, contiguous voicing exists No contiguous voicing at any fingering
Lead Guitar80 of 12164.5% 4238
Rhythm Guitar89 of 14360.1% 5930

So the tier is mechanically reachable. Re-voicing those 101 chords onto contiguous strings, without altering a single pitch, would put Lead at 31.4% and Rhythm at 21.0%, both under the threshold.

Every recorded attempt at that re-voicing broke a tier the gate ranks higher. This is not a prediction. It is what the checkpoint ledger already holds, from earlier runs:

  • s6389251-iter5.gp reached hand skip 25.6%, comfortably under the threshold, and was rejected for SAME_STRING 0 to 10, TIE_COLLISION 0 to 3, TIE_INCONSISTENT 0 to 60, and Tier 1 preservation FAILED, so a pitch left the music or a pitch was invented.
  • s6389251-iter55.gp reached 46.4% and was rejected because Tier 1b POSITION FAILED, and Tier 1b outranks the Tier 2 hard profile, so a better hand count does not license a relocated performance.
  • s6389251-iter48.gp, the blanket refret, produced 310 and 405 open notes against roughly 29 and 25 in the stem-derived source.

The gate is behaving consistently. It ranks pitch preservation and fret position above hand comfort, and a mass re-voicing trades the first two for the third.

The audio cannot license the relocation, and this is the hard limit. Every check on this page answers a question about pitch: is this pitch sounding, at this instant, in this stem. A re-voicing changes none of that. The same pitch fretted at s1f9 or at s0f14 produces nearly the same sound, and the timbral difference between two fingerings of one pitch sits far below what a Demucs guitar stem preserves. The recording does not contain the information the position gate needs.

So the blocked operation is exact: re-voice 101 chords onto contiguous strings, and satisfy Tier 1b position for each. The missing evidence is per-note fingering evidence, which no measurement of this audio can supply. Methods attempted: per-note peak salience across six partials in both stems, three controls, a synthetic ceiling, and per-chord adjudication. All of them answer pitch, and none answers fingering.

Closing that gap is a decision rather than a measurement. It means accepting a blanket refret whose last outing measurably degraded the transcription, and the ledger holds the receipt for that.

10. Still open

Attack density is not established for any of the three. The onset detector finds 387 onsets in the left stem and 321 in the right. All three tabs write between 700 and 1076 attacks per staff. Even on the synthetic control, where the answer is known, only 86.5% and 89.9% of written attacks matched a detected onset and roughly 75% of detected onsets were explained. The detector under-detects in dense material, so this number cannot yet separate an over-written tab from a limitation of the detector.

Timing cannot be resolved finer than 23 ms. On the synthetic control the median timing error is exactly one analysis hop, which is a resolution artifact rather than a real offset.

The low register is unmeasurable from these stems. 102 Lead notes and 36 Rhythm notes in c-tied.gp sit below 130 Hz, and the Demucs guitar stem keeps roughly 1.32% of the mix energy at 60 to 90 Hz. Those notes are reported as unmeasurable rather than absent. Settling them needs the source mix, which was not on this machine.

Hand skip sits at 62.1%. Every hard gate is clean and the gate labels hand skip a diagnostic to be read after the gates. It is the last tier still in the uncorrected band, which is why the artifact holds status best_validated_so_far rather than BEST.

8 chords remain unexplained, and each has a stated reason removal is not evidenced. Five hold no supported note at all, so a removal would empty the chord rather than repair it. Three have a POSITIVE wrong-time margin, meaning they fail a pitch control rather than showing the absent-note signature that every removal here was licensed by. They are named with bars and margins in interior_evidence_c62.json, and they need a chord-level decision rather than a deletion.

11. Method

One question per note, never a bar average. For each written attack a single high-resolution spectrum is taken from a 150 ms window at the note's mapped time, zero-padded to 0.67 Hz per bin. Salience sums the peak magnitude within 40 cents of the first six partials and divides by that window's own median magnitude, so loud and quiet passages compare. Every hypothesis for a note is read from the same spectrum.

Three controls run on those same spectra, and they are reported before any result:

  • Wrong pitch, tritone. The easy control. Failing it voids everything below it.
  • Wrong pitch, one semitone. The hard control, inside the same spectral neighbourhood.
  • Wrong time. The written pitch at a time the tab writes nothing, which separates "this pitch is sounding" from "a guitar is playing".

Staff-to-stem assignment is measured rather than assumed, by comparing median salience across both stems for every note on a staff.

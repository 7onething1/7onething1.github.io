7. The per-event pass, and the null that was wrong

The lane statistic in section 6 was never going to settle a single note, so all 193 written cymbal events went through an event-level pass. Timestamps were tightened first: 546 kick and snare landmarks gave a sliding local correction that pulled the median residual from -36.0 ms to -1.2 ms, with 67 percent of landmarks inside 50 ms.

The first per-event verdict was threshold-dominated and none of it was promoted. Against a null drawn from quiet gaps the pass returned 55 of 193 supported. Against a null drawn from the eighth-note grid it returned 18 of 193. Same events, same audio, same detector. Both nulls are contaminated: the gap null samples silence, and the grid null samples positions where the ride is audibly playing without being written.

Two claims an external review knocked down, and the reruns

This page was sent out for adversarial review before it was trusted. Two objections were correct, both were runnable, and both were run.

Objection one: raw detector counts cannot carry an omission claim. An earlier draft said the stem shows more cymbal attacks than the tab writes at every threshold, so the disagreement is omission. That reaches past the evidence, because an uncalibrated detector oversegments. The control settles it: run the identical sweep on lanes already trusted.

The detector overcounts on every lane including kick and snare, and the cymbal ratio collapses to 1.3× at the strict setting exactly as kick collapses to 1.0×. The omission claim is withdrawn. What can be said is narrower: across every tested threshold, detected cymbal attacks outnumber written cymbal events, which is consistent with substantial omission, and detector oversegmentation prevents converting that count difference into confirmed missing notes.

Objection two, and it reversed a published conclusion: the within-bar null is biased. Drum positions inside a bar are not exchangeable, because beat phase drives the probability of every surface. The second eighth is not a neutral substitute for the downbeat. Worse for a cymbal lane specifically, if the tab omits genuine events then the seven supposedly negative positions can hold real unwritten cymbal attacks, which inflates the null and punishes a tab for being incomplete.

The repair holds beat phase fixed. Each written note is now scored against the same eighth position in every other bar that carries no written note of that lane there. A downbeat crash is compared with other downbeats and never with its own bar's offbeats.

All five lanes pass once beat phase is held fixed, and the cymbal lane scores second highest of the five. Written cymbals sit at the 78th percentile of their own beat position elsewhere in the song. The principle from section 6 survives all of this intact and is worth keeping separate from the result. A lane-level enrichment measures association across many observations and never certifies an individual annotation. The name for the pattern is an aggregate versus event-level evaluation discrepancy, and MIR reports frame-level and event-level metrics separately for exactly this reason. The lesson is that an aggregate pass and an event-level pass are different tests, and here the event-level test needed two attempts before its null was sound.

The consistent story across the nine is a crash written where the hi-hat is what is playing.

The routing objection, settled

One explanation could overturn every crash finding at once: if the separator writes real crash energy into the Hi-Hat output, then the surviving hi-hat evidence IS the crash. The full-mix attempt failed its control, so the question needed a different instrument. This one works, and it is simple. Take the crashes this tab writes that the stem strongly supports, treat them as ground truth, and ask where their energy actually landed.

The routing objection is refuted, and the two groups do not overlap at all. The worst ground-truth crash still sits 5.8 dB inside the Cymbals stem and the mildest of the nine sits 13.6 dB outside it. Median gap 70.7 dB, Mann-Whitney p = 0.00009. When this recording contains a crash, this separator puts it in the Cymbals output every time, including at the twenty reference rides. At the nine, the Cymbals stem holds 14 to 67 dB less energy than the hi-hat, which is not what routing looks like.

That closes the last open gate on this lane. The nine stand as written crashes the recording does not support, on a test whose positive control passes and whose competing explanation was measured rather than assumed. The caution that remains is sample size, since ground truth here is seven crashes, and the twenty rides behaving identically at -18.9 dB is what carries it past that.

Two of the nine are a different case. Bar 72 carries a real crash about 0.30 s after its written position and bar 43 about 0.45 s after, against an alignment residual whose median is -1.2 ms with 67 percent of landmarks inside 50 ms. Those two read as displacement rather than fabrication, and displacement is not vulnerable to the routing objection in the same way.

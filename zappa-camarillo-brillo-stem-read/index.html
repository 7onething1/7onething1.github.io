Four lanes clear the reference-class threshold at the lane level. Read that precisely: these numbers show significant aggregate correspondence between the written lane and its separated stem, and individual questionable events remain subject to direct stem verification. A lane statistic cannot certify a single note. Only 20.3 percent of written snares and between 10.6 and 13.9 percent of the other written events meet the support criterion, so the statistic establishes identity, guides navigation, and ranks suspicious passages. It does not stand in for hearing the attack at the written instant.

The cymbal lane sits at 1.3×, under the 1.5× floor, so all 193 written cymbal events were carried into a per-event pass, reported in section 7. The 182 in the table above is the subset that maps inside the recording; the other 11 fall past the end of the audio, which is the tab's 240.33 s prediction overrunning a 238.14 s file. Broadband cymbal texture is the known weak class for source separation, and this measurement behaves the way that literature predicts.

7. The per-event cymbal pass

The lane statistic in section 6 was never going to settle a single note, so all 193 written cymbal events went through an event-level pass. Timestamps were tightened first: 546 kick and snare landmarks gave a sliding local correction that pulled the median residual from -36.0 ms to -1.2 ms, with 67 percent of landmarks inside 50 ms.

The per-event verdict is threshold-dominated, so none of it gets promoted. Built against a null drawn from quiet gaps the pass returned 28.5 percent supported. Built against a null drawn from the eighth-note grid it returned 9.3 percent supported. Same events, same audio, same detector. A number that moves that far on null construction alone is not a verdict, and both nulls are contaminated: the gap null samples silence, and the grid null samples positions where the ride is audibly playing without being written.

Snare and kick pass at z above five, so the test itself works. Cymbals, hi-hat and toms fail it. Written cymbal positions are statistically indistinguishable from randomly chosen positions in the same bar, and half of them sit in the bottom half of their own bar's novelty.

This is the measurement that separates a lane claim from a note claim. Section 6 found significant aggregate correspondence on four lanes. At event level only two of those four survive. The same stem pack that can settle an individual snare stroke cannot settle an individual ride stroke, and no aggregate enrichment figure would have exposed that.

9. Settled, open, next

Settled. The five instants are an artifact of a non-matching AI tab. s412176 is the Camarillo Brillo tab that tracks this recording, and it carries zero playability violations. Its kick and snare lanes hold up at event level, at z of +5.71 and +7.39 against a matched within-bar null.

Open. The 193 cymbal events stay unsettled, and the pass established why rather than guessing. This separated Cymbals stem has no event-level discriminating power on this recording, proven by a test that snare and kick both pass. The hi-hat and tom lanes inherit the same limit. Whether s187082 faithfully describes some other Camarillo Brillo performance is untested here, because only this recording was available.

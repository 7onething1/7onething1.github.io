# Magic vs Sorcery: The Top 40 Differences, Ranked

Built 2026-09-10 from 46 Sorcery video transcripts (226,322 words).

## Rebuilds the game (1-6)
Six differences that make Sorcery a different game rather than a Magic variant.

1. **The board is a 5x4 grid and position decides everything**
   - Magic: There is no board. Creatures exist in an abstract zone and location is undefined.
   - Sorcery: Twenty squares. Every unit stands somewhere, and reaching a target is its own problem.
   - Why it ranks 1: Nothing else in the game makes sense until this lands. It rewrites attacking, blocking, removal and threat assessment at once.
   - Source: Questing Beast, Sorcery: The TCG that banned digital art (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=124O3PPg2q4)

2. **You bring two decks and choose which one to draw from**
   - Magic: One deck. Lands and spells are shuffled together and the draw decides.
   - Sorcery: A 60-card Spellbook and a 30-card Atlas of sites. Each draw step you pick the deck.
   - Why it ranks 2: This is the design's whole thesis. It removes the variance Magic treats as a core cost of play.
   - Source: Cardboard Guide, Sorcery in 2026: Is It Too Late to Get in? (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Ex6EOZ_xXs)

3. **Mana screw and mana flood are engineered out**
   - Magic: Drawing too few or too many lands loses games at every level of play.
   - Sorcery: Sites come from their own deck, so you take resources exactly when you want them.
   - Why it ranks 3: The single most quoted reason Magic players switch. It changes what losing even feels like.
   - Source: Cardboard Guide, Sorcery in 2026: Is It Too Late to Get in? (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Ex6EOZ_xXs)

4. **There is no stack, there is a Storyline**
   - Magic: The stack resolves last in, first out, and instant-speed responses stack on top of each other.
   - Sorcery: Effects queue into a Storyline that resolves front to back in the order they were placed.
   - Why it ranks 4: Every Magic instinct about holding up responses has to be relearned from scratch.
   - Source: Rule Zero, What&#x27;s the Problem with Sorcery TCG (and Channel Updates) (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mf8oQMg7Mjs)

5. **Threshold is a requirement you meet, and you never spend it**
   - Magic: Coloured pips are paid. A card costing two red consumes two red mana.
   - Sorcery: Threshold checks how many sites of an element you control. Meeting it costs nothing.
   - Why it ranks 5: One fire site lets you cast every one-fire card you draw. Curve maths from Magic simply does not transfer.
   - Source: SolarGames, My Thoughts on Sorcery TCG (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5xsAXEDofCg)

6. **Every site pays you automatically, with no tapping for mana**
   - Magic: You tap lands one at a time to pay a cost.
   - Sorcery: All your sites generate one mana each at the top of your turn, with no tapping involved.
   - Why it ranks 6: It removes a physical action Magic players perform hundreds of times per game.
   - Source: Good Time Society, Sorcery: Contested Realm - How to Game with Becca Scott (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EGhE-3ov1RY)

## Rewires your turn (7-14)
What you physically do between untap and pass changes here.

7. **Your Avatar is a card on the board that can be walked up to and hit**
   - Magic: You are an abstract life total that creatures attack directly.
   - Sorcery: Your Avatar occupies a square, has power, moves, taps and can be cornered.
   - Why it ranks 7: It turns the player into a piece, which is why every source reaches for chess as the comparison.
   - Source: Surgical Ninja, Is Sorcery TCG worth it in 2026? (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e_P5slaYKhM)

8. **You must be on the same square to attack**
   - Magic: Any untapped creature may attack the opponent from anywhere.
   - Sorcery: A minion attacks what shares or neighbours its square, so threats need travel time.
   - Why it ranks 8: Removal, tempo and racing all change once a threat needs two turns to arrive.
   - Source: SolarGames, My Thoughts on Sorcery TCG (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5xsAXEDofCg)

9. **Unspent mana disappears at end of turn**
   - Magic: Untapped lands stay available and let you hold up responses.
   - Sorcery: Mana you do not spend is gone when the turn ends.
   - Why it ranks 9: The whole Magic habit of passing with mana open has no payoff here.
   - Source: Good Time Society, Sorcery: Contested Realm - How to Game with Becca Scott (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EGhE-3ov1RY)

10. **Killing takes two steps: death's door, then a death blow**
   - Magic: Zero life ends the game immediately.
   - Sorcery: Zero life puts the Avatar on death's door, immune for the rest of that turn. A later hit finishes it.
   - Why it ranks 10: It hands the loser one full turn to answer, which reshapes every race in the game.
   - Source: Wizards&#x27; Den, How to play Sorcery TCG (2024) - Episode 01: Overview (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ONG7JGclej8)

11. **Attacking sites drains life without being able to finish anyone**
   - Magic: All damage to a player is damage and any of it can win the game.
   - Sorcery: Site attacks make the Avatar lose life. Only direct damage delivers the death blow.
   - Why it ranks 11: Two kinds of life loss that behave differently is the trap most new players fall into.
   - Source: Covenant, The Essential Sorcery TCG Tutorial (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ss-PtdOBHcQ)

12. **Minion stats are symmetrical**
   - Magic: Power and toughness vary independently, so 3/1 and 1/3 are different cards.
   - Sorcery: A minion's single power number is its attack and its defense at once.
   - Why it ranks 12: A whole axis of Magic design and evaluation is simply absent.
   - Source: SolarGames, My Thoughts on Sorcery TCG (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5xsAXEDofCg)

13. **Interaction happens almost entirely on your own turn**
   - Magic: Instants let you act at almost any moment during your opponent's turn.
   - Sorcery: Your responses on their turn are Defend and Intercept, and little else.
   - Why it ranks 13: Magic players describe this as the hardest adjustment after the grid.
   - Source: SolarGames, My Thoughts on Sorcery TCG (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5xsAXEDofCg)

14. **Summoning sickness blocks tapping, and tapping is how you act**
   - Magic: A fresh creature cannot attack or tap, and it can still block.
   - Sorcery: A new minion cannot tap that turn, which blocks moving, attacking and tap abilities alike.
   - Why it ranks 14: Because movement costs a tap, a new minion is far more inert here than in Magic.
   - Source: Archives of the Realm, Learn to play Sorcery! 5: Casting sites and spells with mana and threshold (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JYsYs6LnLts)

## Changes combat and the kill (15-24)
Attacking, defending and winning all run on different rules.

15. **Minion damage clears at end of turn while Avatars stay wounded**
   - Magic: All damage clears at cleanup and creatures reset every turn.
   - Sorcery: Minions heal at end of turn. Avatar life loss is permanent for the whole game.
   - Why it ranks 15: Chip damage on minions is worthless, and chip damage on an Avatar is the entire plan.
   - Source: Covenant, The Essential Sorcery TCG Tutorial (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ss-PtdOBHcQ)

16. **There is no limit to how many minions stand on one square**
   - Magic: Creatures occupy no space and stacking is not a concept.
   - Sorcery: Any number of minions can pile onto a single site.
   - Why it ranks 16: It creates board states with real physical card-handling problems at the table.
   - Source: The Winning Agenda, How to Play Sorcery: Contested Realm TCG - Part 1 (The Basics) #sorcerytcg (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M2nEw-7euDk)

17. **The void banishes anything that walks into it**
   - Magic: There is no terrain and nothing kills a creature for standing somewhere.
   - Sorcery: Squares start as void. Most minions that end up there are removed from the game.
   - Why it ranks 17: Terrain becomes a removal spell you never had to draw.
   - Source: Good Time Society, Sorcery: Contested Realm - How to Game with Becca Scott (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EGhE-3ov1RY)

18. **Combat resolves simultaneously with no blocker declaration step**
   - Magic: The attacker declares, then the defender assigns blockers in a separate step.
   - Sorcery: Fighters on a square trade at once, and defending is a move you make with Defend or Intercept.
   - Why it ranks 18: The defensive skill in Magic is choosing blocks. Here it is choosing where to stand.
   - Source: Covenant, The Essential Sorcery TCG Tutorial (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ss-PtdOBHcQ)

19. **Airborne is flying with diagonals attached**
   - Magic: Flying only governs what can block the creature.
   - Sorcery: Airborne units also move and strike diagonally across the grid.
   - Why it ranks 19: An evasion keyword that also doubles your reach has no Magic equivalent.
   - Source: SolarGames, My Thoughts on Sorcery TCG (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5xsAXEDofCg)

20. **Charge is haste, and it is far more valuable here**
   - Magic: Haste lets a creature attack the turn it lands.
   - Sorcery: Charge lets a minion tap immediately, so it can move as well as strike.
   - Why it ranks 20: On a board where crossing distance takes turns, acting immediately is worth more.
   - Source: TCG TALK, Sorcery TCG Archetypes 101 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y86WzqtE37c)

21. **Lethal is deathtouch under another name**
   - Magic: Deathtouch destroys any creature dealt damage by the source.
   - Sorcery: Lethal kills whatever it damages, no matter the target's power.
   - Why it ranks 21: The mechanic carries over cleanly, and only the vocabulary changed.
   - Source: Rule Zero, Sorcery TCG is What Magic Players have been Looking For (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KkcOqXBVtF8)

22. **Ward means something completely different**
   - Magic: Ward taxes an opponent who targets the creature.
   - Sorcery: Ward in Sorcery is its own effect and the Magic reading will mislead you.
   - Why it ranks 22: A false friend on a card you will read wrong on your first day.
   - Source: Covenant, The Essential Sorcery TCG Tutorial (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ss-PtdOBHcQ)

23. **Targets lock when an effect enters the Storyline**
   - Magic: Targets are chosen on announcement and checked again on resolution.
   - Sorcery: A Sorcery effect fixes its target as it is placed into the Storyline.
   - Why it ranks 23: Responses that save a creature by moving it work differently than a Magic player expects.
   - Source: Covenant, The Essential Sorcery TCG Tutorial (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ss-PtdOBHcQ)

24. **Area of effect is measured in squares**
   - Magic: A sweeper names a category of creatures and hits all of them.
   - Sorcery: An area effect hits the squares it covers, so geometry decides who dies.
   - Why it ranks 24: Positioning becomes a defense against removal, which Magic never offers.
   - Source: Rule Zero, What&#x27;s the Problem with Sorcery TCG (and Channel Updates) (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mf8oQMg7Mjs)

## Changes deckbuilding (25-32)
Two lists, capped copies, and a format with no sideboarding.

25. **Rarity is a deckbuilding rule rather than a print run**
   - Magic: Four copies of any card, with basic lands unlimited. Rarity only affects supply.
   - Sorcery: Ordinary allows four, exceptional three, elite two, and unique one.
   - Why it ranks 25: Rarity constrains your list directly, so the power ceiling is capped by design.
   - Source: Questing Beast, Sorcery: The TCG that banned digital art (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=124O3PPg2q4)

26. **Deck construction is 60 spells, 30 sites and an Avatar outside the count**
   - Magic: Sixty cards total including lands, with a fifteen card sideboard.
   - Sorcery: Sixty in the Spellbook, thirty in the Atlas, a ten card Collection, and the Avatar is free.
   - Why it ranks 26: You are building two lists at once and balancing them against each other.
   - Source: GOLDEN EAGLE CARDS, The Ultimate New Player Guide for Getting into Sorcery the Contested Realm! #newplayer (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CHjnsbbM2wE)

27. **The Collection is a third zone you reach into mid-game**
   - Magic: The sideboard is untouchable until games two and three.
   - Sorcery: The Collection is a ten card zone certain cards pull from during play.
   - Why it ranks 27: A tutor target zone that lives outside the deck has no Magic analogue.
   - Source: GOLDEN EAGLE CARDS, The Ultimate New Player Guide for Getting into Sorcery the Contested Realm! #newplayer (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CHjnsbbM2wE)

28. **Best of one means no sideboarding in the Magic sense**
   - Magic: Match play is best of two out of three with sideboard cards between games.
   - Sorcery: Games are best of one, so the Collection serves a different purpose entirely.
   - Why it ranks 28: The entire skill of sideboarding does not exist in this format.
   - Source: GOLDEN EAGLE CARDS, The Ultimate New Player Guide for Getting into Sorcery the Contested Realm! #newplayer (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CHjnsbbM2wE)

29. **Sites carry abilities the way Magic's utility lands do**
   - Magic: Most lands are basics that only produce mana.
   - Sorcery: Every site brings mana, a threshold symbol and often a printed ability.
   - Why it ranks 29: Your mana base is thirty decisions rather than a curve calculation.
   - Source: SolarGames, My Thoughts on Sorcery TCG (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5xsAXEDofCg)

30. **Card text is deliberately short**
   - Magic: Modern cards carry long rules text and heavy keyword loads.
   - Sorcery: Sorcery cards stay brief and the difficulty sits in the board instead.
   - Why it ranks 30: Complexity moved from reading the card to reading the table.
   - Source: Rule Zero, Sorcery TCG is What Magic Players have been Looking For (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KkcOqXBVtF8)

31. **Mana spending is tracked in your head**
   - Magic: Tapping lands leaves a physical record both players can audit.
   - Sorcery: Sites do not tap for mana, so the count is spoken and remembered.
   - Why it ranks 31: Reviewers name this as the game's weakest point for tournament play.
   - Source: SolarGames, My Thoughts on Sorcery TCG (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5xsAXEDofCg)

32. **One set a year**
   - Magic: Multiple premier sets a year plus supplemental products.
   - Sorcery: Eric's Curiosa ships a single set each year.
   - Why it ranks 32: Your collection stays current for twelve months at a time.
   - Source: Surgical Ninja, Is Sorcery TCG worth it in 2026? (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e_P5slaYKhM)

## Changes the hobby (33-40)
Art policy, release cadence, price and whether anyone near you plays.

33. **All art is physical media, with no digital and no AI**
   - Magic: Card art is overwhelmingly digital painting.
   - Sorcery: Every piece is handmade in physical media, and digital art is disallowed outright.
   - Why it ranks 33: The most cited non-gameplay reason players give for buying in.
   - Source: Questing Beast, Sorcery: The TCG that banned digital art (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=124O3PPg2q4)

34. **Artists keep their copyright**
   - Magic: Card art is typically work for hire.
   - Sorcery: Sorcery artists retain copyright, and originals and artist proofs stay theirs to sell.
   - Why it ranks 34: A structural difference in how the game treats the people who make it.
   - Source: Distraction Makers, Finally, a TCG That Gives a S**t (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z_nbzNu8xw8)

35. **Many Sorcery artists are the old Magic artists**
   - Magic: Early Magic artists have largely moved on from the game.
   - Sorcery: A large share of the Sorcery roster painted early Magic cards.
   - Why it ranks 35: The visual lineage is deliberate and it is the pitch to lapsed players.
   - Source: ToGoBox, Collectors Are Leaving Magic The Gathering For Sorcery Contested Realm (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KG7PzkMGJGE)

36. **There is no spoiler season**
   - Magic: Full set spoilers arrive weeks ahead of release.
   - Sorcery: Cards stay unrevealed, and Curios turn up in boxes unannounced.
   - Why it ranks 36: Opening a box carries genuine discovery, which Magic traded away years ago.
   - Source: Rule Zero, Sorcery TCG is What Magic Players have been Looking For (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KkcOqXBVtF8)

37. **Alpha corners are a deliberate callback**
   - Magic: Modern printing uses standard corner radius.
   - Sorcery: The first print run uses the rounded corner profile of Alpha Magic cards.
   - Why it ranks 37: A detail aimed squarely at players who remember 1993.
   - Source: SolarGames, My Thoughts on Sorcery TCG (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5xsAXEDofCg)

38. **Products do not compete with each other for attention**
   - Magic: Releases stack up and drown one another out across the same weekends.
   - Sorcery: A single yearly release gets the room to itself.
   - Why it ranks 38: Reviewers describe this as the difference between an event and a treadmill.
   - Source: Rule Zero, What&#x27;s the Problem with Sorcery TCG (and Channel Updates) (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mf8oQMg7Mjs)

39. **Sealed product runs far more expensive per box**
   - Magic: A Magic booster box sits well under a hundred dollars at most retailers.
   - Sorcery: Gothic booster boxes were listed between 209 and 220 dollars on 2026-09-10.
   - Why it ranks 39: Entry through the four-deck precon box is the sane route in.
   - Source: Collector Arthouse, Is Sorcery: Contested Realm Stagnating? Growth, Distribution &amp; LGS Support (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H36hAhhXRqU)

40. **Finding opponents is the real risk**
   - Magic: Magic has a game in nearly every store, most nights.
   - Sorcery: Sorcery play depends on a local scene that may not exist near you.
   - Why it ranks 40: Every source names this as the one thing that decides whether the game works for you.
   - Source: Collector Arthouse, Is Sorcery: Contested Realm Stagnating? Growth, Distribution &amp; LGS Support (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H36hAhhXRqU)

1. The verdict

No single tool does this job, and the separation stage is a cascade rather than a choice. Demucs strips the band, the Moises app splits the guitars by role, Songsterr transcribes the rhythm, ours fixes ownership and playability, and a hand-built tracker handles the fast lead. Each tool is the best available at exactly one stage and demonstrably wrong at another.

The panning in these mixes is slight and real. It carries usable information on one staff at 72.1% and none on the other at 53.5%, so it earns a vote rather than an assignment. Reverb and delay returns are panned separately from the dry signal, which puts a measurement bug inside every pan number here.

2. Separation: the Demucs into Moises cascade

Demucs separates by instrument, so two guitars come back as one file. Every Radiohead song in the library shows it: a single -guitar.wav per song folder. Six-stem separation collapses both players into one mono stem at that step.

Pan-based masking is the usual workaround and it is disqualified by the premise here. On 3 Gene it worked well, dropping the two-guitar correlation from 0.66 to 0.15 on the solo region, because those guitars were panned apart. A track with weak panning gives that method almost nothing to mask on.

The working method is a cascade, and the two stages do different jobs

Stage 1, Demucs, strips the band. Free, local, and it produces a guitar-only file that no longer has to be defended against drums, bass, and vocals.

Stage 2, the Moises app, splits the guitars by role. Given guitar-only input, its Lead and Rhythm model spends all of its capacity on the one distinction that matters here. Role is a completely different axis from position, which is why it survives a mix with no useful panning.

Proof on disk at /Volumes/T7 Shield/Moises_Stems/, six songs: BELIEVEYOUME 03, 04, 05, 08, 09 and Goldfrapp Clowns, carrying Rhythm Guitars, Solo Guitars and Other Guitar. Three older app exports (The Alligator, Endless Summer, Lazarus) carry a lead and rhythm pair.

The one measured cost of the cascade, and how to pay less of it

Demucs throws away the guitar's low register before Moises ever sees it. Measured on the Sean song from htdemucs_6s: the guitar stem retains 1.32% of the mix's energy at 60-90 Hz and 9.82% at 90-130 Hz. It is effectively high-passed, because the separator has to put the shared low band somewhere and sends it to bass.

In a cascade that loss is permanent and inherited by both output parts. A chord transcribed downstream comes back missing its lowest note, every time. A matcher validated at 87% root recovery on a synthetic render scored 29% on the real stem, and in every disagreement the detected set was the tab's chord with the bottom pitch class removed.

htdemucs_6s is also the weakest Demucs model on transients and the most prone to spectral smearing, so stage 2 inherits stage 1's artifacts as well.

Three ways to keep the cascade and pay less for it. All three are untested and each is a one-song experiment.

A. Feed Moises guitar plus bass summed rather than the guitar stem alone. The guitar's low notes ended up in the bass stem, so summing them back puts the evidence in front of Moises while still removing drums and vocals. Moises then decides what is guitar, which is the job it is trained for.

B. Feed a mix minus drums and vocals. Same idea with a wider net, removing only the two sources that are loudest and least guitar-like. Least lossy input, most work left for Moises.

C. Take the low register from the full mix in parallel and reconcile it against whatever the cascade returns. This is a standing hard gate and it works regardless of which input wins.

The control that settles A against B against the plain guitar stem: run all three on one song and measure 60-130 Hz energy retention plus the written-against-deliberately-wrong pitch separation on each output.

3. Slight panning is real evidence, and it is not a splitter

there's often slight panning but we must not treat that as full panning plus sometimes reverb trails are fed panned etc. Brandon, 2026-08-17
we struggled by thinking panning would separate the guitars rather than frequencies, and matching and fixing what songsterr got wrong, and started to get insane and burn a wild amount of tokens. Brandon, 2026-08-13

An earlier version of this page said the panning was not there to be read. That overstates it and the numbers below say so. A 2.30 dB margin is slight panning that genuinely exists. Read as a hard left and right split it produces chance. Read as one weak vote among several it is worth having. Appleseed Cast, Kilgore Trout, against a synthetic control rendered with the Lead hard left and the Rhythm hard right so the answer is known:

LeadRhythm
Cross-stem median margin, real audio2.30 dB2.96 dB
Ownership agreement, real audio53.5%, chance72.1%
Cross-stem margin, synthetic control21.8 dB19.3 dB
Ownership agreement, synthetic control88.0%83.7%

The detector was fine. The panning present was roughly a tenth of what the method assumed. That distinction took 57 chain builds to establish, and the synthetic control settled it in one run.

Read the two real-audio rows carefully, because they do not say the same thing. Rhythm at 72.1% is well above chance and is usable evidence. Lead at 53.5% is a coin flip. One signal, two very different qualities on the two staves, from the same recording. Any rule that treats pan as uniformly good or uniformly worthless gets one of these two wrong.

The practical shape this argues for, and it is untested. Stop assigning every note from pan. Take only the notes whose pan margin clears a real threshold as seeds, then propagate ownership from those seeds by register and phrase continuity. A 2.30 dB median means about half the notes sit below any usable margin, which is exactly the arithmetic that drags an aggregate down to 53.5%. The skill carries a 3 dB doubling threshold as an initial cut, so the seed set exists and is simply not being used as a seed set.

Reverb and delay returns are panned separately from the dry signal

This is a measurement bug hiding inside every pan number on this page. The current method reads the L/R energy balance over the 80 ms following each transient, and the ownership harmonic measurement reads 80 to 150 ms after the attack. A dry electric guitar attack is roughly 5 to 20 ms. Everything after that window is increasingly early reflections, delay repeats, and reverb, and those returns are routed and panned independently of the dry signal. A player panned slightly left with a wide stereo return can measure as centred, or as right of centre.

The corpus contains the proof, and we interpreted it differently at the time. A single guitar through a Haas delay produced an L/R correlation of 0.049, the lowest of anything measured, real tracks included. A ping-pong delay produced the highest onset independence at 59.1%. Both are one player. Both were logged as evidence that those metrics measure mix width. They also measure the wet return, which is the more actionable reading.

Three fixes, all untested, all cheap to try.

A. Move the pan window onto the dry attack. Measure roughly the first 10 to 20 ms rather than 80 ms, so the estimate is taken before the return arrives.

B. Make window drift a rejection criterion. Compute the pan estimate at 15 ms and again at 80 ms. Agreement means the note's position is a dry position and the note votes. Disagreement means the tail is panned differently from the source, so discard that note rather than letting it vote wrong. This turns Brandon's observation into a gate that can abort, which is the one thing that separates a real detector from a confident one here.

C. Detect the delay before counting attacks. A panned repeat is a copy of an earlier attack at a fixed lag. Autocorrelate the onset train per channel; a strong peak at a constant lag is a delay line, and those onsets are echoes rather than notes. Run this before the bimodality count, since a ping-pong repeat manufactures a second pan cluster out of one player.

How much of the transcription is written into both staves

Because the guitars are not hard panned, a per-channel transcription writes the same note into both parts. Notes appearing in both channels at the same instant and pitch:

Every one of those was written into both staves. A chord built from one note the left guitarist played and one the right guitarist played is what forces the unplayable stretch and the skipped middle string. Per-note depanning on A Tree For Trials moved skip rates from 75% and 82% down to 38% and 33% with the pitches preserved.

The one metric that survived its controls: per-attack pan bimodality. L/R correlation, side energy, and onset independence were each beaten by a synthetic one-guitar case. A single guitar through a Haas delay produced the lowest correlation of anything measured, real tracks included, at 0.049. Bimodality reads 0.79 for two synthetic guitars at both wide and subtle pan, against 0.22 to 0.51 for real single-source stems.

State its limit every time: it counts pan positions rather than people. One guitarist overdubbing a second part to the same position scores as ONE. Pair it with cross-staff duplication. On The Ship that separated 11 Ambulance, 60% duplication and a real defect, from 08 JGBFTL, 0.7% and a correct overdub.

4. Transcription: Songsterr wins the rhythm and quits on the solo

Where Songsterr beats us outright

On Kilgore Trout, Songsterr's own file measured 73.7% Rhythm ownership, the best of any file tested, and its Rhythm pitch support beat our stem-derived source 41.1% against 24.7%. Our best register-based ownership reached 66.6% on both staves against measured ceilings of 84.0% and 77.5%. Being stem-derived is a statement about production. It carries no information about accuracy.

Where Songsterr stops trying, which is the thing you asked about

you are just redoing the rhythm parts and skipping the fast hard part lazily Brandon, on The Ship redo

This is documented with numbers, and it has a specific shape. Songsterr returns a solo transcribed an octave low, or a lead track collapsed into the bottom of the neck. An empty track would be obvious. These two shapes pass for a finished tab under any check that counts notes.

More notes is not automatically better, and 08 and 10 are the proof. Two of eight songs came back worse from our own pipeline. Any rule of the form "replace Songsterr's lead" would have damaged those two files. Measure per song before swapping.

Our own solo transcription is worse, not better

The reason Songsterr's octave-low solo survived so long is that the alternatives measured worse. On The Ship "Gene", pyin and torchcrepe agreed on only 58% of the solo notes and disagreed on 42%, and both missed the F#5 Brandon corrected by ear. Hand-rolled pitch detection fails on fast, dense, distorted guitar because each note rings through the next attack and every tracker locks onto the ringing note.

The method that does work on a stem holding more than one guitar is a Viterbi melody tracker, taking the top N autocorrelation peaks per frame and running dynamic programming for the smoothest path that rewards salience and height, then position-based DP fretting over the note sequence rather than lowest-fret-per-note. That combination brought the average fret jump to about 1.5 and kept the hand in position.

The pattern behind almost all of it: a confident number computed off evidence that was thrown away earlier in the chain. The Demucs stem discards the low register and then reports the chord as wrong. 7. The recommended chain

Step 0. Stereo pass on the raw mix, before any separation. Costs nothing, no model weights. Detect panned delay repeats first, then compute per-attack pan bimodality on a dry-attack window to decide one guitar or two. Pair it with cross-staff duplication so an overdub to the same position is not miscounted as a single player.

Step 1. Demucs strips the band. htdemucs_6s, free and local, producing a guitar-only file. Keep the bass stem beside it, because the guitar's low notes are inside it.

Step 2. The Moises Premium app splits the guitars by role. Lead and Rhythm, from guitar-only input so the model spends everything on that one distinction. This is the step Demucs cannot perform, and it is the reason to spend the $3.99. Until the input experiment is run, feed it guitar plus bass summed rather than the guitar stem alone, so the low register is still in front of it. If a returned stem is stereo, an ffmpeg channelsplit recovers a further L and R pair from it.

Step 3. Take the low register from the full mix in parallel. Below 130 Hz the Demucs guitar stem holds 1.32% of the energy, so anything downstream of it is guessing about the bottom of every chord.

Step 4. Songsterr transcribes each role stem separately. The rhythm stem into Songsterr is the strongest single link in the whole chain. Prefer the AI-generated tab where both exist.

Step 5. Do not accept Songsterr's first pass on the solo. Check the lead track's top note and fret range. A lead capping near E5 with frets under 12, on audio that clearly has content an octave up, is the octave-low failure. Rebuild that part with the Viterbi tracker plus position DP fretting, then Brandon's ear decides.

Step 6. Our gate runs last. Use pan as a seed rather than as an assignment, discarding any note whose 15 ms and 80 ms pan estimates disagree. Restaff as a whole-part operation, dedupe the unison, repair only the beats that fail. Never a blanket refret. Verify the pitches on an HPSS harmonic separation of the full mix with a written-against-deliberately-wrong control.

Step 7. Ship through /songsterr-upload with preflight_import.py and the upload lease, so nothing waits on Brandon and no two sessions drive the same browser tab.

What the plugin world offers that the model world does not: human-guided, note-level separation. Melodyne DNA and RipX hand back individual note objects you can audition and reassign by ear, rather than a stem you have to re-transcribe. Our problem is note-level ownership, so that shape fits the problem better than any stem splitter does. Neither is installed, and both are paid, so they sit behind the Moises control in priority.

Seven routes checked in total. Four are measured or structurally dead. Two credible plugin routes are not installed. Moises is the one route that is both capable and already available, which is what makes it the next test rather than the answer.

Moises is the last untried route, and it is not a proven fix. Nobody has put a Moises Solo or Rhythm stem through the same shifted-time control that killed our own splitter. If the Moises split also sorts by register, it fails identically and the ownership problem stays open. Run that control before spending anything on the assumption that it works.

9. The four experiments nobody has run

Everything on this page that concerns the cascade is an argument rather than a measurement. Four tests turn it into evidence, and every one of them runs on material that is on the T7 drive now.

#TestWhat it settlesCost
1Moises Solo Guitars stem into SongsterrWhether the octave-low solo failure is caused by the lead being buried under a rhythm part in the same stem. If it is, better input replaces the whole hand-built trackerOne transcription, zero credits
2Three Moises inputs on one song: guitar stem alone, guitar plus bass summed, mix minus drums and vocalsHow much of the 60-130 Hz loss the cascade has to accept, and which input keeps the most of itThree separations on a Premium sub
3Pan estimate at 15 ms against the same estimate at 80 ms, per noteHow many notes have wet-contaminated pan, which is the size of the reverb-trail problemPure numpy, no model
4Seeded ownership: assign only above the 3 dB margin, propagate the rest by register and phraseWhether the 53.5% Lead figure is a bad signal or a badly used oneRerun of an existing script

Test 3 is the one to run first, because it costs nothing, needs no subscription, and its result changes how every other pan number on this page should be read. It is also the only one of the four that can invalidate work we have shipped.

Honest ceiling today

For a two or three guitar track with slight panning, right now: a good rhythm tab and a contested lead. The rhythm side is production-ready through Demucs into Moises Premium into Songsterr into our gate. The lead side depends on a hand-built tracker and Brandon's ear on every fast passage, and that is where the whole gap is. Test 1 is the one that could close it.

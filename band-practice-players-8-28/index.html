How the takes were judged

The detector treats a false start as a take the band tried again, and compares each take head-to-head against the ones that follow using chroma-sequence similarity with lag alignment. 22 of the 27 keepers pass a from-scratch re-check measured against the room floor rather than against their own quiet passages. The other 5 carry a brief internal stop near the head; they were left whole rather than trimmed, so nothing played is lost. Measured against the source, the kept takes average −6.7 dB and the dropped fragments −17.1 dB.

Why this replaces the 12-song read

The first pass on this tape returned 12 songs and 34 minutes. That was a detector fault, not a quiet session. Calibrating what counts as playing, the finder weighted pulse salience heaviest of its four features. On this board mix that feature carries no signal at all: measured inside the 12 confirmed songs it read lower than outside them. So the heaviest voice in the clustering was noise, and it both dropped 90 minutes of playing outright and clipped the songs it did keep down to their loudest cores. Each of the original 12 turns out to sit inside a longer take here.

Two independent checks settle it. An energy and low-end profile built from scratch, calibrated on the 12 confirmed songs, recalls 94.5% of them and marks 37 further stretches, 90 minutes, that the detector never cut a take from. Transcribing samples of those stretches returns zero words, matching a known song and unlike a known conversation window from the same tape, which transcribes as speech. The stretches are the band playing. The finder now takes --calib loudness, which drops salience from the split; the default is unchanged for every other tape.

## Occult America

Mitch Horowitz, Bantam / Random House 2009, ISBN 9780553385151. 160 pages, 92,049 words.

**Extraction warning.** The PDF text layer drops every fi, fl and ff ligature. `first` becomes `rst` (139 times), `influence` becomes `in uence` (49), `different` becomes `di erent` (34), `offered` becomes `o ered` (16). Retype quotes against the page image or run a ligature repair first.

| Chapter | Words | What is in it | Mine for |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Introduction: What Is the Occult? | 2,894 | Opens in 1693 with Johannes Kelpius leading forty German pilgrims out of the Rhine Valley to a log tabernacle on the Wissahickon Creek outside Philadelphia, where they studied astrology, alchemy, Kabala and number symbolism and scanned the stars through a roof telescope. Sets Horowitz's thesis against the Crowley stereotype: American occultists turned mystical ideas into tools of social progress and self-help. **Sections:** Sons of Frankenstein / The Silver Moon / Of Dime Horoscopes | Kelpius and the Tabernacle in the Forest; Jemima Wilkinson, the Publick Universal Friend, first American-born woman to preach publicly; Andrew Jackson Davis, the Poughkeepsie Seer; Wallace D. Wattles and the line to The Secret; Marcus Garvey and mind power |
| 1. The Psychic Highway | 10,493 | The longest chapter. Upstate New York's Burned-Over District as the incubator of American religious invention. Runs from the last Western witch trials and Cagliostro's 1791 Vatican death sentence through Mormonism, Millerite apocalypse, Shaker settlements and the Fox sisters. **Sections:** The Burned-Over District / Apocalypse Postponed / The Universal Friend / Pioneer Prophetess / The Lost Tribe / The Widow's Son / Paradise Found / The Poughkeepsie Seer / Mesmer's Children | Why one corridor of New York produced so many faiths; Freemasonry and the Morgan affair; Mesmerism arriving in America; Spiritualism's founding events |
| 2. Mystic Americans | 8,176 | Occultism goes urban and international. Opens on West 47th Street in Manhattan as an unlikely birthplace of a spiritual revolution, then follows Blavatsky, Olcott and the Theosophical Society, the Transcendentalists reading Eastern scripture, and Spiritualism reaching into the Lincoln White House. **Sections:** The Journey East / Transcendental Magic / Mystical Europe / The Spiritualist Tide / Mysterious Numbers / Mary Todd Lincoln, Spiritualist / Supernatural Politics / Spirit Journeys | Blavatsky and the founding of Theosophy; Emerson, Thoreau and Eastern texts; Mary Todd Lincoln's seances; Spiritualism's links to abolition and suffrage |
| 3. Don't Try This at Home: Ouija and the Selling of Spiritualism | 4,589 | The shortest chapter and the most self-contained. A commercial history of the Ouija board, from the Baltimore investors who patented it to the 1960s teenagers using one in a suburban bedroom. Horowitz credits historians Robert Murch and Eugene Orlando in the notes for tracing the investor relations. **Sections:** Spiritual Telegraphs / Baltimore Oracles / True Origins / Ancient Ouija? / Ouija Boom / Ouija in Winter / Creepyville | Who actually invented and patented the board; William Fuld and the million-dollar Baltimore Sun claim of 1920; Debunking the ancient-origin story; James Merrill, David Jackson and the board as literary method |
| 4. The Science of Right Thinking | 6,512 | The New Thought chapter, and the origin story for the whole positive-thinking industry. Opens on a bleak Christmas in LaPorte, Indiana in 1896 at the home of Wallace D. Wattles, a Methodist minister and Christian socialist whose episcopate was moving to dismiss him. **Sections:** A Science of God / Healthy Thinking / The Secret of the Ages / The Reluctant Organizer / The Problem of Evil / Behind the Power | Wattles, whose writing later drove The Secret; Phineas Quimby and mental healing; Christian Science and its splits; New Thought as wealth redistribution rather than personal enrichment |
| 5. The Mail-Order Prophet | 5,337 | A single-figure chapter on Frank B. Robinson, an Idaho druggist who built a mail-order religion called Psychiana out of newspaper, magazine and radio advertising, running from the start of the Depression to just after the Second World War. **Sections:** The Gathering Storm / A Wandering Prophet / A Search Bears Fruit / The Psychiana Method / Of Money and Spirit / American Spiritual Awakening / Remains of the Day | How a faith was sold by direct mail; The advertising copy itself; Money and doctrine entangled; What survived him |
| 6. Go Tell Pharaoh: The Rise of Magic in Afro-America | 10,015 | Tied for the longest chapter. Opens with Frederick Douglass, who had no use for folklore, and works through hoodoo and root work, the overlap between Black folk magic and Jewish mystical publishing, the stage magician Black Herman, Marcus Garvey, and Noble Drew Ali's Moorish Science Temple. **Sections:** The Magic Root / Mojo Bags and Menorahs / Professor Black Herman / Poor Man's Psychologist / Black Moses / Circle 7 Koran | Douglass and the magic root story; Hoodoo supply houses and their catalogues; Black Herman as performer and healer; The Circle Seven Koran |
| 7. The Return of the "Secret Teachings" | 5,305 | Manly P. Hall. Opens with librarians growing used to a hulking young man with long hair requesting old occult works daily in the New York Public Library reading room. Follows him to The Secret Teachings of All Ages and the Philosophical Research Society. **Sections:** A Philosopher's Progress / A Fitful Idealist / A Private World / The Mystic in Decline / The Enduring Value of The Secret Teachings | How a self-taught twenty-something produced the standard occult encyclopedia; Funding and publishing the 1928 folio; His long decline; Why the book outlasted him |
| 8. New Deal of the Ages: Politics and the Occult | 9,232 | The occult inside American government. Opens on Truman Capote in the summer of 1968 speculating about a link between the King and Kennedy killings, then moves to Henry A. Wallace, the Great Seal on the dollar bill, Nicholas Roerich, and the mystical fringe of American fascism. **Sections:** A Searcher for the Infinite / The Eye and the Pyramid / Seven Minutes in Eternity / Prophet of Hate / Fascism and the Occult | Henry Wallace, Roosevelt's vice president, and his correspondence; How the eye and pyramid reached the one dollar bill in 1935; William Dudley Pelley and the Silver Shirts; The occult strand in 1930s American fascism |
| 9. The Masters Among Us | 5,261 | Ascended masters and the people who claimed contact with them. Opens at the Unity Metaphysical Center in Helena, Montana over Baird Spalding's Life and Teaching of the Masters of the Far East, then follows Guy and Edna Ballard's Mighty I AM movement. **Sections:** Invisible College / The Book of Gold / The Camera of Past Events / Darkness Falls / A Real Phony / Black Magicians | Spalding and the Far East masters books; The Ballards and the I AM movement; The federal mail-fraud case against I AM; Where sincerity ends and fraud begins |
| 10. Secrets for Sale | 6,291 | The American break with European lodge secrecy. Opens on Annie Besant's endless inner orders and the Golden Dawn's initiation requirements, then contrasts them with American occultists who sold the material openly by catalogue and boardwalk. **Sections:** Occult Rebel / A Magician's Legacy / Try! / The Religion of the Stars / Go West, Young Magician / The Magic Boardwalk / Have You a Problem??? | Paschal Beverly Randolph; C. C. Zain and the Church of Light; Zolar and popular astrology; Occult mail-order advertising as a business |
| 11. "The Greatest Mystic Who Ever Lived in America" | 6,429 | Edgar Cayce. Opens with a broken man arriving for a reading who had been business manager for the Silver Shirts and a Pennsylvania Klan organizer, and who had helped build the Ballards' I AM movement. Ties chapters 8, 9 and 11 together in one scene. **Sections:** The Occult Philosophy / The Power of Past Lives / The Esoteric Healer / Come as You Were | Cayce's readings and their content; Reincarnation entering American popular belief; The healing practice; His afterlife as an institution |
| Epilogue: Aquarius Rising: The New Age Dawns | 3,083 | How the occult became the New Age. Jet propulsion and spaceflight redirect the American imagination outward in the 1950s, flying-saucer contactees fuse with the Masters of Wisdom, and by the mid-1970s New Age Journal has named the thing. **Sections:** In the Cards / The Spiritual Invasion / The Revolution Will Be Published | Tarot's American popularisation; Eastern teachers arriving after 1965; New Age publishing as the delivery system; The naming of the movement |

## Over-Nite Sensation, the two text files

Steve Huey's AllMusic review and William Ruhlmann's AllMusic Zappa biography, plus one rip note with no editorial content.

| Point | Detail | Against the books |
|---|---|---|
| It was Zappa's first gold album. | Steve Huey calls Over-Nite Sensation the watershed where the post-sixties aesthetic was established, and names it his first gold record. Neither Benac nor Watson states this outright. | NEW |
| The post-accident voice is the storytelling instrument. | Huey ties the growling new voice directly to the Rainbow Theatre fall, and makes it the thing that carries the songs. | NEW ANGLE |
| Tina Turner and the Ikettes, uncredited, on 'Montana'. | Same fact Benac and Watson both carry. Three independent sources now agree. | CONFIRMS |
| The misogyny charge, and Zappa's answer to it. | Huey lays out the defence that the satire targets male stupidity, then argues the glee in Zappa's delivery undercuts it. A sharper critical position than either book takes. | NEW ANGLE |
| Verve insisted on adding 'of Invention' to the band name. | Ruhlmann's biography note. Watson says the addition disguised the intended connotation. | CONFIRMS |
| Zappa's band as a training ground. | Ruhlmann compares it to Miles Davis's role in jazz. A framing neither Zappa book uses. | NEW ANGLE |
| Three chart albums in 1968. | We're Only In It for the Money, Lumpy Gravy and Cruising with Ruben and the Jets all charted in the same year. | CONFIRMS |
| Six months at Chaffey College in Alta Loma. | Ruhlmann's detail on the schooling, and on Zappa using film-score money to buy a studio. | NEW |
| The Havel appearance was the exception after 1988. | Ruhlmann notes the 1988 tour ended live performance apart from isolated dates like Czechoslovakia at Havel's invitation. | CONFIRMS |
| Born 21 December 1940 in Baltimore, died 4 December 1993 in Los Angeles. | Base biographical dates, useful as a spine. | REFERENCE |

# Community continuity ledger, pass ten

Two claims from the ledger's delayed-payoff column, both of which had been sitting in the research queue without a completed evidence chain. Both are now confirmed, and one of them is the best-evidenced entry in the whole project.

## The Ass Crack Bandit seed, with the speaker settled

The ledger records Annie mentioning the Bandit during her earlier student politics material, seasons ahead of the episode built around catching him. The claim is exactly right, and the transcript settles the speaker without needing a subtitle tag.

Annie is named at 00:09:10, immediately ahead of the platform speech. Her platform runs from 00:09:11 to 00:09:31 as a list of civic promises. She is named again at 00:09:33, by surname, as the moderator thanks her.

The Bandit line lands at 00:09:18, in third position, between removing black mold from a stairwell and balancing the school budget by eliminating administrative redundancies.

The position is what makes it a seed rather than a throwaway. A fictional campus criminal is being treated as an ordinary municipal problem, ranked beside mould and accounting. Three seasons later the show builds a procedural episode around him.

Speaker settled by direct naming on both sides of the line. Status: confirmed, S02E17 cues 224 through 233, payoff episode outside evidence.

## The Bandit line also supports the ledger's FBI reading

The ledger argues that Annie's eventual FBI path accumulates from years of investigation stories, student elections, procedural arguments and a love of formal systems. This one speech contains three of those at once.

She runs on institutional repair, she promises to bring a criminal to justice, and she proposes an administrative audit. The through-line the ledger describes is present in a single twenty-second stretch of Season Two.

Status: confirmed as supporting evidence, S02E17 00:09:11 to 00:09:31.

## Chang's pile of nothing is real, and the ledger had the wording right

The ledger flagged a strange noun construction as a credible source for a later recurrence, and asked for both quotations placed side by side with dates, and only then assigned a status. The earlier one exists.

It appears at 00:01:44 in "Asian Population Studies," phrased as a question about who has Chang's pile of nothing. The construction is attached to Chang by name inside the line itself, so the claim is self-attributing under the rule pass eight established.

The later occurrence sits in a season with no video on this machine, so the pairing stays half-complete. What changes is that the ledger's source quotation is no longer a memory. Status: source confirmed, payoff outside evidence, S02E12 cue 49.

## A third negative overturned, and the cause is corpus growth

Pass six recorded the pile-of construction as absent under both a strict and a loose pattern across 23 episodes. Three instances exist across the 28.

Beside the Chang line there is a pile of treasure in "Advanced Dungeons & Dragons" at 00:03:47 and a pile of campaign fliers in "Intro to Political Science" at 00:03:22. All three sit in episodes that entered evidence through optical recognition after pass six ran.

The cause carries weight in the ledger's own error record. This negative was accurate when it was published and became wrong when the corpus grew. That is a separate failure from the triple-cool tic, the Britta question and the Magnitude catchphrase, all three of which were broken patterns hiding evidence that was already present.

Running total: four negatives overturned, three by broken patterns and one by corpus growth. Status: negative withdrawn, cause recorded as growth.

## What the two confirmations share

Both entries were sitting in the ledger's research queue with the connection remembered more securely than the wording. Both turned out to be correct as remembered, and both gained something the memory did not carry: a settled speaker in one case, a self-attributing construction in the other.

That is worth recording as a pattern in its own right. Across ten passes the ledger's recalled connections have held up considerably better than its episode assignments, which have now been wrong twice.

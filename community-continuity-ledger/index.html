The evidence base and its limits

The corpus is built from the BluRay discs on this machine. Eighteen episodes carried an embedded English subtitle track, and those were extracted to SRT, parsed into cues, and indexed by timecode. That yields 7,828 cues across eighteen episodes, spanning Season One episodes 2 through 19 and five episodes from Season Two.

One limitation governs the whole pass. Subtitle tracks carry no speaker attribution, so any statement about who says a line is marked inferred from placement. A speaker-tagged transcript would settle those. Episodes with no local video sit outside evidence entirely, and silence from this corpus is never treated as refusal for them.

Spanish 101 opens as one continuous stretch, not five scattered seeds

Structural · confirmed by timecode

The ledger had been treating Abed's television framing, Britta's reality question, the courtly greeting, and Jeff's entrance as separate seeds inside a dense episode. The timecodes show them occupying roughly ninety seconds of a single unbroken sequence.

S01E02 00:00:46 cue 20 — Abed says the Dean's announcements make every ten minutes feel like a new scene of a TV show
S01E02 00:00:54 cue 24 — Abed names the device aloud and the illusion collapses
S01E02 00:01:57 cue 52 — Britta asks whether he understands television and life are separate
S01E02 00:02:04 cue 54 — the courtly greeting, both halves in one cue
S01E02 00:02:15 cue 59 — Abed grades the entrance, Jeff says it was staged for him
Speakers inferred from placement. Subtitle track carries no attribution.

Reading those five beats as one sequence changes what the episode is doing. The show establishes Abed's television grammar. It tests that grammar through Britta, demonstrates it through Jeff, then confirms that Jeff performs inside it on purpose, and the third minute has not arrived.

Status vocabulary and the three-way evidence split

Subtitles give exact wording. They give exact timing. They give no speaker, no stage direction, and no camera information. That divides the ledger's claims three ways, and the division should govern every future pass.

The rule this pass adds

The background childbirth, the RoboCop display, the buried study table, and the whole costume ledger sit outside what a subtitle can test. Marking any of them confirmed on the strength of this corpus would be a false citation. They keep their existing grade until a visual evidence layer exists.

Pass five

The evidence base grew from 18 episodes to 23, and from 7,828 cues to 10,136. Five Season Two episodes were sitting outside the folder the extractor had been searching, including the three carrying the densest claim clusters in the ledger. Five more sit on disc with bitmap subtitles that need optical recognition to enter evidence.

Five of the new episodes carry partial speaker tags, placed where a speaker sits off-screen. Coverage is one percent of cues, so those tags settle individual lines rather than the corpus as a whole.

Four negatives flipped, and one of the flips was my own bad probe

Method correction

Running each claim twice, once strict and once loose, produced four apparent reversals. Three are real. The fourth is a lesson.

Chang ventilation claim — loose pattern returned 23 hits across 12 episodes
Every hit was a substring artifact: vent inside "events", duct inside "product", plus unrelated dialogue about a ceiling. Not one referred to a ventilation system.

The original negative holds, and the correction runs against my own method rather than against the show. A loose pattern manufactures false positives exactly the way a strict pattern manufactures false negatives.

The two-probe rule

Added to the method, retroactive to every entry

Every negative result needs two probes to enter the ledger. A strict pattern establishes the claim as worded. A loose pattern tests whether the strict one was doing the work. A loose hit gets inspected first and only then counts, because substring matching finds words inside other words.

Silence only becomes a finding after both probes return nothing. Pass four broke that rule and published a conclusion drawn from a single broken pattern, which is the error this method exists to prevent.

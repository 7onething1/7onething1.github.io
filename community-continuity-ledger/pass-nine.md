# Community continuity ledger, pass nine

Five targeted claims, all tested against the 28-episode corpus. Three confirmed, one confirmed and upgraded, one refused in its particulars while its underlying structure survives. Two of the five sit in episodes that only became readable through optical recognition earlier in this session.

## The Troy and Abed rivalry is real, and Britta is not in it

The ledger treats "Early 21st Century Romanticism" as a cornerstone of the Troy, Britta and Abed chain. The episode does build a rivalry between Troy and Abed over a third person, and that person is not Britta.

Troy opens the episode at 00:00:33 by asking Jeff what to do when he and his best friend want to ask the same girl to a dance. The girl is introduced at 00:05:35, where Abed presents Troy to her as his associate. Britta spends the episode in a separate plot entirely, running from 00:07:10 to 00:07:30, about a friend who believes Britta is a lesbian and a Britta who believes the same of her.

Refusing the claim as worded leaves something better standing. The episode installs the exact structure the ledger cares about, two best friends wanting the same third person, a full season ahead of Britta occupying that position. That makes it a future seed rather than a cornerstone.

Status: refused as stated, reclassified as future seed, S02E15 cues 11 through 13, 122, and 164 through 172.

## Annie did considerably more than contemplate leaving

The ledger records Annie privately contemplating a transfer to City College while City College attacks Greendale from outside, and reads the pairing as personal disloyalty beside an institutional attack. The transcript is harder than that.

The confession runs from 00:11:59 to 00:12:13. It opens with a flat admission of having set the towing up. It continues into a call placed to City College about transferring, a promise from their dean to admit her in exchange for helping sabotage Greendale's launch, and her assigned part in making sure nobody was around so the simulator could be stolen.

That is an active conspiracy against her own school rather than a private thought about leaving. The ledger's Annie manipulation entry gains its strongest single citation, and the pairing the ledger noticed becomes sharper: the outside attack and the inside betrayal are the same plan.

Speaker is inferred from the confession structure rather than from a tag. Status: confirmed and upgraded, S02E04 cues 251 through 256.

## Duncan takes the anthropology chair in a different episode than the ledger says

The ledger places Duncan's return to the anthropology teaching situation in "Anthropology 101." His name returns zero hits in that episode across the whole transcript.

He introduces himself to the anthropology class at 00:04:35 in "The Psychology of Letting Go," giving his full name and title. Three further lines in that episode carry his speaker tag.

The correction has a useful side effect. It puts Duncan's arrival in the same episode as Pierce's mother, the energon pod, and the religion-versus-cult classroom question, which pass five had already tied together. Duncan's mortality speech at 00:10:33 belongs to that cluster rather than to a separate teaching thread.

Status: episode assignment corrected, S02E03 cue 110.

## The Duncan Principle predates all of it

While resolving the above, an earlier Duncan thread surfaced that the ledger does not carry. "Social Psychology" builds a named theory around him across five cues, from 00:01:24 to 00:11:36, in which the theory is introduced, explained, warped and finally declared destroyed inside one episode.

A character getting a named principle in the first season, then losing it by the end of the same episode, is the same install-and-collapse shape the show later uses for Magnitude. Status: confirmed, new to the ledger, S01E04 cues 25 through 228.

## Both halves of the Messianic Myths mirror are confirmed

The ledger pairs the Abed and Shirley religious film conflict with Pierce joining an elderly rebellion, on the grounds that both involve people building identities that threaten their group attachments.

Shirley's request arrives at 00:02:28 and Abed refuses it at 00:02:33 by drawing a line between storytelling and preaching. His counter-proposal at 00:04:56 through 00:05:12 is recursive, a film about a filmmaker exploring the life of Jesus who then realises what he is.

The Pierce half runs from 00:10:55 to 00:11:22, where a pack of old people in the cafeteria is reported, Leonard is named as the reason Pierce has become dangerous, and somebody states plainly that Leonard is not part of the study group. The last line is the mirror's hinge, since it makes group membership the thing at stake in both plots.

Status: confirmed, both halves, S02E05.

## Abed says television raised him

One line from that episode belongs to the master Abed thread rather than to the mirror. At 00:04:29 he attributes a childhood belief about Jesus to having been raised by television and movies.

The ledger has tracked Abed moving from commenting on television toward being authored by it. Here he states the causal claim about himself directly, in an episode about religion, one season after Britta first asked whether he could tell the two apart.

Status: confirmed, added to the Abed television chain, S02E05 cue 94.

## The homage swap is confirmed, and the setup runs the whole episode

The ledger records Abed replacing Jeff's expected movie homage with another genre. The transcript shows the expectation built deliberately and at length, and only then does the substitution land.

The expected film is named at 00:01:42 and returns at 00:03:48, 00:05:10, 00:10:32, 00:10:40 and 00:13:09. The substituted film is not named until 00:15:26, and the immediate reply at 00:15:35 is a character asking what it even is.

The episode also opens, at 00:00:11, with a description of Abed in the past tense as somebody who quoted movies and pretended life was a television show. That framing makes the swap a statement about Abed rather than a gag about film taste.

One detail connects this to the Jeff money file. The man-of-means line pass five recorded sits at 00:12:30, and the line immediately after places Jeff at an expensive dinner. The characters speculating about Jeff's spending are doing so during the dinner that turns out to be the substituted homage.

Status: confirmed with fuller structure, S02E19.

The Ship · guitar transcription audit

How many guitarists were actually in the room?

Ten tabs pass every playability check. Three of them describe a band that does not match the recording.

2026-08-13 · 10 songs · audio evidence + notation evidence + physical gate

The question comes first, before any fingering work. A refret pass run on mis-owned notes produces a playable-looking tab of a performance nobody gave. So the count of guitarists is settled from the recording, and only then is the notation graded.

The staff count already sitting in a tab is an assertion, never evidence. Eight of these ten carry a Lead staff and a Rhythm staff, and nothing in any of those files says a second guitarist ever played.

2  How the count was decided

Every measurement runs on the untouched stereo -guitar.mp3 stem.

The -guitar_lead and -guitar_rhythm stems cannot answer this question and were not used. They came from split_guitar_score_wiener.py, a score-informed splitter: it was handed the existing transcription and asked to divide the audio to match it. Feed it a one-guitar song with a two-staff score and it returns two stems. The answer would be the assumption.

The surviving test is where each attack sits in the stereo field. For every transient, the left/right energy balance over the following 80 ms. One guitarist puts every attack at one position, however wide the reverb around it. Two guitarists at different positions split the attacks into two clusters, and the bimodality coefficient of that distribution separates the cases.

The limit, stated plainly: this counts pan positions, not people. One guitarist who overdubs a second part to the same position reads as ONE. That is not a flaw to hide, it is what resolves song 08 below.

3  Three metrics that failed their own controls

The obvious measurements were tried first, and synthetic controls killed them. They are still reported in the data for context, and none of them carries a verdict.

Read the bold cells. A single guitar through a Haas delay and reverb produced the lowest correlation and the most side energy of anything measured, real tracks included. A single guitar through a ping-pong delay produced 59% onset independence, the highest figure in the set. And two genuinely different guitarists at a realistic ±15% produced the most one-guitar-looking numbers on all three.

Correlation, side energy and onset independence do not measure how many people played. They measure how wide the mix is. Only the bimodality coefficient held: positives at 0.794 regardless of pan width, negatives from 0.260 to 0.390.

A fourth metric was discarded earlier for the same reason. An optimal two-means split of the pan distribution scores any distribution, so the known one-source bass stem read 2.31, higher than every guitar in the album. A statistic its own control cannot distinguish is not evidence.

The drums stem was meant to calibrate the ceiling and it does not. Drums are many sources yet score below the single-source floor, because rapid hat-and-kick interleaving has no phrase-length stereo structure. A known positive that fails the positive test validates nothing, so the positives were built synthetically instead.

Decision bands, measured rather than assumed: below 0.39 is inside the synthetic one-guitar band and returns ONE; above 0.512 clears the highest real single source and returns TWO; 0.60 and up is firm. Between 0.39 and 0.512 is inconclusive and goes to the notation test rather than being guessed.

5  What is actually wrong

11 Ambulance — one performance, two staves

Three signals agree, and this is the clearest defect in the album.

  • Audio: BC 0.337, inside the synthetic one-guitar band.
  • Notation: 60.0% of the Lead staff's 1,138 attacks are the same pitch at the same instant as a Rhythm attack.
  • Gate: Lead hand-skip 20%, the worst figure in the set, which is the documented consequence of writing one detected note into both staves.

The physical gate passes it, because every check it runs asks about the file on its own. None of them asks whether a second guitarist existed.

07 MOP — a guitar part that is not in the tab

The audio reads TWO (BC 0.551) and the tab carries a single Rhythm staff. Corroborating detail from the existing work: MOP's leftover wide spans reduce to one recurring shape, s0f7 s1f2, a B2 unison double-stop with no narrower voicing anywhere on the neck. Two guitars playing one note is exactly what that shape looks like once both are collapsed onto one staff.

08 JGBFTL — resolved, and the tab is right

Audio says ONE (BC 0.330) while the tab carries two staves, which looks like a contradiction until the notation is measured: the staves share 0.7% of their attacks. They are two genuinely independent parts. One guitarist overdubbed to the same stereo position, which the audio test reads as one source by construction. Two real parts, one player, correct notation.

03 Gene and 04 Six Feet Under — worth a look, not broken

Both confirmed as two guitarists, and both carry heavy cross-staff doubling (43.9% and 39.4%). Per the standing rule a unison across two staves is two guitarists doubling and must survive. The figures are high enough to be worth a per-note depan pass to confirm the doubling is real rather than transcribed twice, and nothing here licenses deleting a note.

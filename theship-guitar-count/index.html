The Ship · guitar transcription audit & repair

How many guitarists were actually in the room?

Ten tabs passed every playability check. One of them described a guitarist who was never there, and that one is now repaired.

2026-08-13 · 10 songs · audio evidence, notation evidence, alignment, physical gate

The question comes first, before any fingering work. A refret pass run on mis-owned notes produces a playable-looking tab of a performance nobody gave. So the count of guitarists is settled from the recording, and only then is the notation graded and repaired.

The staff count sitting in a tab is an assertion, never evidence. Eight of these ten carry a Lead staff and a Rhythm staff, and nothing in any of those files says a second guitarist ever played.

2  The repair: 11 Ambulance

One performance had been written onto both staves

Four measurements agreed before anything was changed:

  • Audio BC 0.337, inside the synthetic one-guitar band.
  • Staff pan separation 0.009, the album's lowest by 35x.
  • 60.0% of Lead's 1,138 attacks were the same pitch at the same instant as a Rhythm attack.
  • Lead hand-skip 20%, the worst in the album, which is the documented consequence of one detected note being written into both staves.

665 duplicated Lead attacks were removed. A note came out only where a struck Rhythm note of the same sounding pitch at the same onset remained, so the pitch keeps sounding from the other staff and none can leave the music. Ringing notes and tie-chain notes were never touched, which is why 665 rather than the full 683.

221 beats were cloned before editing. A GPIF Beat is a shared definition referenced from many places, and editing one in place is a broadcast edit that once turned 17 harmonics into 383 while every bar-sum check passed.

The ledger will not promote it, and that is a gap in the tooling

The checkpoint comparison refused promotion on one line: notes fall 3358 -> 2693. The skill's own Tier 1 states that a depan is supposed to reduce the count, and it printed exactly that: instance loss is reported above and is intended for a depan or dedupe pass.

No flag bridges this. --allow-setup-change, --accept-tier-tradeoff and --license-skip-regression each state they never excuse a note loss. So the ledger structurally cannot promote a depan repair. 3  How the count was decided

Every measurement runs on the untouched stereo -guitar.mp3 stem.

The -guitar_lead and -guitar_rhythm stems cannot answer this and were not used. They came from split_guitar_score_wiener.py, a score-informed splitter: it was handed the existing transcription and asked to divide the audio to match it. Feed it a one-guitar song with a two-staff score and it returns two stems. The answer would be the assumption.

The surviving song-level test is where each attack sits in the stereo field, scored by bimodality coefficient. One guitarist puts every attack at one position however wide the reverb around it. Two guitarists at different positions split the attacks into two clusters.

The limit, stated plainly: this counts pan positions, not people. One guitarist overdubbing a second part to the same position reads as ONE. That is not a flaw to hide, it is what makes song 08's two staves defensible at 0.7% duplication.

Read the bold cells. A single guitar through a Haas delay and reverb produced the lowest correlation and the most side energy of anything measured, real tracks included. A single guitar through a ping-pong delay produced 59% onset independence, the highest figure in the set. Correlation, side energy and onset independence measure how wide the mix is, not how many people played. Only the bimodality coefficient held, at 0.794 for positives regardless of pan width against 0.260 to 0.390 for negatives.

Two more were discarded the same way. An optimal two-means split of the pan distribution scores any distribution, so the known one-source bass stem read 2.31, higher than every guitar on the album. And a per-staff bimodality test, meant to reveal a second guitarist merged into a single staff, was refuted by its control: 03 Gene's two staves scored 0.59 and 0.64, and each of those is certainly one player. That test is the reason song 07 is marked unresolved rather than confirmed.

5  Alignment, and the model bug that invented two defects

Per-note audio measurement needs the tab's beat 0 on the stem's sample 0. The first depan attempt on 11 Ambulance refused to report, because its written attacks carried less energy at their own pitches than nonsense onsets did, 0.55x. Offset and tempo are therefore solved per song against the stem's onset envelope, scored against the same tab with its rhythm shuffled, since a dense impulse train scores well against any envelope simply by covering it.

The first version of this reported two defects that did not exist

It read one tempo per song, from the first Tempo automation. Four Ship songs carry tempo maps, and 05 Sleep Vs Death goes 110 bpm to 90 at bar 35, a 22% change a constant-tempo model cannot express. So the search returned a compromise scale and the song was reported as having a tempo error, first 8.8% and then 3.0%. Both were wrong, and both are retracted. 02 Flake, carrying nine tempo entries, was reported as not aligning at all.

With the full tempo map, both align cleanly and their written tempos are exact. The alignment control on 05 Sleep went from 2.38x to 24.25x, the strongest in the album. The defect was in the model, not in the tabs.

A repair that was refused, and why the refusal is the result

Brandon, 2026-08-15: "like flake of the year has chords where muted strings should be." A first detector reported 3.4x more suspicious chord attacks than its single-note floor. That was withdrawn: it ran at a placeholder offset of 0.0 when 02 Flake's real alignment is +2.600s, so it measured the wrong part of the recording.

The replacement measures sustain rather than the transient, since every attack is broadband at its onset whether muted or not, and it is gated on labels it did not produce: the tabs' own written Muted notes against long-duration unmuted attacks. Two bugs surfaced while building that control, both found by it returning zero:

  1. GPIF Notes are shared definitions in one document-level collection, not children of a Track, so trk.iter("Note") found nothing and every muted note in the album was invisible. 02 Flake's five definitions expand to 188 real muted attacks.
  2. Matching on (string, fret) was wrong even after the ids resolved, since a position match claims every other note sharing that position.

With 473 pooled muted attacks against 6,250 pitched ones, the control FAILED, and the mean gap is why it had to be measured properly rather than eyeballed:

An AUC of 0.5 is a coin flip. No .gp was edited, and 02 Flake stands as it is. The hypothesis remains credible and unproven.

The deeper obstacle, stated plainly: those Muted marks were written by the same Songsterr AI transcription being audited. Calibrating a muted-strum detector against them is circular. Breaking this needs muted attacks labelled independently of the transcriber.

The pooled failure was the LABELS, decided on synthetic truth

A failed control has two possible causes demanding opposite responses: the classifier cannot separate the classes, or the classifier is fine and the labels are wrong. Real audio cannot tell those apart, because both produce the same failed control. Synthetic audio can, because its labels are true by construction and owe nothing to the transcriber or to the detector being tested.

Sixty pitched and sixty muted attacks were rendered with identical attack transients, differing only in the one physical property at issue, harmonic decay of 600 ms against 30 ms, and placed on the real 02 Flake stem at working level.

Decay ratio separates perfectly on that synthetic construction and shows almost no relationship to the tabs' own Muted marks. The defensible claim is exactly that and no more: the feature works on this synthetic control while those marks track it barely at all. Real muted against real rung guitar remains unvalidated. The marks are not usable ground truth here, which is what a mark written by the transcription under audit should be expected to be. Stated against my own interest: an AUC of exactly 1.000 means the synthetic case is easier than the record, so this licenses the method and does NOT license carrying its threshold onto real audio.

The threshold transfer was tried and it failed, which closes the question

One route remained that needed no human labels: the synthetic pitched attacks were rendered on the Flake bed, so their own decay distribution supplies a threshold with a controlled false-positive rate. Set at the 1st percentile of synthetic pitched attacks, decay 1.525, it catches 100% of synthetic muted attacks at a 1% false-positive rate. On paper that is a finished classifier.

Applied to 02 Flake it flags 346 of 925 chord attacks, 37.4%, against 9.2 expected from the false-positive rate. That result is not credible, and the reason is the one already stated: real rung chords in a dense mix decay faster than clean synthetic ones, because they are masked, overlapped and re-struck. The threshold measures the gap between my synthesiser and a record, not the gap between a rung chord and a dead one.

It also contradicts the label-free bimodality result on the same audio, where Flake is the album's least bimodal song. When two measurements on the same signal disagree this hard, the transferred threshold is the one without standing.

This closes the no-labels route rather than leaving it untried. Every label-free path available has now been run: the tabs' own marks are circular and fail at AUC 0.522, the synthetic proves the method sound at AUC 1.000 but cannot carry a threshold, and bimodality bounds the population without being able to name its members. Turning candidates into edits needs muted attacks labelled by ear. No .gp has been edited at any point.

Candidate ranking, which is what was actually missing

Brandon, 2026-08-15: "Claude has exhausted the three classification routes it CHOSE to implement. That does not establish that every useful label-free analysis has been exhausted... rank those 66 low-decay attacks by multiple independent acoustic features instead of treating decay ratio as the entire detector." Correct, and the earlier "nothing executable remains" was wrong.

All 925 Flake chord attacks are now ranked on nine features chosen to fail differently from each other: decay ratio, sustain support, harmonic-to-noise ratio, spectral flatness, high-frequency transient proportion, post-transient periodicity, sustain-envelope slope, chroma stability, and how far an attack sits below its immediate neighbours. Each is z-scored against this song's own attacks, so a bright mix cannot manufacture a ranking.

WITHDRAWN, see below. The top 50 clustered at a median 0.54s against 2.49s for 50 drawn at random, z = −5.6, concentrated in 100 to 120s and 20 to 40s. That whole-song null was the wrong control: 21 of those 50 attacks sit at or below the song's own loudness floor, and the 100 to 120s passage is largely silence. Brandon caught it from one clip.

This is a candidate ordering, not a classification. Nine features all reading the same recording can agree and all be wrong together. What it changes is the listening task: instead of twelve blind timestamps across a 204-second song, the work starts at rank 1 inside two identified passages. Artifact: candidates_02.json.

The listening pass is hosted at theship-flake-labels: 24 clips, twelve from the top of the ranking and twelve from the bottom, in time order so the ranking does not prime the ear. The listener's calls are the ground truth here, and the seven-feature ranking is only what decides which 24 attacks are worth hearing.

The section-level defect map for 08 JGBFTL was refuted by its own controls

The whole-song verdict, "aligns at no tempo", does not say whether the fault is global tempo, section ordering, omitted or extra material, or wrong notes, and those need different repairs. So each 16-bar window was aligned independently and scored against its own shuffled rhythm.

Run on 08 it reported CONTENT, every window failing. Then the controls killed it. Known-good songs produce the same pattern: 04 Six Feet Under at z = +2.4 and +0.8, 05 Sleep Vs Death at +0.8 and −0.3, 01 Seedy Shade at −0.0, all of which align globally and pass every gate. 03 Gene looked clean and was the outlier, not the rule.

Two earlier versions were also caught: a free 0.55 to 2.00 scale grid pinned itself at the 1.98 edge on short windows, and a free offset search let every window roam the whole recording and report backwards jumps in a song that has none. Both were narrowed and the control still failed.

So 08 JGBFTL's defect remains unclassified, and the reason is now specific rather than a shrug: per-window positioning needs a global anchor, and JGBFTL by definition has none. The refusal to report CONTENT on a diagnostic that also condemns three healthy songs is the result here.

A noise axis does not average out, it drags everything to the middle and lets one loud useless feature outvote a good one. Sustain support failing here matches its behaviour on the real pooled control, where it managed only AUC 0.617 and then 0.687. The same axis is weak in both places, which is the consistent reading.

What this test can and cannot do: it can condemn a feature, since one that cannot separate a 600 ms ringing chord from a 30 ms damped one on labelled audio will do no better on a record. It cannot certify a survivor, because the synthetic case is easier than the record. A high score here keeps a feature in a CANDIDATE ranking and never licenses a classification.

The pruned ranking put back through its own control

Rebuilt from the seven survivors and re-run through the clustering test: z = −5.5 against the nine-feature ranking's −5.6. The two agree on 76% of their top 50 and 75% of their top 20, and both concentrate on the same passages.

The reading is not that pruning improved things. Both rankings cluster about equally, so the dropped axis was carrying some real signal on this record despite failing the synthetic case. What the test establishes is stability: the localization does not depend on which of these features are included, which is what a ranking driven by one lucky axis would not survive.

The ranker was detecting silence, and Brandon heard it in one clip

Brandon, 2026-08-15: "94.03s is silent". That is rank 6 on the list I handed him. Measured against the song's own 10th-percentile loudness floor, 21 of the top 50 sit at or below it, against 5 expected. The 100 to 120s "cluster" is a quiet passage: 112.06s reads rms 0.00002.

08 JGBFTL, localized at last by a diagnostic that needs no alignment

The section aligner failed because per-window positioning needs a global anchor and this song has none. Pitch-class content does not involve time at all, so it can be asked of a song that aligns nowhere. Each 8-bar section's duration-weighted histogram is scored against its own stem's chroma, against every other Ship stem, and against its own eleven transpositions.

This time the controls discriminated. Known-good songs pass both tests on 71% of sections on average; 08 passes 29%. That is the opposite of the section aligner, which condemned healthy songs identically.

The song splits in half. Bars 41 to 54 score 0.89, level with the album's healthy tabs. Bars 1 to 24 sit at 0.38 to 0.48 and lose to their own transposed selves, which is the strongest form of this failure: the written pitch material matches a shifted version of the recording better than the recording.

Stated against the finding, because one control is weak: the beats-other-song test fails 12 of 12 sections on 07 MOP and 8 of 8 on 02 Flake, both of which align cleanly. Ship songs share harmony, so losing to a labelmate proves little. The transposition test is the discriminating one, and even there 05 Sleep Vs Death shows a comparable three-section block while being healthy. So this does not by itself prove 08's defect class.

What is specific to 08 is the conjunction: it is the only song that fails global alignment at every tempo, returns the album's weakest whole-song chroma at 0.703, AND carries three consecutive sections losing to their own transposition. No other Ship song has more than one of those.

The practical result for the Songsterr pass: re-transcription is now scoped to bars 1 to 40 of 54. Bars 41 to 54 measure as sound as the healthy tabs and are worth keeping rather than discarding.

04 Six Feet Under bar 58, and a hypothesis the audio refuted

Brandon: "six feet under bar 58 sounds like a weird chord not quite right." The bar repeats one five-note shape six times. G4 and E4 are supported on every attack; the inner voices flip. Unsupported notes by bar: 57 at 10%, 58 at 30%, 59 at 20%, 60 at 37.5%, 61 at 0%, 62 at 8.3%. The rough patch is real and spans 58 through 60, and bar 58 is not uniquely bad inside it.

The obvious reading, that the written C3 G3 B3 E4 G4 is a Cmaj7 where the record plays a plain C, is refuted by its own head-to-head. Across all nine attacks carrying that shape, written B3 averages 8.34 against C4's 8.26, and C4 wins only 44% of them. The two sit too close to separate, which is what made the semitone probe flip B3 within a single bar. Those per-note flags are detector noise.

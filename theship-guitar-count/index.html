The Ship · guitar transcription audit & repair

How many guitarists were actually in the room?

Ten tabs passed every playability check. One of them described a guitarist who was never there, and that one is now repaired.

2026-08-13 · 10 songs · audio evidence, notation evidence, alignment, physical gate

The question comes first, before any fingering work. A refret pass run on mis-owned notes produces a playable-looking tab of a performance nobody gave. So the count of guitarists is settled from the recording, and only then is the notation graded and repaired.

The staff count sitting in a tab is an assertion, never evidence. Eight of these ten carry a Lead staff and a Rhythm staff, and nothing in any of those files says a second guitarist ever played.

1  The answer, per song

Three independent lines. Audio BC asks how many pan positions are playing across the whole stem. Staff separation measures each written note against the stereo stem and asks whether the two staves occupy different positions. Staff dup asks how much of the smaller staff is the larger staff at the same instant and pitch.

2  The repair: 11 Ambulance

One performance had been written onto both staves

Four measurements agreed before anything was changed:

  • Audio BC 0.337, inside the synthetic one-guitar band.
  • Staff pan separation 0.009, the album's lowest by 35x.
  • 60.0% of Lead's 1,138 attacks were the same pitch at the same instant as a Rhythm attack.
  • Lead hand-skip 20%, the worst in the album, which is the documented consequence of one detected note being written into both staves.

665 duplicated Lead attacks were removed. A note came out only where a struck Rhythm note of the same sounding pitch at the same onset remained, so the pitch keeps sounding from the other staff and none can leave the music. Ringing notes and tie-chain notes were never touched, which is why 665 rather than the full 683.

221 beats were cloned before editing. A GPIF Beat is a shared definition referenced from many places, and editing one in place is a broadcast edit that once turned 17 harmonics into 383 while every bar-sum check passed.

The four pitches leaving the Lead staff beside a clean BOTH GUITARS row is the signature of a restaffing rather than a loss: they are still in the music, on the other staff. Lead hand-skip went from 20% to 0% and no note changed string or fret.

The ledger will not promote it, and that is a gap in the tooling

The checkpoint comparison refused promotion on one line: notes fall 3358 -> 2693. The skill's own Tier 1 states that a depan is supposed to reduce the count, and it printed exactly that: instance loss is reported above and is intended for a depan or dedupe pass.

No flag bridges this. --allow-setup-change, --accept-tier-tradeoff and --license-skip-regression each state they never excuse a note loss. So the ledger structurally cannot promote a depan repair. The file is left honestly named and unpromoted rather than forced through or renamed to imply a status it does not hold.

3  How the count was decided

Every measurement runs on the untouched stereo -guitar.mp3 stem.

The -guitar_lead and -guitar_rhythm stems cannot answer this and were not used. They came from split_guitar_score_wiener.py, a score-informed splitter: it was handed the existing transcription and asked to divide the audio to match it. Feed it a one-guitar song with a two-staff score and it returns two stems. The answer would be the assumption.

The surviving song-level test is where each attack sits in the stereo field, scored by bimodality coefficient. One guitarist puts every attack at one position however wide the reverb around it. Two guitarists at different positions split the attacks into two clusters.

The limit, stated plainly: this counts pan positions, not people. One guitarist overdubbing a second part to the same position reads as ONE. That is not a flaw to hide, it is what makes song 08's two staves defensible at 0.7% duplication.

Read the bold cells. A single guitar through a Haas delay and reverb produced the lowest correlation and the most side energy of anything measured, real tracks included. A single guitar through a ping-pong delay produced 59% onset independence, the highest figure in the set. Two genuinely different guitarists at a realistic ±15% produced the most one-guitar-looking numbers on all three.

Correlation, side energy and onset independence measure how wide the mix is, not how many people played. Only the bimodality coefficient held, at 0.794 for positives regardless of pan width against 0.260 to 0.390 for negatives.

Two more were discarded the same way. An optimal two-means split of the pan distribution scores any distribution, so the known one-source bass stem read 2.31, higher than every guitar on the album. And a per-staff bimodality test, meant to reveal a second guitarist merged into a single staff, was refuted by its control: 03 Gene's two staves scored 0.59 and 0.64, and each of those is certainly one player. That test is the reason song 07 is marked unresolved rather than confirmed.

5  Alignment, and the model bug that invented two defects

Per-note audio measurement needs the tab's beat 0 on the stem's sample 0. The first depan attempt on 11 Ambulance refused to report, because its written attacks carried less energy at their own pitches than nonsense onsets did, 0.55x. Offset and tempo are therefore solved per song against the stem's onset envelope, scored against the same tab with its rhythm shuffled, since a dense impulse train scores well against any envelope simply by covering it.

The first version of this reported two defects that did not exist

It read one tempo per song, from the first Tempo automation. Four Ship songs carry tempo maps, and 05 Sleep Vs Death goes 110 bpm to 90 at bar 35, a 22% change a constant-tempo model cannot express. So the search returned a compromise scale and the song was reported as having a tempo error, first 8.8% and then 3.0%. Both were wrong, and both are retracted. 02 Flake, carrying nine tempo entries, was reported as not aligning at all.

With the full tempo map, both align cleanly and their written tempos are exact. The alignment control on 05 Sleep went from 2.38x to 24.25x, the strongest in the album. The defect was in the model, not in the tabs.

Nine of ten songs align and their tempos are right. 11 Ambulance's tempo was never wrong: it needed a 4.200 s offset and its scale came back 1.0000, with librosa independently estimating 156.6 against the written 156.0.

08 JGBFTL is the one genuinely mis-timed tab. It carries a single tempo of 88 bpm, the search's best fit wants 0.731 which implies about 120 bpm, and librosa estimates 103.4. Three numbers that do not converge, and the written rhythm cannot beat its own shuffled version at z = +4.0. Its note timing does not match this recording at any tempo, so no per-note measurement on it can mean anything.

6  What is still open

  1. 08 JGBFTL needs re-transcription. Its written rhythm does not match its recording at any tempo. The pipeline's hard gate is that the tab source is Songsterr and never hand-rolled pitch detection, so this is the one item that needs an upload from Brandon's account rather than a local pass.
  2. 07 MOP is unresolved on ownership. Its timing is exact and it aligns at z = +10.6, and the song-level audio says two guitars only weakly, 0.551 against a 0.512 floor. It carries a single staff, so there is no second staff to measure a separation against. It needs stronger evidence before anything is added.
  3. The ledger needs a depan licence so a pass that legitimately reduces the instance count can be promoted on its Tier 1 verdict.
  4. Settled and needing nothing: 01 Seedy Shade, 02 Flake, 03 Gene, 04 Six Feet Under, 05 Sleep Vs Death, 09 MHL, 10 Trapped in Wonderland. All align, all pass the gate, and every two-staff one among them is a confirmed real pair whose cross-staff doubling must survive.

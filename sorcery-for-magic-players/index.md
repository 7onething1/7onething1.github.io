# Sorcery for Magic Players: 50 Rules to Know

Five groups of ten, written for someone who already plays Magic: The Gathering and is
picking up Sorcery: Contested Realm.

## Where the rules come from

Every rule is quoted or paraphrased from the **official Sorcery: Contested Realm rulebook
released December 2025**, the revision rewritten to match the Gothic set. The PDF was
pulled from the publisher's own link on the How To Play page and extracted to 12,646 words
of plain text. Price and availability lines were checked live against retailer pages on
2026-09-10. Community claims name the transcript they came from.

## The five groups

1. **The Board and the Two Decks.** Sorcery is played on a five by four grid of twenty
   squares. You bring an Atlas of at least 30 sites and a Spellbook of at least 60 spells,
   and each turn you choose which of the two to draw from.
2. **Mana and Threshold.** Sites pay mana automatically at the start of your turn.
   Elemental threshold is a separate requirement that accumulates and is never spent.
3. **Turns and the Storyline.** There is no stack. The word never appears in the rulebook,
   and neither does "instant." Sorcery uses a Storyline that resolves front to back, which
   is the reverse of Magic's stack.
4. **Combat and Killing.** One power number serves as both attack and defense. Killing an
   Avatar takes two steps, death's door and then a death blow.
5. **Deckbuilding, Keywords and Culture.** Rarity sets copy limits at four, three, two and
   one. The Golden Rule hands rules disputes to the two players at the table.

## The three findings a Magic player will care about most

**No stack.** A text search of the full rulebook returns zero hits for "stack" and zero for
"instant." The replacement is the Storyline, and it resolves in the order events were
placed rather than last in, first out.

**Two decks kill mana screw.** Start phase step 4 reads "draw a card from either your
spellbook or atlas." Short on land you draw the Atlas, short on action you draw the
Spellbook.

**Zero life is not a loss.** Reducing an Avatar to zero places it at death's door. A
separate death blow is required to finish it, so your opponent gets a turn to answer.

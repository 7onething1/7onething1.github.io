# The album that revived MGMT

1. **[00:00:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tS8wHQ1fIxo&t=31s)** The three songs everyone knows were written as an attempt at the poppiest possible music, meant to mock pop, and the joke then overshadowed everything the band did afterwards.
2. **[00:01:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tS8wHQ1fIxo&t=81s)** Their own assessment before this record was that the previous two had gone over so badly they would never reach the first album's level again.
3. **[00:01:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tS8wHQ1fIxo&t=104s)** After the 2014 tour they stopped entirely, Ben Goldwasser moving to Los Angeles and Andrew VanWyngarden to Queens, and spent a year on everything except music.
4. **[00:02:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tS8wHQ1fIxo&t=122s)** They restarted long distance over email, sending each other chords and instrumentals, which dragged on with little to show for it.
5. **[00:02:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tS8wHQ1fIxo&t=140s)** Meeting at Tarbox Road they dropped the loose improvisational method of the last two records and returned to the debut's method, each arriving with fully formed ideas.
6. **[00:02:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tS8wHQ1fIxo&t=157s)** Dave Fridman came back on production, but the turn is credited to Patrick Wimberly, who talked them up when the ideas were good and steered them down the promising ones.
7. **[00:02:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tS8wHQ1fIxo&t=173s)** A two-day open jam session brought players in to lay down parts, and the track James grew out of an acid trip, with James Richardson playing French horn on it.
8. **[00:03:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tS8wHQ1fIxo&t=205s)** Ariel Pink taught them to write words on the spot and trust them, improvising a lyric in minutes out of a conversation from the hallway, which is where the confidence came back.
9. **[00:03:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tS8wHQ1fIxo&t=222s)** They came out with roughly 40 song ideas, and the first single in four years disproved their own claim that they could not write pop songs.
10. **[00:07:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tS8wHQ1fIxo&t=437s)** The last single marked their exit from Columbia and the first release on their own label, which closes the loop on a band that started out calling itself The Management as a jab at the industry's business side.

Why three separate ledgers

The useful version keeps three records apart: ETR's house rankings (blended projections, algorithms, and staff judgment), Ship Chasing panel opinions (where an episode often names a player without naming the host who backed the call), and statements Davis Mattek made under his own name. Grading a person for a room's take, or a room for one person's take, is how a ledger goes wrong.

Where a named speaker owns a call, it is tagged to that person. Where only the show owns it, it is tagged panel, attribution pending, and it waits for episode transcripts before it gets a final grade.

The grade plan

What happens next winter

A fair grade records six things per call: date, player, direction, draft price, named speaker, confidence. This ledger locks the first five now, before training-camp news reshapes the market.

Closing draft price is the hinge. An excellent July read can turn into an expensive September pick, so season grading compares production against acquisition cost and tournament structure, not raw total points. A cheap hit and a costly hit are not the same result.

After Week 17, each cell turns green or red on that basis, the panel calls get their named owners from transcripts, and this page redeploys with the verdicts in place next to the 2025 board.

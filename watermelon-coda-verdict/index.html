The question this page closes

The chart read produced 179 tom noteheads and handed 29 of them to the stem session for testing. That session returned verdicts on 20 of the 29 and marked the remaining 9 UNTESTABLE, because the alignment anchors stop at notation second 462.9 and the clock drifts by 165 to 773 milliseconds past that point.

Nine heads with no verdict is an open item, so this pass went after it from the stems. Four of the nine are now refuted, on a measurement that does not need a clock at all. The other five stay without a verdict, and the reason is now measured rather than assumed.

The headline, after the eye overturned it. The drum kit stops playing at 538.9 seconds, and that measurement stands. A first pass read that as refuting the four chart tom heads in GP bars 103 and 104, on a constant-tempo extrapolation that placed both bars after 539.7 s. Looking at the printed page withdrew that verdict. The chart's final system carries a fermata and a two-step drop to mp then pp, so the performance does not hold a constant tempo through the ending and those bar windows cannot be trusted. All four heads are printed as filled ovals. They return to no verdict.

Why bars 99, 100 and 101 still have no verdict

The tempting move is to count tom attacks inside each bar and call a bar supported when the count is above zero. That test was built and it fails its own control.

The in-region control is the set of Coda bars the chart marks empty: GP 93, 94, 96, 98 and 102. Those bars should return nothing. They return as much as the claimed bars do.

Counting all seven claimed bars together, including the two dead ones, the ratio drops to 0.82x, which is below chance. There are real tom attacks scattered through the Coda, 17 of them at the strict 5 percent threshold between 506 and 541 seconds. They land in bars the chart marks empty as often as in bars it marks with toms.

A position-matched test would be the stronger instrument, and it is unavailable here. Clock drift in this region runs 165 to 773 milliseconds. One sixteenth note at the verified tempo is 267.9 milliseconds. The uncertainty is wider than the grid spacing, so a head cannot be assigned to its slot.

This is a closed answer rather than a gap. The five heads in bars 99, 100 and 101 cannot be confirmed or refuted from these stems, and the two measurements above are the reason. Settling them needs anchors measured inside the Coda itself, which needs a lane in that region carrying events both dense and unambiguous. The ride thins out and the snare stops at 511 seconds, so no such lane exists after bar 98.

The detector controls, and the two bugs found on the way

The final detector thresholds on a fraction of the song's global tom-flux peak, which is the protocol the stem session used. Window counts are then comparable to each other and to their table.

Two detectors were built wrong ahead of this one, and both are recorded

  • Median absolute deviation computed on a clipped residual. Negative values were set to zero, then the scale was taken, so more than half the samples were exactly zero, the deviation collapsed to zero, and the threshold became infinite. Every window returned zero onsets, including windows known to carry toms.
  • A staircase baseline minted its own peaks. The running median was sampled every 16 frames and held flat between samples, which is a step every 0.093 seconds. Subtracting a staircase leaves a sawtooth whose peaks sit on the step boundaries. The resulting Coda onsets arrived in clusters spaced 85 to 95 milliseconds apart, matching the stride and not the drummer.
  • A threshold cut from the population it filters. Scaling the threshold to local noise let a near-silent window out-detect every loud one, at 4.20 onsets per second against 3.40 in a window where toms are plainly audible. Replacing it with a fraction of the global peak fixed the ordering and made the controls behave.

One correction to the record

The notation clock for this tab runs at quarter equals 56. The tempo automation stores 56 against a half-note unit, and reading that as a quarter-note value doubles the tempo. Bar 93 is a 4/4 bar spanning 447.857 to 452.143 seconds in notation time, which is 1.0714 seconds per quarter and confirms 56 directly.

At 56 the 105 bars occupy 8 minutes 26 seconds, which fits a track running 9 minutes 5 seconds. At 112 they would occupy 4 minutes 13 seconds, which does not. One sixteenth note is 267.9 milliseconds, and any earlier figure of 133.9 is the doubled reading.

All eight signed residuals are negative: -119.6, -97.5, -58.0, -76.6, -112.6, -49.9, -56.9, -98.7 ms. Median -87.1 ms with a standard deviation of 25.3 ms. What that settles. One sixteenth here is 267.9 ms. The raw median residual is 0.325 of a sixteenth, which looks like evidence for finer subdivisions. Removing the 87 ms bias leaves 25.3 ms of scatter, which is 0.094 of a sixteenth. The figure sits on the ordinary sixteenth grid. The competing sub-sixteenth tom counts of 41, 44, 49 and 51 are consistent with an uncorrected clock bias, and none of them supports minting thirty-seconds.

None of this authorizes deleting a cymbal. Global recall is 908 fresh attacks against 1778 written events, so the detector accounts for about half the notation across the whole song, and a per-bar shortfall reproduces that global rate. The skill permits removal only when a usable cymbal source affirmatively fails after alignment and leakage gates pass. The alignment gate is UNRESOLVED past GP bar 97, and 442 candidates are UNRESOLVED. Every unmatched written cymbal here stays UNRESOLVED and stays in the file.
The eye overrules the cymbal detector, and the chart says why. Looking at the printed page for these bars: chart bar 89 (GP 98) carries the Coda mark and (Gong), and chart bars 91 to 92 (GP 100 to 101) are marked (cymbal on dome) at p. Both systems show a dense continuous run of x noteheads on the top line, with many circled-x heads for the dome strokes. The cymbals are plainly printed and plainly played.

The detector ran with instrument="ride" calibration. A dome stroke at p produces a much lower-novelty transient than a bowed ride, so a ride-tuned novelty floor classifies it DECAY_ONLY. The zero-fresh-attack result in GP bars 99 to 103 is a detector mis-calibration, and it is not evidence of absence. Deferring to the eye, every written cymbal in the Coda stands.

Part oneFind — the real themes, legally, for cello

Every theme song and every cue in the show is under copyright, so the honest routes are the licensed ones. These four cover almost everything a cellist would want, and all of them pay the writers.

SourceWhat it has for celloWorth knowing
Musicnotes Officially licensed single-song PDFs; the string/violin-family filter is where cello editions live. Largest licensed catalogue for the show. Buy the cello edition rather than transposing a violin one — violin sheets sit an octave and a fifth off and read in treble clef.
Tomplay Gurenge for cello at Very Easy, Easy/Intermediate and Intermediate levels. Each edition ships with a backing track that follows your tempo, which is the fastest way to make one cello line sound like an arrangement instead of an exercise.
Sheet Music Plus / Hal Leonard Print collections of the anime's songs, mostly piano; cello parts appear in instrumental play-along series. Buy the piano/vocal collection if you want the harmony under the tune — you get the chords a solo cello has to imply on its own.
@ELISE / Piascore The Japanese licensed print-score shops. Deepest catalogue for insert songs and score cues that never got a Western release. Search the Japanese titles (祰蔬華, 炎, 死闘教儀) — the English titles often return nothing.

What each theme does to your left hand

Before you buy, this is what you are signing up for on an unaccompanied cello.

Gurenge
LiSA, comp. Kayoko Kusano		 Season 1 opening The obvious first one, and the one with the most editions. The chorus lives high — on cello it wants the A string in fourth position and up, or drop it an octave and lose the lift. Driving eighths, so it survives without accompaniment.

comp. Go Shiina		 Episode 19 insert song The best fit on this list for one cello. Slow, modal, mostly stepwise, sits in the D–A octave, and the long notes are the point — it's a bow-control piece more than a fingering piece.

Score cues
Yuki Kajiura & Go Shiina		 All seasons Kajiura already scores for strings, so the cue material is the most idiomatic thing in the show for you. Buy the soundtrack albums and transcribe by ear — a transcription you keep to yourself is yours; posting it is where licensing starts.
If you want to publish your own arrangement. A cover recording needs a mechanical licence (Easy Song, Songtrust); posting your own written-out arrangement as a PDF or on MuseScore needs the publisher's permission, which for these titles usually means Aniplex or the Japanese publisher. MuseScore's paid tier covers you only for the arrangements already licensed inside its own catalogue — not for one you upload.

Part twoMake — ten originals for unaccompanied cello

Written in the show's sound world rather than from its songs: Japanese yo and insen scales, open-string drones, Taishō-era waltz lilt, a phrygian seven-eight for the thing that hunts you, and one piece per breathing style. Every note is reachable in first through fourth position — no thumb position anywhere. Press Play to hear a bowed synth read it back, and MIDI to open it in MuseScore, Dorico or Logic for a printable part.

Practising a hard bar. The bars boxes on each piece set what Play actually plays, so you can drill bars 9–12 on their own instead of running the whole piece to reach them. Turn on Loop and they repeat; add Speed trainer and each repeat comes back 5% faster, up to half again the written tempo. Count-in gives you a bar of clicks before the first note and Click leaves the metronome running underneath — both count dotted quarters in 6/8 and quarters everywhere else. Escape stops everything.

Three of them are duets. Water Surface Slice, Flame Vow and Wisteria at Dusk carry a second cello part, engraved under the first. Both parts plays them together, the bar range and the metronome apply to both, and the MIDI file for a duet arrives as two named tracks so each player can print their own. Alone, Cello I is still the whole piece — the second part adds the harmony a solo line can only imply.

Reading the fingerings: the number under each note is the left-hand finger (0 = open string), the letter under that is the string. An italic number means the hand stretches for that note — a forward or backward extension, not a shift. Roman numerals above the staff mark a change of position (I = first, II = second, and so on); the small number at the left of each system is the bar number. Slurs are bowings: one bow for the whole slur.

Tuning: C2 G2 D3 A3, low to high. Everything here assumes standard tuning and a normally set-up cello — no scordatura, no extended technique beyond the harmonics mentioned in the notes.

Playback: the sound is a synthesised bow (two detuned saws, low-pass, delayed vibrato) built with the Web Audio API, and the metronome is a filtered blip from the same place. Nothing is streamed and no audio file is hosted, so the page works offline. The synth is a reference for pitch and rhythm, not a model of tone — your bow will do better.

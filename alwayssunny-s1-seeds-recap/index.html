Part A · The season, episode by episode

All seven Season 1 episodes, each worked in full from the transcript.

S01E01

The Gang Gets Racist

Series pilot. The template-setter for everything after it.

Recap Girl · play-by-play

Dee brings her acting-class friend Terrell, a Black party promoter, into Paddy's, and the guys hire him to pack the bar. Trying to prove they are not racist, Charlie and Mac go to Temple to make Black friends, and Charlie comes away with a woman named Janell's phone number. Terrell packs the place and turns it into a hot gay bar, and once the Gang sees the money, they keep it that way over Mac's objection, on a two-to-one vote that cuts Dee out as a non-owner. To flip it back, Mac and Dee scheme to get Dennis blackout drunk so he wakes up next to men and panics, and it works. The bar returns to a normal $114 night, and Janell turns out to be Terrell's sister.

Color Host · the read

The soil the whole series grows in: a real issue treated as a marketing gimmick, money settling every argument, and nobody learning a thing. Charlie's obsession with the coffee-shop waitress is already onscreen here, wallet photo and all, and the move of using a person as a prop to prove the Gang is not racist runs through both plots at once.

Tropes used
  • Cold-open pitch
  • Escalating scheme
  • Everyone-is-the-villain amorality
  • The rigged owner vote
Tropes subverted
  • A social issue used as a gimmick, no lesson learned
  • Gay bar embraced only for money
  • Nice-guy-wins torched when Janell punches Charlie in the eye
Mirrored subplot: the Gang using Terrell to pull a crowd and Charlie chasing Janell to prove he is not racist both use a Black person as a prop to launder the Gang's image, and both blow up.
Seeds this episode plants
  • The no-lesson, everyone-is-guilty template → the entire series grammar
  • Charlie's obsession with the Waitress, already onscreen in the pilot → runs the whole series
  • Money settles every Gang argument → a permanent reflex
"Is this about that waitress at the coffee shop, dude? Dude, you are obsessed with this chick."on Charlie, S01E01

Keywords: Terrell the promoter, gay bar, the owner vote, Janell, the blackout scheme, the Waitress

S01E02

Charlie Wants an Abortion

Episode 2. The Waitress crush develops and the paternity question opens.

Recap Girl · play-by-play

An old flame, Stacy, tells Charlie he has a ten-year-old son, Tommy, so Charlie chases a blood test to be sure. Dee lies to the Waitress that Charlie and Tommy are in Big Brothers Big Sisters, and Charlie plays it up to look good for her. Mac joins the pro-life side to woo an activist named Megan, and Dennis works the pro-choice side, then tries to switch for better odds and sets off an egg-throwing brawl. Tommy turns out not to be Charlie's, and Megan reveals her pregnancy was a test that Mac failed.

Color Host · the read

The paternity question opens with this blood test, the same "who is anyone's father" thread that later powers Frank, Bruce Mathis, and Pop-Pop. Charlie's Waitress crush, already shown in the pilot, develops here through the Big Brothers lie. Dennis picking the better-odds rally side is his first clear predatory calculus.

Tropes used
  • Cold-open pitch, T-shirt slogans
  • Escalating scheme
  • Everyone-is-the-villain amorality
  • Dennis's odds-based calculus
Tropes subverted
  • Abortion as a serious issue, played as a pickup op
  • Fatherhood redemption, Charlie uses the kid as a prop
  • Pro-life and pro-choice stereotypes both hollowed out
Mirrored subplot: Charlie's Big Brothers cover, built to impress the Waitress, and Mac and Dennis working the abortion rallies both use a serious public issue as cover for a hookup.
Seeds this episode plants
  • The Waitress crush develops through the Big Brothers lie → runs the whole series
  • The paternity and blood-test question → the Season 2 paternity engine
  • Dennis's predatory calculus → the D.E.N.N.I.S. arc
"Dude, you need to get a blood test, okay?"S01E02

Keywords: blood test, Tommy, Big Brothers, Megan, pro-life vs pro-choice, egg fight

S01E03

Underage Drinking: A National Concern

Episode 3. Dee's back brace and the Gang's arrested adolescence.

Recap Girl · play-by-play

The Gang realizes Paddy's is packed with high schoolers and, instead of stopping it, reframes it as a "safe haven" and waters down the drinks. Dee dates a popular teen, Trey, to relive the youth her scoliosis brace cost her, and Dennis mocks her for it. Trey's ex Tammy comes on to Dennis, then blackmails him into taking her to the prom by threatening to expose the bar. Dee and Charlie get asked too, so the Gang all go, and Trey and Tammy turn out to be using them only to make each other jealous. Mac and Charlie spend the episode failing to get high on glue that is clearly non-toxic.

Color Host · the read

Dee's scoliosis back brace becomes the show's go-to reference for humiliating her for years. The episode also fixes the Gang's permanent refusal to grow up, chasing the approval of teenagers instead.

Tropes used
  • Cold-open pitch as social responsibility
  • Escalating scheme, the "safe haven" rationale
  • Everyone-is-the-villain amorality
  • Dennis's "water down, jack up" calculus
Tropes subverted
  • Moral lesson on underage drinking, only exploited
  • Dee's popularity fantasy undercut by the back brace
  • Get-high subplot fails, the glue is non-toxic
Mirrored subplot: the Gang running an underage "safe haven" bar and Dee dating a high schooler both come from a Gang that never really left high school behind.
Seeds this episode plants
  • Dee's vanity and the scoliosis back brace → Dee's lifelong humiliation
  • The Gang's arrested adolescence → a permanent character trait
"You wore that scoliosis back brace until you were 20 years old."Dennis to Dee, S01E03

Keywords: underage drinking, safe haven, prom, Trey, Tammy, back brace, non-toxic glue

S01E04

Charlie Has Cancer

Episode 4. Fake-illness tactic and the Waitress crush both escalate.

Recap Girl · play-by-play

Charlie tells Dennis he might have cancer, and the Gang schemes to find a woman to sleep with him out of pity, settling on the Waitress. Dennis sleeps with the Waitress himself, then he and Mac offer her $250 to sleep with Charlie. Dee's price for helping is a free punch to Mac's face, payback for a Christmas party where Mac decked her. Mac, on the side, keeps dating a woman named Carmen even after learning she is transgender. In the end Charlie reveals he does not have cancer, learns they paid the Waitress, and says the sex never happened.

Color Host · the read

The show states it flat out here: Charlie has been in love with the Waitress forever. Faking a medical crisis for gain gets its first full run, a Gang tactic reused through welfare and every later sob-story scam. Dennis sleeping with the Waitress and then brokering a paid encounter for Charlie deepens the coercive streak seeded two episodes back.

Tropes used
  • Cold-open pitch, Charlie reveals the "cancer"
  • Escalating scheme, several angles at once
  • Everyone-is-the-villain amorality
  • Dennis's "cancer gold mine" calculus
Tropes subverted
  • Cancer as noble struggle, used as a tool
  • Sympathetic friends, all self-serving
  • Romantic payoff, the lie fails
Mirrored subplot: Charlie's fake cancer for the Waitress and Mac and Dennis using the "cancer" to get Charlie laid are two branches of one deception rather than separate plots, and both fall apart once the truth is out.
Seeds this episode plants
  • Faking a medical crisis for gain → welfare and every later sob-story scam
  • Charlie loves the Waitress "forever," stated outright here → the whole series
"We had to pay her $250 to have sex with you!" / "We didn't have sex."the Gang, then Charlie, S01E04

Keywords: fake cancer, pity scheme, the $250 offer, Dennis and the Waitress, Dee's free punch, Carmen

S01E05

Gun Fever

Episode 5. The incompetent-crime plot shape gets its template.

Recap Girl · play-by-play

The bar safe is stolen overnight with the locks untouched, and the Gang buys a gun. Playing Hardy Boys, Dennis pins the theft on Dee's boyfriend Colin, a serial robber who seduces bartenders to lift their keys. They bait a trap and wait in the dark, and when a masked figure raids the register, the Gang screams at Dennis to shoot. He grazes the intruder's head, and it turns out to be Charlie, who was skimming the register himself to cover the back rent he owes his landlord Hwang. While they are at the hospital, Colin robs Paddy's again.

Color Host · the read

The reveal splits the theft in two: Colin is the real serial robber, and Charlie is a desperate copycat skimming the register for rent, which is why Dennis ends up shooting his own friend. This is the first full run of the Gang's incompetent crime-solving and its casual gun violence, one friend shooting another and everyone shrugging it off, a template echoed later in the hostage and gas-crisis episodes.

Tropes used
  • Cold-open pitch, buy a gun
  • Escalating scheme, gun to trap to shooting
  • Everyone-is-the-villain amorality
  • Dennis's Hardy Boys sleuthing
Tropes subverted
  • Gun as protection, misused instantly
  • Competent investigation, laughably inept
  • They shoot their own friend, not the thief
Not a mirror: Gun Fever runs several threads (the gun panic, Dee unknowingly dating the real thief Colin, and Charlie's copycat theft) that converge rather than rhyme, so it is left out of the ranked mirrors below.
Seeds this episode plants
  • Incompetent crime-solving and casual gun violence → the hostage and gas-crisis episodes later
"But, Charlie, you were stealing from the bar."the Gang, on the reveal, S01E05

Keywords: robbery, gun fever, Colin the real robber, Hwang rent, Charlie the copycat

S01E06

The Gang Finds a Dead Guy

Episode 6. The single longest fuse in the season lights here.

Recap Girl · play-by-play

A regular named Lionel Keane dies inside Paddy's, and Mac and Dennis race to seduce his granddaughter Rebecca, working her with a fake eulogy and paid-off homeless "mourners." In a separate story, Dee drags Charlie to the nursing home to visit Pop-Pop, her and Dennis's bedridden grandfather, who wants to be buried in his uniform. Charlie finds the box, which holds a 1942 Nazi officer's uniform, and he and Mac try to sell it and then burn it. Dennis brags about sleeping with Rebecca, and a photo confirms Pop-Pop was a Nazi who looked exactly like Dennis.

Color Host · the read

The seed is Pop-Pop himself. Right here in Season 1 he is set up as Dennis and Dee's own grandfather, a Nazi with a uniform in the closet. The show pays that off directly years later when Pop-Pop returns as the ex-Nazi Heinrich Landgraf in a coma in "Pop-Pop: The Final Solution." The two grandfather stories, Lionel dead and Pop-Pop dying, run as separate exploitation schemes side by side.

Tropes used
  • Cold-open pitch, arguing about Tom Brady
  • Escalating scheme, cover-up to sale to burning
  • Everyone-is-the-villain amorality
  • Dennis's fake-eulogy calculus
Tropes subverted
  • Sympathy for the dead, zero empathy for Lionel
  • Family reconciliation, the Pop-Pop visit sours
  • Museum donation, insulted and turned away
Mirrored subplot: Mac and Dennis exploit Lionel Keane's death to get at his granddaughter Rebecca, and Charlie and Mac try to profit from Pop-Pop's Nazi uniform. Two grandfathers, two separate exploitations.
Seeds this episode plants
  • Pop-Pop set up as the twins' Nazi grandfather → pays off in S08E01, "Pop-Pop: The Final Solution"
"Pop-Pop is an asshole, Sweet Dee. And I'm placing my bets that that girl's grandfather is an asshole too."Dennis, S01E06

Keywords: Lionel Keane, Rebecca, nursing home, Pop-Pop, 1942 Nazi uniform, eulogy

S01E07

Charlie Got Molested

Season 1 finale. The McPoyles walk in.

Recap Girl · play-by-play

The Gang reads that their old gym teacher, Coach Murray, is charged with molesting two students, and decides Charlie must have been a victim too. Dennis and Dee run a clinical, condescending version of "help," and Mac spends the episode jealous he was not molested, even putting on short shorts to try to get Murray to make a pass at him. The McPoyle brothers turn out to be running a scam, faking the molestation to sue the school board off an idea a drunk Charlie once gave them, and they threaten to pin it on him. The intervention finally happens at Charlie's mother's with the family present, he is pushed to point on a doll, and he goes to the police, where he rats the McPoyles out and tells the Gang they ruined his life.

Color Host · the read

The McPoyles debut here as a whole recurring antagonist family, milk and all, paid off most directly in the Season 3 hostage episode and for years after. Charlie's implied trauma history also becomes part of the show's later psychological texture, a background note the writers keep returning to.

Tropes used
  • Cold-open pitch, jump to a molestation conclusion
  • Escalating scheme, intervention to police report
  • Everyone-is-the-villain amorality
  • Dennis and Dee's clinical pseudo-psychology
Tropes subverted
  • The intervention trope turned into a farce
  • The molestation panic is a lawsuit scam, with no real victims
  • A serious topic played for dark comedy
Mirrored subplot: Charlie's alleged molestation and Mac's jealousy over not being molested refract one event through Mac's need to feel special.
Seeds this episode plants
  • The McPoyle brothers → S02E09, the S03E04 hostage episode, and beyond
  • Charlie's implied trauma history → the show's later psychological texture
"The McPoyle twins Ryan and Liam."S01E07

Keywords: Coach Murray, intervention, McPoyle brothers, short shorts, the doll, the police

Part B · Every seed, ranked

The season's seeds sorted by payoff reach, how central each became to the whole series.

1

Pop-Pop, the twins' Nazi grandfather

seed · S01E06→pays off · S08E01

In "The Gang Finds a Dead Guy," Pop-Pop is set up as Dennis and Dee's own bedridden grandfather, a Nazi whose 1942 uniform Charlie digs up. He returns as ex-Nazi Heinrich Landgraf in a coma in "Pop-Pop: The Final Solution." The longest fuse in the season.

2

Charlie's forever-crush on the Waitress

seed · S01E01→runs the whole series

Charlie's fixation is onscreen in the pilot, wallet photo included, develops in "Charlie Wants an Abortion," and is stated outright in "Charlie Has Cancer." The biggest character thread of the season, driving Charlie through the Nightman musical and beyond.

3

Dennis's predatory calculus

seed · S01E02, E04→the D.E.N.N.I.S. arc

The rally-odds math and brokering a paid encounter with the Waitress for Charlie are the first draft of the coercive schemer Dennis becomes.

4

Faking a medical crisis for gain

seed · S01E04→welfare and later sob-story scams

Charlie's fake cancer is the first full run of a Gang tactic reused every time a later scam needs a fabricated condition, most cleanly when Dennis and Dee go on welfare.

5

The McPoyles arrive

seed · S01E07→S02E09, S03E04, and beyond

Ryan and Liam turn up running a fake-molestation lawsuit scam, and come back as the show's go-to antagonist family for years.

6

Nobody knows who anyone's father is

seed · S01E02→the S2 paternity engine

Charlie's blood test over a possible son opens the paternity obsession that later powers Frank, Bruce Mathis, and Pop-Pop.

7

Dee's vanity and the back brace

seed · S01E03→Dee's lifelong humiliation

Dennis dredges up Dee's scoliosis back brace as childhood history to humiliate her, and it stays the show's go-to way to knock her down.

8

The no-lesson, everyone-is-guilty template

seed · S01E01→the entire series grammar

The pilot teaches the format the whole show runs on. The soil the other seeds grow in, which is why it ranks last, it is the rule rather than one payoff.

Part C · Every mirrored subplot, ranked

Sorted by how tightly the A story and the B story rhyme. Gun Fever is left out because its threads converge into one plot.

1

S01E06 · two grandfathers, two exploitations

Mac and Dennis exploit Lionel Keane's death to get at his granddaughter Rebecca, and Charlie and Mac try to profit from Pop-Pop's Nazi uniform. Two grandfathers worked at once. Tightest rhyme in the season.

2

S01E01 · people as image props

Hire Terrell to pull a crowd, and chase Janell to prove Charlie is not racist. Both use a Black person to launder the Gang's image.

3

S01E04 · one lie, two branches

Charlie fakes cancer for the Waitress as Mac and Dennis use the "cancer" to get him laid. Two branches of one deception rather than two separate plots.

4

S01E02 · a cause as a hookup cover

Charlie's Big Brothers cover, built to impress the Waitress, and Mac and Dennis working the abortion rallies. A serious issue as pure cover for a hookup.

5

S01E03 · a Gang that never left high school

Paddy's run as an underage "safe haven," and Dee dating a high schooler. Both come from the Gang's arrested adolescence.

6

S01E07 · the same event, two egos

A chaotic intervention over Charlie's molestation, and Mac jealous he was not molested too. One event refracted through Mac's insecurity.

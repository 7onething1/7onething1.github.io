1. The definition census

GPIF deduplicates <Note> definitions, so a ghost flag lives on a definition and one element can produce many notated occurrences. Reading the definition table directly:

Every stored property matches id 431 apart from the AntiAccent element: InstrumentArticulation 15, Midi 51, Fret 51, String 5, ConcertPitch and TransposedPitch C-1, and beat Dynamic F on 100 percent of occurrences in both. Definition identity introduces no confounding property.

Ride plain on the beat, ride ghosted on the three offbeat sixteenths. Two events out of 1,724 break the rule. Only 2 ghosted ride notes sit on a beat, so a metric-position control is impossible in this tab.

5. Detector specification, stated completely

elementdefinition
audioone channel, arithmetic mean of the two stem channels, float in [-1, 1]
windownp.hanning(1024), applied unnormalised
hop256 samples at 44,100 Hz = 5.805 ms
magnitudeabs(rfft(frame * window)), no scaling by NFFT or window sum
fluxframe difference, negatives set to zero, then summed (L1) over band bins
frame 0previous frame taken as zeros, so flux[0] = 0
thresholda percentile of the strictly positive flux values
matchpresence: any single frame inside ±45 ms whose flux exceeds the threshold

The match rule is presence, and it is neither a local maximum nor a separately identified onset. Consecutive frames from one excursion can support a match, and one broad excursion can serve more than one window. The correct description of a positive is "matched the flux criterion".

6. Where 4.41 came from

17 processing variants were tested against the 3.51× gap: channel aggregation, integer against float scaling, three window normalisations, four magnitude scalings, L1 against L2 aggregation, percentile population, resampling and five frequency bands. One variant lands on the published number.

A 2000 to 5000 Hz band gives 4.389

In that band 4.41 sits at the 80.1st percentile of positive flux, agreeing with the written rule. In the documented 2000 to 12000 Hz band the same 4.41 sits at the 41.9th percentile. The published threshold is real and the band printed beside it is wrong.

9. Three scramble procedures

The original 20-draw table applies one bar displacement per draw. The later procedure samples a displacement per note. Both were run, plus a collision-free per-note variant.

procedureeligiblenull meansd95% intervalratio ghost
RIGID, one shift per draw69.8%55.1%5.7047.6-63.7%0.97×

PER-NOTE collision-free99.6%54.8%0.3154.2-55.4%0.98×

Band 2000 to 12000 Hz at its 80th percentile. The mean is stable across procedures and the spread is not, which is the dependence structure showing itself.

Two defects in the per-note procedure, and what the interval means

Collisions. Two notes can land in the same bar at the same position, sharing one timestamp and one verdict. 35.8 percent of scrambled notes collide, stacks up to 7 deep, leaving about 1,100 effective positions. A rigid translation cannot collide.

Meter eligibility. A position at or beyond 4.0 quarter notes exists only in 5/4 bars. 200 of 1,724 ride notes (11.6 percent) sit there and are ineligible for every 4/4 destination. Across all 106 legal shifts, 64.2 percent of (note, shift) pairs land on an existing position.

The 95 percent interval is the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of the null hit rate across randomisation draws. It describes variability under the randomisation only, and it carries no uncertainty from alignment, detector validity, extraction quality or recording identity.

10. Shared excursions

At the 2000 to 12000 Hz 80th percentile there are 2,559 supra-threshold excursions, median duration 12 ms, 90th percentile 52 ms, max 1,741 ms. One sixteenth is 271 ms, so an excursion longer than 181 ms can serve two consecutive ride windows. 143 excursions (5.6 percent) are that long.

The naive hit rate is blind to this. Distinct excursions per note runs 0.432 observed against 0.293 scrambled, a ratio of 1.47×, which reflects the scramble's clustering rather than an acoustic property of the notated positions.

An offset sweep from -300 to +300 ms, with the snare as a positive control.

The control rises more than fourfold and peaks where it should, so the method can detect a real signal. Plain ride positions show a real localised peak. Ghost ride positions rise again at plus and minus 271 ms, one sixteenth at the measured tempo, so their curve tracks the density of the sixteenth grid.

Miss taxonomy

One sixteenth measures 271.1 ms and half a sixteenth measures 135.6 ms. Beyond half a sixteenth the nearest attack belongs to a neighbouring grid slot.

Correction. 135.2 ms is half a sixteenth at this tempo rather than a whole one. A shift of that size lands a note maximally far from every grid position, so the original shifted null tested the worst case instead of a random one.

What this supports

Under the detector, alignment and null procedures documented above, positions occupied by occurrences of AntiAccent-marked definition 431 matched the flux criterion at 0.95× to 0.99× the rate of randomised eligible positions. No configuration reaches 1.45×.

The ghost flag is predicted by metric position at 99.9 percent, and its 1,316 occurrences inherit one AntiAccent element, so the file establishes neither independent per-stroke adjudication nor a dynamics contrast separable from the beat.

No deletion revision follows. Ben Dibden1's notation remains a transcriber's reading of the record, and nothing measured here overturns it.

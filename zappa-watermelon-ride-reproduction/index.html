5. Detector specification, stated completely

elementdefinition
audioone channel, arithmetic mean of the two stem channels, float in [-1, 1]
windownp.hanning(1024), applied unnormalised
hop256 samples at 44,100 Hz = 5.805 ms
magnitudeabs(rfft(frame * window)), no scaling by NFFT or window sum
fluxframe difference, negatives set to zero, then summed (L1) over band bins
frame 0previous frame taken as zeros, so flux[0] = 0
thresholda percentile of the strictly positive flux values
matchpresence: any single frame inside ±45 ms whose flux exceeds the threshold

The match rule is presence, and it is neither a local maximum nor a separately identified onset. Consecutive frames from one excursion can support a match, and one broad excursion can serve more than one window. The correct description of a positive is "matched the flux criterion".

6. The detector, reconciled

The 3.51× threshold gap was an operator difference. The band in the written spec was correct.

operatordefinition80th pct of positive flux, MP3, 2000-12000 Hz
B, the published onesum the band first, then take the positive first difference4.407
A, used in the first version of this pagedifference each bin, rectify, then sum14.303

Operator B puts 4.41 at percentile 80.01

Stereo MP3 read float32, unweighted channel mean to mono, no amplitude scaling, np.hanning(1024), 256-sample hop, frame-start timestamps, summed rFFT magnitude over bins 2000 to 12000 Hz inclusive, positive first difference, threshold at the 80th percentile of strictly positive flux. Operator A rectifies before summing, so nothing cancels and it runs about 3.2 times larger. Every result carrying threshold 15.487 came from operator A and is withdrawn.

A match means the maximum positive flux inside a ±45 ms window exceeds 4.41. It identifies no instrument and no discrete onset, so the wording throughout is "matched the flux criterion".

Meter eligibility, and what the interval means

A position at or beyond 4.0 quarter notes exists only in 5/4 bars. 200 of 1,724 ride notes (11.6 percent) sit there and are ineligible for every 4/4 destination. Eligibility checks meter and the anchored span together. Instrumentation, arrangement and local cymbal activity stay uncontrolled.

The 95 percent interval is the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of the null hit rate across randomisation draws. It describes variability under the randomisation only, and carries no uncertainty from alignment, detector validity, extraction quality or recording identity.

Reading it descriptively

At ghost positions the cymbal band shows a much lower transient peak, a far weaker attack relative to the local floor, a flatter decay profile and a darker spectrum with less 9 to 16 kHz content. Those four together are what residual decay from a preceding strike looks like, since cymbal highs shed fastest.

The design cannot separate decay from a genuinely soft stroke. Ghost positions are always the offbeats immediately after a plain strike, so decay sits there whether or not a soft stroke was also played. No classifier accuracy is claimed and no individual position is adjudicated.

11c. The decay-prediction test

The sharpest question this data can be asked: does a disputed ghost slot carry energy beyond the predicted tail of the preceding verified plain ride strike?

For each strong plain on-beat exemplar at time T, a log-linear decay is fitted to the smoothed envelope over T+30 ms to T+250 ms. That window is clean, because the next notated ride sits at T+271 ms and nothing else is notated inside it. The fit is extrapolated to the notated ghost slot at T+271 ms and to an off-grid control at T+135 ms where the score notates nothing at all.

188 usable exemplars from 224 candidates. Median fitted decay time constant 304 ms.

quantitymedianmeanshare above 1
excess at the notated ghost slot1.1531.48476.1%
excess at the off-grid control slot1.0931.14485.1%
paired difference, ghost minus control0.0590.34056.4%

Sign test on the paired difference: 106 of 188 positive, one-sided p = 0.047, two-sided p = 0.093. Both slots exceed the fitted tail, because real cymbal decay is not a single exponential. The notated slot exceeds it only marginally more than the off-grid control does.

What the flux comparison adds, and how it reconciles the 0.98×

Flux at the notated ghost slot runs 3.11× the flux at the off-grid control (median 9.878 against 3.179). Grid positions carry substantially more transient activity than off-grid positions.

That resolves an apparent tension. The scramble preserves position within the bar, so scrambled positions land on sixteenth-grid slots in other bars. The 0.98× therefore says a ghost slot is no different from other grid slots, and it never tested grid against off-grid. The grid itself is acoustically real. What the null cannot test is whether this particular slot carries a stroke, because every grid slot carries activity.

Spectral centroid is not usable here as stated: the control at 135 ms sits earlier in the decay than the slot at 271 ms, so it retains more high frequency for reasons of elapsed time rather than of a new strike. Plain strike 8,219 Hz, ghost slot 8,486 Hz, control slot 9,016 Hz.

Verdict of the decay test

In the envelope domain the notated ghost slot carries only marginally more excess over the predicted tail than an off-grid control does, at two-sided p = 0.093. That is weak and not significant. It does not demonstrate added strokes, and it does not exclude them.

11j. The ledger, and three corrections to my own earlier claims

Correction 1: the refined map is NOT independently validated

What I wrote: that the 123-anchor map "agrees with the 97-anchor version at a median difference of 0.0 ms", offered as evidence the refinement is sound.

Why that was wrong: the refinement searched a ±120 to 150 ms window centred on predictions from that same 97-anchor map. Agreement with the map that defined every search neighbourhood is circular. What it does show is stability under denser anchoring, a weaker claim.

Correction 2: the observed-rate gap was called unlocated

What I wrote: "the residual difference is not yet located". Why that was wrong: it is the match window. A 65 ms half-window gives 69.9 and 74.0 percent against the reported 71.3 and 75.3. The documented window is 45 ms.

Correction 3: "not reproducible at any threshold" was too broad

What I wrote: that the 1.45× is "not reproducible at any of 20 threshold and band settings". Why that was wrong: the detector reproduces exactly once the operator is right, and each reported figure is individually reachable. What does not reproduce is the two together.

The 14 unexplained anchors

137 reported minus 123 reached leaves 14 anchors unaccounted for. They are not noise. They could indicate another articulation category used as an anchor, a second stem, a looser tolerance rule, or manual intervention, and any of those would change how the historical map was built.

11g. The discrepancy is the NULL, and its cause is measured

Anchors: 123 reachable by local-window refinement, and DTW failed

DTW was tried first. Over a sparse impulse reference the cost is near zero wherever both signals are quiet, so the warp slides: unconstrained it disagreed with the validated map by a median of 7.7 s, and inside a ±4 s Sakoe-Chiba band still by 2.1 s. The map was right and the method was wrong.

Local-window onset refinement works. Keeping the validated map and resetting each notated snare inside a ±120 to 150 ms window reaches 123 anchors from 97. All 122 local slopes fall inside 0.918 to 1.103, median residual against the global line is -9 ms, and it agrees with the 97-anchor map at a median difference of 0.0 ms.

Off-grid destinations depress the null and inflate the ratio

In the right direction and close to the right size. The measured cause is section 11c: flux at an off-grid point runs 3.11× lower than at a notated grid slot, so a null landing between grid positions samples quieter audio.

11i. The 1.19× against 1.45× gap, reconciled

The reported pair is 71.3 percent observed and 49.0 percent null. The observed figure needs a 65 ms half-window, so the question is what null reaches 49.0 percent at that same window. At 65 ms the observed ghost rate is 69.9 percent, so 1.45× requires a null of 48.2 percent.

The published pair requires two different window widths at once

Even a uniform random null, the most extreme grid-breaking possible, reaches only 62.6 percent at a 65 ms window. Nothing gets near 48.2 percent there. The reported observed rate is reachable only near 65 ms and the reported null only near 45 ms, so the two figures cannot both come from one consistent procedure. That is the whole residual, stated as a measurement.

11e. Published notation authority

Norman Weinberg, "Guidelines for Drumset Notation", Percussive Notes, June 1994, page 20, section Ghost Strokes, plate Ex. 11 "Parenthetical Notes for Ghost Strokes". Read at 400 DPI from the image-only scan, which carries no text layer.

"It is recommended that parenthetical note heads be used to indicate ghost strokes... Ghost strokes written as parenthetical notes allow the use of this performance technique on any type of instrument (drums, cymbals, cowbells, etc.)"

Ex. 11 draws three parenthesised noteheads and the third is a parenthesised X notehead above the top staff line, a ghosted cymbal. The 1,316 ride ghosts are standard-sanctioned notation, so the sweep removed a convention rather than a defect.

5. Detector specification, stated completely

elementdefinition
audioone channel, arithmetic mean of the two stem channels, float in [-1, 1]
windownp.hanning(1024), applied unnormalised
hop256 samples at 44,100 Hz = 5.805 ms
magnitudeabs(rfft(frame * window)), no scaling by NFFT or window sum
fluxframe difference, negatives set to zero, then summed (L1) over band bins
frame 0previous frame taken as zeros, so flux[0] = 0
thresholda percentile of the strictly positive flux values
matchpresence: any single frame inside ±45 ms whose flux exceeds the threshold

The match rule is presence, and it is neither a local maximum nor a separately identified onset. Consecutive frames from one excursion can support a match, and one broad excursion can serve more than one window. The correct description of a positive is "matched the flux criterion".

6. The detector, reconciled

The 3.51× threshold gap was an operator difference. The band in the written spec was correct.

operatordefinition80th pct of positive flux, MP3, 2000-12000 Hz
B, the published onesum the band first, then take the positive first difference4.407
A, used in the first version of this pagedifference each bin, rectify, then sum14.303

Operator B puts 4.41 at percentile 80.01

Stereo MP3 read float32, unweighted channel mean to mono, no amplitude scaling, np.hanning(1024), 256-sample hop, frame-start timestamps, summed rFFT magnitude over bins 2000 to 12000 Hz inclusive, positive first difference, threshold at the 80th percentile of strictly positive flux. Operator A rectifies before summing, so nothing cancels and it runs about 3.2 times larger. Every result carrying threshold 15.487 came from operator A and is withdrawn.

A match means the maximum positive flux inside a ±45 ms window exceeds 4.41. It identifies no instrument and no discrete onset, so the wording throughout is "matched the flux criterion".

Alignment cannot account for the gap

A rigid offset sweep from -150 to +150 ms under the reconciled detector peaks at exactly 0 ms with 61.2 percent. No shift improves it, so 61.2 percent is the ceiling this alignment and this detector reach on this audio. The reported 71.3 percent sits above that ceiling.

Two numbers reproduce exactly and two do not. The threshold and the no-double-counting result reproduce. The observed rate and the null mean do not, and the residual difference is not yet located.

Meter eligibility, and what the interval means

A position at or beyond 4.0 quarter notes exists only in 5/4 bars. 200 of 1,724 ride notes (11.6 percent) sit there and are ineligible for every 4/4 destination. Eligibility checks meter and the anchored span together. Instrumentation, arrangement and local cymbal activity stay uncontrolled.

The 95 percent interval is the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of the null hit rate across randomisation draws. It describes variability under the randomisation only, and carries no uncertainty from alignment, detector validity, extraction quality or recording identity.

Reading it descriptively

At ghost positions the cymbal band shows a much lower transient peak, a far weaker attack relative to the local floor, a flatter decay profile and a darker spectrum with less 9 to 16 kHz content. Those four together are what residual decay from a preceding strike looks like, since cymbal highs shed fastest.

The design cannot separate decay from a genuinely soft stroke. Ghost positions are always the offbeats immediately after a plain strike, so decay sits there whether or not a soft stroke was also played. No classifier accuracy is claimed and no individual position is adjudicated.

What this supports

The old Ride (middle) transcription is extraordinarily dense, filling about 94 percent of available eighth-note positions. Its 1,316 ghost occurrences originate from one shared AntiAccent note definition rather than 1,316 independent editorial decisions.

Under the reconciled detector on this audio with this alignment, ghost positions matched the flux criterion at 0.98× the rate of the tested null procedures, holding across per-note and block-preserving randomisations. The residual gap against the reported 71.3 percent observed and 49.0 percent null is not located, and it is not the threshold and not a global alignment offset.

Section-matched spectral measurement shows lower transient peak and weaker attack-to-floor at ghost positions in 9 of 9 sections, with a flatter decay profile. That is consistent with residual decay and does not exclude a soft stroke.

Individual Ride (middle) strike validity remains unresolved. No deletion revision follows. Failing this detector does not prove Ben Dibden1's transcription wrong, and nothing measured here overturns a transcriber's reading of the record.

Is the hat stem even an instrument

This ran first. A pitch in one stem never proves that instrument played it, and this tab carries 1,724 notated ride events sharing the same band. Two measurements settle it, and neither one uses the tab or the alignment.

A circular test, caught

The result that looked decisive and was worthless

An early grid test scored the unexplained hat onsets at 0.02x to 0.04x of chance, far below the floor, which reads as a clean negative. It was circular. Residual is defined as more than 45 ms from any notated event, every notated event sits exactly on the grid, so the exclusion by itself forces occupancy below chance. The replacement pushes the null through the identical exclusion filter, and the same data then reads 1.14x to 1.34x.

The gap spread rules out a detector re-triggering on its own decay, which would sit at a fixed multiple of the 5.8 ms analysis hop. Leaders are the strokes the tab already notates. Followers are the ones it omits.

The eye overruled the count

267 became 147, and the notation got better

The first build wrote every follower onset, which is what the measurement literally says. Rendering bars 12, 31 and 45 through alphaTab headless and looking at them showed a size-12 cluster smearing into a six-note run of parenthesised heads along the snare line. That does not read as hit-rebound-rebound. The second build keeps the two loudest followers per cluster, and the same bars now render as one ghost either side of the hit.

The count was not wrong. It was answering a different question from the one the notation has to answer.

Accent plus normal, or normal plus ghost?

The 0.327 ratio measured each follower against its own cluster leader, so it only ever said the leader is louder. It is blind to which of the two carries the marking. Settling it needs a reference population built independently of the cluster structure, and the tab supplies one: all 193 notated snare events carry ghost false, staccato false, dynamic F and the same lane, with zero accents anywhere on the snare.

Verdict

A written accent normally runs 6 to 10 dB over ordinary. The leaders are 1.5 dB over, so the accent reading is refused. The followers are 9.2 dB under, short of the 15 to 25 dB a textbook ghost implies, so the ghost reading is supported and not proven. What the audio carries is an ordinary leader and a distinctly softer follower, and the parenthesis is the standard way to write a quiet unaccented stroke.

A finding that fell out of the reference work

94 of the 193 written snare positions carry no attack transient at all, only noise floor. That agrees with the lane table's 95 unsupported written events, reached by a different route. Roughly half the snare notation on this tab has no audio under it.

The kick: Brandon hears missing kicks and I could not find them

Recorded as a failed search, not as a refutation

Three methods and four bands, nothing clears the 1.5x floor, so nothing was written. His ear is the authority here and the detector is the weak instrument.

What would break the deadlock

One bar where Brandon hears a missing kick. With a single confirmed example the detector can be calibrated against a known-true instance and then swept, rather than searching blind at 87% false-positive density. The 356 grid candidates are listed with bar, beat, stem seconds, dB and decay in out/KICK-candidates.csv, and none were written to the tab.

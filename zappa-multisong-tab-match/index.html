Two more tabs put against their own recordings, and both of them hold

Keep It Greasey and I’m the Slime, scored note by note against isolated Moises stems on 2026-09-08. Same frozen detector as the Watermelon pass: 1024-point Hann, 256 hop, band-limited positive spectral flux, 95th-percentile local peaks, 30 ms refractory, 25 ms one-to-one tolerance.

10 lane verdicts 10 of 10 separate clock fitted on kick only wrong recording given equal freedom

The short version. Nobody listened to anything. Both tabs were expanded into note instances, both recordings were split into kick, snare, hat, tom and cymbal stems, and a clock was fitted on the kick lane alone. Every other lane was then scored on a ruler it never helped carve. All ten lane verdicts separate from a wrong recording that received the same fitting freedom.

The result that matters for the open Watermelon question: cymbals are confirmable by this machinery. Keep It Greasey separates its cymbal lane at 7.97 times and I’m the Slime at 1.87 times, so the Watermelon cymbal lane sitting at 1.45 is a fact about that tab rather than a limit of the detector.

Why playback was never part of this

The question that started the session was whether letting audio play out of the Mac would let the tab be graded by ear. It would not. Sound leaving the speakers arrives in the room. The measurement below reads waveforms as arrays and score files as XML, and it produces a number for every written note. That is the form the answer takes.

Watching a tab play back is worse than useless for this, because the playhead reports where the tab believes it sits. A reading taken from the tab and checked against a clock also taken from the tab will agree with itself and settle nothing about the record.

How a lane earns a verdict

  1. Expand the score into note instances. GPIF deduplicates note and beat definitions, so one definition can appear at many positions. Everything here counts instances.
  2. Fit the clock on kick alone. 24 windowed anchors, monotone in time, local offset chosen to maximise kick agreement.
  3. Score the other four lanes on that clock. They had no say in building it.
  4. Put a chance floor under every number. 20,000 uniform random times through the same matcher, so a dense stem cannot buy a high score with coincidence.
  5. Refit the whole thing on the wrong recording. Same anchors, same freedom. A fit that survives a different song is not measuring that song.

What the low confirm rates on Keep It Greasey do and do not mean

Greasey separates on all five lanes, and its absolute confirm rates stay low: kick at 29.4 percent and snare at 21.5 percent. Separation earns the clock. The remaining written notes have no stem onset inside 25 ms, and two causes are live. The song runs 161 bars of 19/16 out of 248, with 19 tempo automations, so a windowed clock is following real rubato. The detector also carries a 30 ms refractory, which merges two hits played closer than that into a single onset, and Colaiuta plays a great deal faster than 33 strokes a second in places. Neither cause is settled here, and neither licenses an edit to the tab.

I’m the Slime behaves differently for a readable reason. Its score carries 63 tempo automations across 80 bars, so the notation clock is already fitted almost bar by bar, and the anchor fit had little left to do.

What is not covered

  • Shiner BelieveYouMe has six songs with a full split kit and no tab on this machine. Asleep in the Trunk, So Far So, My Mirror Hates Me, Not Too Much, Broken Satellites and Jackie all carry kick, snare, hihat, toms and cymbals. Stems without a score cannot be scored.
  • No note was added, moved or deleted. This pass measures and reports. Any repair runs through the copy-tab rule rather than a revision to an original.
  • Ride and crash are not separated. Both fall in the cymbal lane, the way the Watermelon pass defined it.

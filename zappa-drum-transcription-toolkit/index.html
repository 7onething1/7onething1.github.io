1. The seven-stage skill stack

Each of these is a real skill with its own SKILL.md and scripts under ~/.claude/skills/. Invoke by name with the Skill tool rather than running the scripts by path.

Stage 1 · separate

/composite-stem-alignment

Builds one clean multitrack from several microphone captures of the same performance after each has been separated. Settles which mic owns which lane and applies one transform per mic.

"align the mics", "the offsets disagree", "which mic for the bass"
Stage 1 · separate

/multimic-stem-fix

Repairs a stem set where separation left phase cancellation or bleed between kit lanes. Runs before any onset detection, since a contaminated tom stem produces tom claims that are separator residue.

"the stems are bleeding", "kick and bass are cancelling"
Stage 2 · read

/perfect-stem-read

The governing document. Enforces the order stem event, instrument identity, score timing, Guitar Pro lane last, so lane binning happens only at final encoding. Carries the recording-identity gate and the wrong-recording alignment controls.

"read the stems into notation", "which drum is that"
Stage 2 · read

/drum-dynamics-from-audio

Fixes the velocity and articulation layer of a score that already has the right notes. This is the skill that turns quiet snare strokes into ghost notes by measuring the recording rather than by guessing.

"the dynamics are flat", "turn the quiet snares into ghost notes"
Stage 3 · notate

/stems-to-guitar-pro-drums

Turns drum stems into a Guitar Pro drum file ready for the Songsterr editor, recovering flams and ghost notes that a full-mix pass drops. This is the build step for a new tab.

"make a better drum tab from the stems", "turn the drum stem into Guitar Pro"
Stage 3 · notate

/audio-stems-to-midi

The cheaper first pass. Demucs plus crepe plus basic-pitch plus a librosa onset fallback for drums, with a theory JSON per song. Good for a rough map of where events sit.

"convert these stems to MIDI", "transcribe the stems"
Stage 4 · repair

/transcription-repair

Treats the best human transcription as the musical model and the stems as evidence testing single claims inside it. Carries the recording-identity gate, the alignment gate with wrong-recording controls, grid snapping, a two-hands playability check, and an append-only edit ledger.

"fix this drum tab against the stems", "the toms are in the wrong place"
Stage 4 · repair

/songsterr-drum-repair

Restores a drum staff on a community tab without damaging the rest of it. Written after the ghost sweep. Knows why accents come out as staccato and how to undo a bad revision.

"restore the ghost notes", "my revision broke someone's tab"
Stage 5 · standard

/notation-evidence-gate

Settles a notation-symbol dispute against the document that governs the symbol. Resolves ghost strokes, pedal hi-hat placement and X noteheads to the Weinberg drumset standard, then refuses any claim with no stated null model.

"what does this ghost flag mean", "did we prove that", "check the baseline"
Stage 6 · grade

/reference-class

Decides whether a number earned the right to ship. Six checks against a threshold cut from the population it filters, a density no musician would play, a filter that rejects nothing, and a missing chance baseline.

"is this number real", "the counts look high", "what is the null here"
Stage 7 · publish

/songsterr-upload

Puts a corrected Guitar Pro file onto Songsterr as a private tab synced to the real recording. Checks before import whether the file will lose tracks.

"upload this tab to Songsterr", "will this import lose tracks"
Stage 7 · publish

/songsterr-tab-guide

Builds a data-driven tab-guide page for a band's albums. This is the skill that produced the Zappa drum-transcription inventory covering three albums.

"which of Zappa's songs have no Songsterr tab", "are these tabs AI or hand-written"

Two rules govern all of them. The first is that a pitch in one stem does not prove that instrument played it, so identity comes before lane binning. The second is that a Demucs drums stem has to be split into kick, snare, toms, hat and cymbals before any judgement about a drum tab, because a full-kit stem cannot answer a per-lane question.

3. The Zappa drum program, current state

This is not a fresh field. A repair program has been running since late August, and it has its own front door at zappa-program-scope.

The damage that started it

A sweep on 2026-08-29 and 30 read Songsterr's parenthesised noteheads as an error and submitted revisions removing them across twenty Zappa tabs. Sixteen of those strips are the current published revision. Norman Weinberg's 1994 Guidelines for Drumset Notation, page 20, plate Ex. 11, prescribes exactly that notehead for a ghost stroke, and its wording covers cymbals as well as drums, which settles the ride-lane question too. That page was read on 2026-09-06 by rendering the image-only scan at 400 dpi, because pdftotext returns 12 bytes on it: source PDF, 15.2 MB, 12 pages.

Where the ghosts actually sat

Of the 3,842 removed flags, 1,549 are snare and 2,293 sit on cymbals, hats, kick, toms and tambourine. The largest single entry, Watermelon In Easter Hay at 1,317, carries no snare ghost at all.

The source archive

Hand transcriptions by professional transcribers live under ~/Projects/_outputs/zappa-drum-sources/ with ARCHIVE-STATE.md as the manifest. Seven folders: DRUM Magazine notation PNGs, the Cruise Ship Drummer charts, the Pete Sweeney Vinnie Colaiuta PDF, the Drumnet.ru charts, the Kasper Sloots per-album analysis with its section-letter bar maps, two academic theses, and the Rondo Hatton Report editions.

4.3 The audio on disk

Fifteen separated stems, 44.1 kHz stereo, each 501.84 seconds, at:

/Users/Shared/202 Keep It Greasey-D minor-142bpm-442hz/

The kit is already split into six lanes: kick, snare, hat, cymbals, toms, other_kit. The rest are backing_vocals, bass, lead, other, piano, rhythm, vocals, wind, and a metronome stem. The mixture sits beside them as a 320 kbps mp3.

The metronome stem is not a recording. It was measured on 2026-09-06 and found to be a beat tracker's output rendered to a wav: 1,171 bit-identical copies of one click over digital silence, every peak exactly 0.0 dBFS, and 55 distinct 300 ms window hashes each appearing exactly 1,171 times. Its 1,170 intervals all land on a 20 ms lattice, so it can only report tempo in quantized steps. The folder name's 142bpm is the same estimator talking, and it is one estimate rather than two.

4.7 The validation pass of 2026-09-07

Every lane was tested in both directions at passage level. Two tests that need no alignment carried the result: a bimodal peak-dB histogram for a quiet population, and a sixteenth-grid lock against a phase-uniform baseline.

The score agrees with itself from its own side. Resolving the two ways the file stores loudness gives the 629 flagged events a mean dynamic rank of 2.227 against 4.031 for the 794 unflagged, a gap of 1.804 ladder steps, Mann-Whitney z = -30.58. The flagged set sits one or two sixteenths apart on 62.89 percent of its within-bar gaps against 35.88 percent for the unflagged, with zero exceedances in 2,000 within-bar permutations. That is the notation and the audio arriving at the same picture with no alignment between them.
The alignment never closed, and that is the honest ceiling. Eight procedures place bar 102's downbeat between 197.88 and 198.20 seconds, and they are not independent, since six read the same fifteen-stem separation. The drum lanes then tested that map from outside: snare improves 17 to 4, tom 20 to 4 and hat 25 to 11 under a window slid one bar later, kick leans the same way, cymbal is flat. So 0 of 248 bars carry a surviving two-direction result, and no single notated event was matched to a single detected onset. Repairs applied: zero.

Two GPIF counting traps, both hit and both fixed during this pass. First, Notes are shared definitions, so counting <Note> elements returns 680 on a file carrying 14,444 events. Second, a ghost is a direct <AntiAccent>Normal</AntiAccent> child of Note, never a <Property name="AntiAccent">. Reading it as a Property returned zero ghosts on a file holding 629 of them, which is the same shape of error that let the original sweep proceed.

7. The order to run them in

  1. Nothing needs separating. The fifteen stems exist and the kit is already in six lanes. Skip stages 1 and 2 of the acquisition work.
  2. Read the Marc Atkinson chart. It is now rendered at 400 dpi across four pages and it is the only complete-song printed source. Bar-by-bar it is the strongest test available for the notated drum part.
  3. Run /perfect-stem-read on the six kit stems, holding the identity-before-lane order.
  4. Run /notation-evidence-gate on any symbol dispute the read produces, ahead of writing a claim down.
  5. Run /reference-class on every count before it enters a deliverable.
  6. Apply KIG-M001 only, onto s6862054, which is the staged copy. The other three findings have no evidence that closes them.

5. Where local and online disagree

This is the payoff of the cross-reference. The 1983 drought sign is the load-bearing miracle of the whole prophetic history, and the local telling and the published critiques do not describe the same weather.

What the local transcript says

From IHOPKC_s Prophetic History__7wk5hw5.txt, Bickle recounting Bob Jones speaking on May 28, 1983:

"there's a drought coming to this city. It'll be three months. And the drought is, is July 1st to October 1st."

He describes it as "the second worst drought on record" and reports the promised sign of rain arriving on August 23.

The revision sits inside the local file itself

The same passage carries an explicit adjustment of the start date:

"I think that day the drought started in June 1st, but when we looked at all the records, it was July 1st, October 1st."

What the 1990 documentation records

The full text of the Gruen report is now held locally, pulled from archive.org at 342,674 characters. Its Section I addresses this exact claim. Two kinds of material sit in it, and they carry very different weight.

The checkable part

The report cites National Weather Bureau readings from Richards-Gebaur Air Force Base, minutes from the church, showing over seven inches of rain in June 1983, which it describes as well above normal. It records the August 23 rainfall as under one third of an inch, and notes that six of the twelve rainy days in June exceeded it, with one day reaching 2.35 inches.

It also transcribes Bickle's telling at the time, in which the drought runs from June and August 23 brings three to four inches of rain.

Handle Gruen carefully

Gruen made partial concessions in a July 1, 1990 letter to John Wimber. He affirmed the document's essential accuracy and said he did not regret releasing it. He also conceded that some testimonies were disputable, retracted an account of a baby dying after a prophecy on the grounds it was undocumented, and completely exonerated Paul Cain of any negative charges, which contradicts parts of his own document.

The practical rule for using this source: the weather-station figures and the transcribed direct quotes stand, since none of those were retracted. His characterizations and conclusions do not, and nothing negative about Paul Cain from this document should be carried forward at all.

Session 3 answers the charge directly

Session 3 of the 2019 series was absent from this Mac in every format. Pulled from YouTube, it runs 73,356 characters and addresses both contested points head on.

On the drought window

The 2019 telling states the word was given on May 28 promising rain on August 23, and adds that the drought did not begin until July 1, with the remark that this was not understood at the time. That is the same July 1 start the other local transcript gives.

On the rainfall amount, Bickle records his own correction

The 2019 account explains where the larger figure came from. Immediately after the August 23 meeting he asked people present how much it had rained, nobody had measured, and a rough estimate of a couple of inches circulated. He told the story afterward as three or four inches. He says the group had assumed rain continued through the whole meeting when it had actually stopped, that fact-checking some years later showed it did not rain that much, and that he corrected it publicly, adding that a wrong statement needs correcting.

What the cross-reference actually establishes

  • The 1990 documentation caught a real discrepancy. Weather-station figures contradict the three-to-four-inch telling it transcribed, and those figures were never retracted.
  • The later telling concedes the point and explains it. The correction is in Bickle's own mouth in the 2019 session, not extracted from him.
  • The drought start date moved from June to July 1 across the two tellings, which is the shift a reader should be able to see for themselves.
  • The August 23 rain itself is described modestly in 2019, as a torrential burst in the minutes either side of a seven o'clock meeting rather than a sustained soaking.

Anyone writing from this material should carry all four points together. Presenting the 1990 figures without the recorded correction misstates the record, and presenting the correction without the figures hides what prompted it.

What the local Sam Storms interview concedes

The one 2019 transcript held locally is the outside-voice session. It names the Arrowhead Stadium prophecy as the thing critics press hardest on, and it grants that Bickle "made mistakes like all of us do." It frames its own purpose as answering critics, which makes it the natural hinge between the internal telling and the external record.

The through-line between 1990 and 2023

The Gruen material and the 2023 investigation land on the same mechanism, which is prophetic authority used to place a claim beyond examination. That is the axis to organize any writing against, since it connects the founding history to the collapse without needing either one to explain the other.

# Which Zappa drum tab to redo first

Over-Nite Sensation and One Size Fits All. Measured 2026-09-05.

## The two answers

**Over-Nite Sensation, redo Dinah-Moe Humm.** Six minutes and three seconds of Ralph
Humphrey that no human has ever written down. No printed chart, nothing of ours, and the
only coverage is two AI passes that both landed on the flattest lane profile measured
across either record: 0.56 tom notes a bar, the lowest anywhere here, against 5.5 hi-hat
notes a bar, the highest anywhere here. Two independent AI attempts agreeing on a flat
shape is the signature of a mechanical ostinato rather than a reading of what Humphrey
played.

Close second is Fifty-Fifty, also with no human reading and also six minutes. Its single
AI tab writes 1.7 toms a bar, inside the human range on this album, so its shape is at
least plausible. Take Fifty-Fifty first if the priority is the busier drumming rather than
the worse existing tab.

**One Size Fits All, redo San Ber'dino.** Five minutes and fifty-nine seconds with no drum
notation of any kind. No Songsterr tab carries a drum staff, no printed chart exists, and
MusicBrainz credits Chester Thompson on drum set, so the playing is definitely there. It is
the longest completely untranscribed track on either record. Then Sofa No. 1 and Sofa No. 2,
both also at zero with Chester Thompson credited. Then Florentine Pogen, whose human tab is
a 112-note stub across 132 bars carrying zero hi-hat notes.

The two records are short of coverage in different ways. Over-Nite Sensation has a tab for
every track and the gap is quality. One Size Fits All has four tracks with no drum notation
at all, three of them with drums credited on the recording.

## How the ranking was earned

Four signals, applied in order. First, is there any drum notation at all, paired with the
MusicBrainz drum credit so a track is only ranked first when the playing is confirmed
present. Second, has a human ever read it, taken off the `aiGenerated` flag in the meta API
rather than guessed from the author name. Third, notes per bar on a lane, used only to order
tracks that already tie on the first two, since a quiet tune genuinely has fewer tom hits.
Fourth, articulation marks as supporting evidence: every AI tab measured across both records
carries zero ghost notes, zero accents and zero staccato marks, and several human tabs also
carry none, so marks present means human and marks absent proves nothing.

Note counts exclude rests. Lane families follow the General MIDI drum map read off the
`fret` field, which is where Songsterr stores the drum lane. Tab durations are computed from
the tempo automation and the per-bar time signatures, and album lengths come from the
MusicBrainz release for each record.

## The metric that failed, recorded so it is not tried again

Bar-pattern uniqueness does not separate human work from AI work. The idea was that a lazy
machine pass would copy one bar across a whole section. Measured across fourteen tabs, the
human Zomby Woof tab is 36.8 percent unique bars and the human Inca Roads tab is 27.0
percent, and the AI Florentine Pogen tab is 85.5 percent and the AI Fifty-Fifty tab is 74.5
percent. The number tracks how repetitive the song is and says nothing about who wrote the
tab. It is excluded from the ranking.

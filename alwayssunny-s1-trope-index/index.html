S01E01 - The Gang Gets Racist

  • Logline: The Gang tries to rebrand Paddy's as a diverse hotspot by hiring a black promoter, but their clumsy attempts to prove they aren't racist backfire when the promoter turns the bar into a gay bar and they embrace it for profit.
  • Tropes used:
    • Cold-open pitch: Dee introduces Terrell as a cool friend, immediately triggering the Gang's scheme to exploit him for diversity points.
    • Escalating scheme: The Gang hires Terrell to attract a diverse crowd, then pivots to embracing the gay bar label when it makes money, overriding Mac's objections via a rigged vote.
    • Everyone-is-the-villain amorality: Each character uses Terrell or the gay bar for selfish ends, Dennis for attention, Dee for acting cred, Charlie to hit on a black woman to prove he's not racist, Mac for business ethics only when he's losing.
    • Dennis's self-serving calculus: Charlie and Dennis rig the vote by excluding Dee because she is not an owner, leaving Mac as the only opposing vote.
    • Later, Dee and Mac try to end the gay-bar experiment by getting Dennis blackout drunk.
  • Tropes subverted:
    • The Gang treats diversity as a marketing gimmick, using Terrell as a prop while skipping the usual 'learning lesson' arc entirely.
    • The gay bar pivot gets embraced purely for the money, draining any feel-good LGBTQ representation out of it.
    • Charlie's date with Janell ends in a violent rejection, torching the 'nice guy wins' trope.
  • Keywords: racism, diversity, gay bar, promoter, acting class, coffee shop, dominoes, tequila, vote rigging, black friends
  • Mirrored subplots: Both A-plot (using Terrell for diversity) and B-plot (Charlie's date with Janell) involve the Gang using black people as props to prove they aren't racist, both ending in failure and exposure of their true motives.
  • Callbacks: none
  • Pays off in: Sets the series template: the Gang's self-serving schemes, amoral pivots, and use of marginalized groups for personal gain recur in episodes like 'The Gang Gets Whacked' and 'The Gang Dines Out'.

S01E02 - Charlie Wants an Abortion

  • Logline: Charlie discovers he might have a 10-year-old son and schemes to get a blood test while the Gang exploits the abortion debate to get laid.
  • Tropes used:
    • Cold-open pitch: Gang argues about T-shirt slogans, establishing their petty dynamic as the plot starts.
    • Escalating scheme: Charlie's plan to get a blood test spirals into lying about a Big Brother program to impress the Waitress, then using the kid to pick up pro-life girls.
    • Everyone-is-the-villain amorality: Each character uses the abortion issue for selfish ends, Mac to get laid, Dennis to score with pro-choice women, Charlie to avoid responsibility.
    • Dennis's self-serving calculus: Dennis calculates that joining the pro-choice side has better odds for hookups, abandoning any pretense of conviction.
  • Tropes subverted:
    • Abortion debate as serious issue: The Gang treats it as a pickup opportunity, not a moral dilemma.
    • Fatherhood redemption arc: Charlie doesn't bond with the kid; he uses him as a prop and threatens violence.
    • Pro-life/pro-choice stereotypes: Mac's 'hard-core' pro-life activist is actually a murderer fantasist, and Dennis's pro-choice stance is purely predatory.
  • Keywords: abortion, blood test, fatherhood, pro-life rally, pro-choice rally, waitress, big brother program, t-shirt slogans, free clinic, pickup scheme
  • Mirrored subplots: Both A-plot (Charlie's fake son) and B-plot (Mac/Dennis exploiting rallies) involve the Gang using a social issue (fatherhood/abortion) as a cover for selfish goals, with each side mirroring the other's hypocrisy.
  • Callbacks: none
  • Pays off in: Sets the template for the Gang treating public moral issues as personal opportunity, and for using children as props (e.g., 'The Gang Gets a New Member').

S01E03 - Underage Drinking: A National Concern

  • Logline: The Gang turns Paddy's into a safe haven for underage drinkers, then gets blackmailed into attending a high school prom.
  • Tropes used:
    • Cold-open pitch: The Gang rationalizes a scheme by framing it as a social responsibility
    • Escalating scheme: from watering down drinks for profit to being blackmailed into prom
    • Everyone-is-the-villain: each character pursues selfish motives (Dennis profits, Dee relives high school, Mac and Charlie seek popularity)
    • Dennis's self-serving calculus: 'We water down the drinks, jack up the prices' as a controlled environment
  • Tropes subverted:
    • Moral lesson: The Gang pretends to care about underage drinking but only exploits it
    • Prom fantasy: Dee's dream of being popular is undercut by her back brace and mom's insults
    • Age-appropriate romance: Dennis calls Mac creepy for liking young girls, then dates an 18-year-old
  • Keywords: underage drinking, high school prom, blackmail, back brace, keg stand, statutory rape, popularity, safe haven, watered down drinks, Trey
  • Mirrored subplots: Both A-plot (the bar scheme) and B-plot (Dee's prom date) involve the Gang exploiting high schoolers for validation and profit, with each member's high school insecurities driving their actions.
  • Callbacks: none
  • Pays off in: Sets the series template for the Gang's morally bankrupt schemes, blackmail dynamics, and character-specific insecurities (Dee's back brace, Mac's glue-sniffing, Dennis's creepy age preference).

S01E04 - Charlie Has Cancer

  • Logline: Charlie fakes a cancer diagnosis to manipulate the waitress into dating him, but the Gang's competing schemes to exploit his 'condition' backfire when the truth comes out.
  • Tropes used:
    • Cold-open pitch: Charlie reveals 'cancer' to Dennis, setting the scheme in motion
    • Escalating scheme: Mac/Dennis plan to get Charlie laid via pity, Dee blackmails for a punch, Charlie lies to waitress, Dennis pays her $250
    • Everyone-is-the-villain: Charlie fakes cancer, Dennis steals the waitress, Mac punches Carmen, Dee extorts Dennis
    • Dennis's self-serving calculus: Dennis calculates that a 'cancer gold mine' can be exploited for sex, then pivots to paying the waitress
  • Tropes subverted:
    • Cancer as a noble struggle: Charlie uses it as a manipulative tool, not a genuine crisis
    • Sympathetic friend response: The Gang's 'help' is entirely self-serving (get Charlie laid, avoid work, settle grudges)
    • Romantic payoff: Charlie's lie fails; the waitress only sleeps with Dennis for money
  • Keywords: fake cancer, pity sex scheme, Lance Armstrong bracelet, trans panic, hate crime, Artemis audition, Coyote Ugly, egg metaphor, Dennis steals waitress, Carmen punch
  • Mirrored subplots: A-plot (Charlie's fake cancer to get the waitress) and B-plot (Mac/Dennis's scheme to get Charlie laid via pity) both rely on exploiting Charlie's supposed illness for personal gain; both fail when the truth emerges.
  • Callbacks: none
  • Pays off in: Sets the template for the Gang's willingness to fake illnesses (e.g., S02E01 'Dennis and Dee Go on Welfare'), use pity for sex (S03E15 'The Gang Dances Their Asses Off'), and escalate lies that collapse (S04E13 'The Nightman Cometh').

S01E05 - Gun Fever

  • Logline: After their safe is stolen, the Gang buys a gun to protect the bar, leading to a botched sting operation that ends with Charlie accidentally shooting himself.
  • Tropes used:
    • Cold-open pitch: Mac proposes buying a gun after the robbery, and the Gang immediately escalates.
    • Everyone-is-the-villain amorality: The Gang frames Colin as the thief with no evidence, plans to shoot him, and Charlie gets shot due to their incompetence.
    • Dennis's self-serving calculus: Dennis orchestrates a sting by leaving a false trail about money in the register, but the plan backfires.
    • Escalating scheme: From buying a gun to investigating to a planned shooting, each step is more reckless.
  • Tropes subverted:
    • Gun as protection: The gun is immediately misused for target practice, then stolen by Charlie, and finally causes an accidental shooting.
    • Competent investigation: The Gang's 'Hardy Boys' investigation is laughably inept, with no real evidence against Colin.
    • Successful sting: The trap fails completely, resulting in Charlie getting shot instead of catching the thief.
  • Keywords: gun fever, robbery, sting operation, accidental shooting, Colin suspect, Hwang rent, Hardy Boys, Paddy's Pub, safe theft, Charlie shot
  • Mirrored subplots: The A-plot (buying a gun for protection) and B-plot (Charlie's debt to Hwang) both involve characters acquiring dangerous objects (gun, gun used as threat) that backfire, highlighting the Gang's inability to handle responsibility.
  • Callbacks: none
  • Pays off in: Sets the series template for the Gang's incompetent crime-solving and accidental violence, later echoed in episodes like 'The Gang Gets Held Hostage' and 'The Gang Solves the Gas Crisis'.

S01E07 - Charlie Got Molested

  • Logline: The Gang discovers that Charlie may have been molested by a gym teacher, leading to a chaotic intervention and a scheme involving the McPoyle brothers to exploit the situation for a lawsuit.
  • Tropes used:
    • Cold-open pitch: The Gang reads a news article and immediately jumps to the conclusion that Charlie was molested, setting the episode's scheme in motion.
    • Everyone-is-the-villain amorality: Each character prioritizes their own agenda, Mac's jealousy over not being molested, Dennis and Dee's pseudo-psychology, the McPoyles' lawsuit scheme, over Charlie's well-being.
    • Escalating scheme: From a 'supportive' intervention to a fake molestation claim to a police report, the plan spirals out of control with no regard for consequences.
    • Dennis's self-serving calculus: Dennis and Dee treat Charlie's trauma as a clinical problem to be solved with their 'expertise,' using buzzwords like 'intervention' and 'acknowledgment' without empathy.
  • Tropes subverted:
    • The intervention trope: Instead of a sincere support gathering, it becomes a farce where Charlie is pressured to 'show on the doll' and his family is manipulated.
    • Victim empowerment: Charlie's eventual decision to go to the police is not about justice but about ruining his own life and getting revenge on the McPoyles.
    • Molestation as serious topic: The episode treats the subject with dark humor, focusing on the Gang's selfish reactions rather than genuine trauma.
  • Keywords: molestation, intervention, McPoyle brothers, lawsuit, Coach Murray, psychology, blow job, doll, police report, sodomy
  • Mirrored subplots: The A-plot (Charlie's alleged molestation) and B-plot (Mac's jealousy over not being molested) both revolve around the same event, with Mac's insecurity mirroring the Gang's exploitation of Charlie's trauma.
  • Callbacks: none
  • Pays off in: Establishes the McPoyles as recurring antagonists, most directly in 'The Gang Gets Held Hostage' (S03E04), while Charlie's implied trauma history remains part of the show's later psychological texture.

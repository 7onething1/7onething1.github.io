Does re-deriving from the stem work?

Three Appleseed Cast songs, each re-transcribed from its own guitar stem with basic_pitch on the ONNX model, nothing carried over but the container. Scored on the meter clock against a cut validated leave-one-song-out. 30 August 2026.

Three songs, three different answers.
Playability improves every time. Audio accuracy does not.
Adriatic clears the cut with margin. Confession moves ten times its starting separation and stays under. Blind Man's Arrow moves the wrong way. The method is 1 for 3 on audio and 3 for 3 on playability.

Where the ceiling is

Blind Man's Arrow is the one song here with a human Songsterr tab on disk, and it is the most useful row on the page. A person reaches 41.0% own alignment on that recording. Our best re-derivation reaches 23.1%. Same tool, same stem, same clock, a gap of 17.9 points.

That gap comes from what basic_pitch detects, rather than from the measure or the grid. Adriatic reaches 30.3% and clears; Confession reaches 18.0% and does not. The songs that clear are the songs the detector happens to hear well, so the method's success is currently a property of the audio rather than of the pipeline.

The finer grid helps playability on every song tested, and the two-staff split resolves the remaining unplayable beats without moving a single pitch. That part of the pipeline is working and is independent of the audio question.

What this does not say

The audio number measures detector agreement, not pitch accuracy. basic_pitch produced these notes from these same recordings, so a rise measures how far its note starts agree with librosa's onset detector. None of these files has been promoted into 09_MATCHED_38.

The cut is validated, and that is what makes 1 of 3 meaningful. Separation ≥ 7.7 with z ≥ 5.0, established leave-one-song-out over 162 known-wrong pairings across 54 songs: the same 7 confirm in-sample and held out, 1 false positive in 162, threshold stable between 5.1 and 7.7.

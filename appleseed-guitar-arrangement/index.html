1. What was asked

Take the single guitar part in each tab, arrange it across two or three guitars, keep every hand inside five frets, and allow a string skip only when the shape is an octave. Compare the result against other transcriptions online where any exist.

2. What the source files looked like

Every one of the four tabs already carried two guitar staves. Not one beat on any staff exceeded five frets, so the five-fret rule was satisfied on arrival. The other rule was broken 700 times: that many beats attacked two or more strings with an unused string sitting between them, and none of those were octave shapes.

3. How the arrangement works

A skip means the pick crossed a string it never struck. Two guitarists collapsed onto one staff produce exactly that shape, so the repair gives the notes back to a second pair of hands rather than re-fingering the beat.

Each failing beat is tested for the smallest set of notes whose removal leaves the rest playable exactly where the source wrote them. Those notes move to a third guitar staff. Nothing else is touched, so a healthy beat keeps the string and the fret the transcription gave it.

Three guitars was the ceiling, and three was never needed for any single beat: the solver reported zero beats requiring a third hand and zero beats with no legal shape at all. Guitar 3 exists to receive what two hands cannot hold at one instant, and it carries between 42 and 202 beats per song.

The position gate reads: every note in a beat that was already healthy kept the string and fret the source gave it. That is the check that catches a blanket refret quietly rewriting a performance while every other number looks fine.

Two traps the build hit and cleared

GPIF Notes are shared definitions. One <Note id> is referenced by many beats, so editing it in place rewrote every beat pointing at it. The first run moved 130 of 205 notes on one staff while only 36 edits had been issued. Copy-on-write brought that to 23.
Beats are shared too. A beat reused at two onsets lost its note in both places when one of them shed it, which showed up as 55 notes placed on Guitar 3 and never removed from the origin. Beat-level copy-on-write took the orphan count to 0 on all four files.

Our A Tree For Trials tuning is independently confirmed. Our file declares pitches 38 45 50 55 59 64, which is D A D G B E. The Ultimate Guitar transcription declares the same tuning in words.
No independent transcription supports capo 7 on Blind Man's Arrow. One declares no capo, one declares capo 6, and our file declares capo 0. The rejected capo 7 that the skill keeps as its standing proof of an ergonomically attractive and musically wrong answer stays rejected.

The human transcribers stayed inside five frets across the material that parsed cleanly, and they wrote string skips of their own. That matches the reading this arrangement takes: a skip is ordinary in a tab, and it often marks a second player.

No tab content is reproduced here. The comparison reads tuning, capo, fret spans and skip counts out of the pages, then discards the notation.

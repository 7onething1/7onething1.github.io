1The abort condition was wrong, and testing it was the whole job

The queued design carried an instruction to give up: if held-out error stayed near four notes a bar, Moises separation is the limit and the answer is a different separator. That instruction needed its own measurement, because a counting failure and an evidence failure look identical from outside.

The test asks one question no detector can beat. Inside the 43 bars that carry a full ride, can any envelope tell a written stroke position from the midpoint between two written stroke positions? Same bars, same audio, same background, 843 positions against 785 midpoints.

The cymbal stem beats the kick stem on its own instrument

Separating ride strokes from the gaps between them scores 0.823. Separating kicks from silence in the kick stem, an instrument nobody disputes, scores 0.750. Moises separation is not the limiting evidence here, and following the abort condition would have thrown away a working signal.

2Stage one of the design is inert on this stem

The design named guitar leakage regression the largest expected gain, since the lead stem measures the contaminating source directly. Fitting a per-bin model on 12,520 frames of trusted audio at least 120 ms from any notated cymbal, and never inside a disputed bar:

QuantityMeasuredConsequence
mean complex coherence, lead into cymbals, 2–16 kHz0.061complex subtraction impossible
mean magnitude correlation0.070magnitude suppression has nothing to suppress
cymbal energy explained by the lead0.8%the stage is dropped

The premise was reasonable, because the bleed is real. A lead-guitar attack far from any notated cymbal raises cymbal-stem flux in 3–10 kHz to 1.75x the random-frame median at z 5.4. A notated ride stroke reaches 2.48x. Guitar bleed therefore arrives at 70.7% of the amplitude of a real ride stroke.

The design's own fallback was taken instead. The guitar measurements stay in as rejection features, and a contaminant a linear model cannot predict is still a contaminant.

3Eight stages, one dropped, one rebuilt

1 · droppedGuitar leakage regression. 0.8% of energy explained, so nothing to cancel. Guitar kept as rejection features.
2 · keptHPSS on the cymbal stem, median filters 31 frames by 17 bins, percussive mask.
3 · keptSuperFlux onset function on the percussive residual, frequency max-filter width 3, lag 2.
4 · rebuiltMultiband proposals, 2–5 / 5–8 / 8–11 / 11–16 kHz, whitened against a 0.5 s running background. Merge widened from 60 ms to 160 ms.
5 · kept23 per-candidate features: band flux, flatness, entropy, bandwidth, centroid, high-band ratio, percussive share, rise and decay, plus three cross-stem guitar features.
6 · keptRegularized logistic regression, group cross-validation by whole bar, never a random stroke split.
7 · keptLearned ride template, median 16-band patch from −40 to +120 ms, scored against both a trusted-attack and a rejected-attack template, rebuilt inside every fold.
8 · keptCounting last, 90 ms refractory, no snapping to the written grid.

4The defect was my proposal geometry, not the stem

The first rebuild returned 3.35 notes a bar and a candidate-level held-out AUC of 0.594, near chance, which read as confirmation of the abort condition. Separability had already ruled that out, so the fault was mine.

Proposals were merged at 60 ms and then labelled against written strokes at a 90 ms tolerance. A sixteenth at 53–59 bpm is about 270 ms. 6Two controls the gate never asked for

The stem played backwards fires just as often

Running the identical model and threshold on the time-reversed stem keeps the spectrum and the level distribution and turns every attack into a decay. It returns 889 detections against 846 forward, a ratio of 0.95x.

A null that returns exactly zero has to be asked whether it could have returned anything else

The first run of that null reported 0 reversed detections and a 846x ratio, which would have been the headline. Reversed detection times were computed by indexing the full frame-time array with proposal-array positions, pushing every hit into the first 12 seconds of the reversed stem and outside the 43 bars. A fairness check asking whether the null had any chance to fire returned 889 of 901 reversed proposals above threshold, which is how the bug surfaced.

Median offset −11.8 ms, median absolute offset 24.6 ms, interquartile range −33 to +12 ms. A sixteenth here spans 268 ms, so 134 ms is the widest window in which a match can be unique at all, and 80 ms is 30% of a sixteenth.

A tight unimodal distribution 5x above a shifted control is not what noise looks like

The tempo map is piecewise constant across bars of 4.1 to 5.6 s, so a written sixteenth is placed by interpolation across a whole bar while a drummer is not metronomic inside one. The detections are the strokes. The 40 ms criterion was tighter than the clock's own precision, and it is corrected to 80 ms with the table above as the reason rather than by moving a goalpost.

The 3x target itself needs a reference class

Reversed cymbal audio genuinely contains roughly twenty energy events per bar, so a counter that counts events would legitimately count them. 3x was set without a reference class. It is not relaxed here. It is recorded as a number that needs one, and the question went out with the measurements attached.

Where the detector and the eye disagree, the eye decides

An earlier pass read one bar per panel on a shared dB scale with post-song silence as the control and found bar 105 genuinely silent. The detector returns 32 there, its highest count anywhere. Bar 105 is silent. That 32 is a false-positive count in a bar known to be empty, and it sits alongside the 1.30x reversed ratio as evidence about the detector rather than about the music. Where the two agree, at bars 17, 51, 99 and 100, the agreement adds nothing the eye had not already established.

No ride note is written to any of them. Agreement with a render is consistency, and consistency is not the standard for adding notes to a published tab. The matched-share half of that gate is now met at a defensible 80 ms. The direction half is not, and it is recorded as an adjudicated blocker rather than waved through.

10Two provenance defects, both now repaired

The alignment file cannot referee the clock that built it

Scoring the published 53–59 bpm tempo map against the snare anchors gives 659 ms rms and 4.1 s peak-to-peak, while flat 56.000 gives 39 ms and 212 ms. Read at face value that reverses the whole drift finding. Those anchors were produced by matching onsets under a flat-56 assumption, and every anchor value divides exactly by 60/56 into an integer quarter.

The non-circular test scores notated kick and snare against onsets in the kick and snare stems, each clock given its own best scale and offset. The repaired map reaches 0.890 at z 9.2, flat 56 reaches 0.573 at z 5.5, and the repaired map's winning scale is exactly 1.0000. The drift finding stands.

Repaired. A replacement alignment now carries 749 anchors (489 kick, 260 snare) matched inside 60 ms, against the old file's 97. Median absolute residual 18.5 ms, and the residual trend across the whole song is 0.078 ms per quarter note, about 37 ms end to end, so no systematic stretch remains. The original file is untouched and a README beside it records why it cannot referee a clock.

The 4.19 notes per bar baseline has no surviving script

Both scripts the ledger names are absent from all four project tool folders, so the number cannot be regenerated. An absolute-threshold detector of the same shape, rebuilt in this session over the same 43 bars and the same 843 strokes, scores 8.26. The honest comparison is within-session: rebuilt baseline 8.26 against pipeline 0.84. A number whose script is gone is a quotation, not a measurement.

What happened

Songsterr draws a ghost note as a notehead inside round brackets. That is the standard rendering and it carries real musical information, the quiet stroke a drummer plays between the accented ones. A sweep run on 2026-08-29 and 2026-08-30 treated those brackets as a defect and removed them across twenty Zappa tabs.

What the sweep believed

Parentheses read as a let-ring marking to a player, so a ghost should carry the staccato dot at velocity 31 instead. Every bracketed notehead on a drum staff is therefore wrong and should be converted.

What the record shows

The bracket is Songsterr's own ghost glyph, and the tabs were already correct. The house rule about staccato dots governs tabs generated from stems in Brandon's pipeline. It does not govern published community transcriptions.

The complaint that surfaced it. Songsterr users reported that 53 ghost notes had been removed from the Terry Bozzio track on Zoot Allures. Revision r8769199 is real, its description says exactly that, and it is still sitting in the queue on that tab.

Root cause, three layers

1. A house rule applied outside its scope

The rule came from stems-to-guitar-pro-drums, which builds a tab from an isolated drum stem. In that context Brandon specified the staccato dot at velocity 31 for a ghost. The sweep carried that rule onto existing community tabs written by other people, where it had no standing.

2. The wrong element name hid the evidence

Guitar Pro's GPIF format spells a ghost note <AntiAccent>Normal</AntiAccent>. A search for <Ghost> returns zero matches in every file. That zero was read as proof that ghosts were absent from the file layer, when the files carried them the whole time.

3. The audit measured note counts and missed the loss

The preservation check compared pitch, rhythm and position across 120,579 beat events and found zero differences, which is correct. Articulation was outside what it measured, so the audit reported a clean sweep while the ghost flags were being dropped.

It was already called wrong on 2026-08-31. The handoff document states that the parentheses sweep was the wrong unit of work and says not to resume it. No revision was withdrawn after that, and Songsterr offers no withdraw control, so sixteen of them went on to publish.

What is already fixed and what is left

Zomby Woof s412162 is repaired. Revision r8904052 restored eight ghost notes on snare across bars 6 to 9, with let-ring at zero and every other lane unchanged. The method is proven and the tooling now works.

  • Sixteen live tabs need a restoring revision that puts the AntiAccent flags back, one tab per pass, each verified against Songsterr's own data.
  • Zoot Allures needs its queued revision neutralised by a corrected one, since the live tab still holds all 53 ghosts today.
  • Watermelon In Easter Hay is the largest single loss at 1,317 flags on the Vinnie Colaiuta staff, and its revision claimed they were ride and crash rather than ghosts, so that reading needs checking against the audio before restoring.
  • The four never-submitted tabs need nothing.

A claim of mine was wrong. I reported that the tab holds no pedal hi-hat events anywhere. The tab holds 23 of them, across bars 6, 7, 8, 9, 15, 17, 18, 41, 42, 43, 44, 99, 100 and 102. Bar 1 is the single omission. The lane already belongs to the tab's own vocabulary, which makes the bar 1 addition a gap fill.

Bar 5 accents sit on notes one and three, and Songsterr cannot draw them

Brown beams bar 5 as five quintuplet groups of five. The measured accent glyphs, width 18 and ink 65, sit above notes one and three of every group. The tab marks note two and nothing else.

What the tab marks

slots 1, 6, 11, 16, 21

five marks, each on note two of its group

What Brown prints

slots 0, 2, 5, 7, 10, 12, 15, 17, 20, 22

ten accents, notes one and three of every group

I shipped that change and then took it back out. Revision r8905411 moved the marks to Brown's positions and doubled them to ten. Reading the revision back from Songsterr's own JSON showed why that was wrong: a Guitar Pro <Accent> arrives in Songsterr as staccato. The note schema for this part holds only fret, string, staccato, rest, ghost and tie. There is no accent field. Zooming the rendered bar confirms it: ten small round dots, no wedges. A staccato dot says short, and Brown's wedge says loud, so the edit swapped one articulation for another. Revision r8905491 puts bars 5 and 16 back to exactly what the tab held.

What that means beyond this bar. Accents cannot be expressed on a Songsterr drum staff through a Guitar Pro import at all. Loudness lives on the beat instead, as ff, pp, mf and mp, and bar 5 already carries an alternating pp and ff shape the transcriber put there. Any future accent work on these tabs has to go through beat dynamics, and that is a larger change than this pass covers.

The barring source count was wrong too. Kasper Sloots' single 10/16 belongs to a passage he transcribed from 1:39 to 2:10, framed by 7/8 and 5/4 and repeated three times against two keyboards. Brown's page is headed @ 0:00. The two describe separate music, so Zappa Analysis never voted on the opening bar at all. For the opening the split is Brown and Bedard on 5/16 plus 5/16 against Drumnet and the tab as published on 10/16. Brown's interior barline is solid and full height, and he beams the two groups apart. Drumnet beams the passage unbroken and numbers its next system from bar 4. Both readings are structural, so this is a source based editorial adjudication, not an objective correction, and the page labels it that way.

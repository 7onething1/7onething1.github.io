What happened

Songsterr draws a ghost note as a notehead inside round brackets. That is the platform's own ghost notation and it carries real musical information, the quiet stroke a drummer plays between the loud ones. A sweep run on 2026-08-29 and 2026-08-30 read those brackets as a defect and removed them across twenty Zappa tabs.

Sixteen of those revisions are the current public revision today. 3,842 ghost note flags are missing from tabs the public reads right now. Two more were rejected by Songsterr moderators before they could publish, and two were overtaken by other people's later revisions.

Root cause, three layers

  1. A house rule applied outside its scope. The staccato-dot-at-velocity-31 rule comes from stems-to-guitar-pro-drums, which builds a tab from an isolated drum stem. The sweep carried it onto community tabs written by other people.
  2. The wrong element name hid the evidence. GPIF spells a ghost <AntiAccent>Normal</AntiAccent>. A search for <Ghost> returns zero in every file, and that zero was read as proof the ghosts were absent.
  3. The audit measured note counts and missed the loss. The preservation check compared pitch, rhythm and position across 120,579 beat events and found zero differences. Articulation sat outside what it measured.

The rule itself was withdrawn on 2026-09-05, in the pipeline as well as here. It had survived as advice while this page described its damage. Measured on Songsterr that day: a GPIF ghost arrives as its own ghost field and draws as a parenthesised notehead, and a GPIF <Accent> arrives as staccato and draws as a dot. So the parentheses are the platform's ghost notation, and the dot is a duration mark standing in for a dynamic one. sd_writegp.py now writes the native ghost flag, and sd_verify.py now fails any file that writes a ghost as a dot. Brandon's dislike of parentheses stands as a display preference for his own files.

Bar 5 accents sit on notes one and three, and Songsterr cannot draw them

Brown beams bar 5 as five quintuplet groups of five. The measured accent glyphs, width 18 and ink 65, sit above notes one and three of every group. The tab marks note two and nothing else.

What the tab marks

slots 1, 6, 11, 16, 21

five marks, each on note two of its group

What Brown prints

slots 0, 2, 5, 7, 10, 12, 15, 17, 20, 22

ten accents, notes one and three of every group

I shipped that change and then took it back out. Revision r8905411 moved the marks to Brown's positions and doubled them to ten. Reading the revision back from Songsterr's own JSON showed why that was wrong: a Guitar Pro <Accent> arrives in Songsterr as staccato. The note schema for this part holds only fret, string, staccato, rest, ghost and tie. There is no accent field. Zooming the rendered bar confirms it: ten small round dots, no wedges. A staccato dot says short, and Brown's wedge says loud, so the edit swapped one articulation for another. Revision r8905491 puts bars 5 and 16 back to exactly what the tab held.

What was tested, and what stays unconfirmed. One import path was measured: a Guitar Pro 7 .gp pushed through the editor's importRevisionInput on tab s412162, read back as revision r8905411, part 9. In that file, on that path, a GPIF <Accent> arrived as staccato and the note schema carried no accent field. Whether some other import format, another instrument set, or Songsterr's own editor can express a drum accent is unconfirmed, and nothing here tested it. Loudness lives on the beat instead, as ff, pp, mf and mp, and bar 5 already carries an alternating pp and ff shape the transcriber put there. For this tab and this path, accent work has to go through beat dynamics, and that is a larger change than this pass covers.

The barring source count was wrong too. Kasper Sloots' single 10/16 belongs to a passage he transcribed from 1:39 to 2:10, framed by 7/8 and 5/4 and repeated three times against two keyboards. Brown's page is headed @ 0:00. The two describe separate music, so Zappa Analysis never voted on the opening bar at all. For the opening the split is Brown and Bedard on 5/16 plus 5/16 against Drumnet and the tab as published on 10/16. Brown's interior barline is solid and full height, and he beams the two groups apart. Drumnet beams the passage unbroken and numbers its next system from bar 4. Both readings are structural, so this is a source based editorial adjudication, not an objective correction, and the page labels it that way.

What is left

  • Sixteen live tabs need a restoring revision, one tab per pass, each verified against the pre-sweep revision rather than against the stripped one.
  • Restore every attribute the sweep touched, not the ghost flag alone. The sweep wrote a staccato dot and set velocity to 31 as it cleared each ghost, so a restoring pass has to put back the ghost flag, remove the substituted dot, and return the velocity, all measured against the pre-sweep baseline.
  • Keep other people's later edits. Several of these tabs have had legitimate revisions since. A restoring pass rebases onto the current public revision and changes only the articulation fields.
  • Watermelon In Easter Hay is the largest single loss at 1,317 flags on the Vinnie Colaiuta staff, and its revision claimed they were ride and crash rather than ghosts, so that reading needs checking against the audio before anything is restored.
  • Verify the revision that actually becomes public, per tab, after moderation. A submission is not a repair.
  • Keep It Greasey and Zoot Allures need nothing. Both stripping revisions were rejected and are blocked.

Watermelon In Easter Hay, verdict

This was the one tab held back by a caveat. Its stripping revision claimed the 1,317 marks were ride and crash at forte, so not ghost notes. Both halves of that reasoning fail against the data.

What r8768414 claimed

"They are all on ride and crash at forte, so they are not ghost notes and no dynamic was changed."

What the pre-sweep data holds

1,316 sit on ride (MIDI 51) and 1 on crash (MIDI 49), so the lane half is right. Every one of the 2,091 notes on that staff carries dynamic: null. There is no forte written anywhere. That reading was invented.

The placement is a perfect accent grid

The ride runs as a continuous sixteenth ostinato. Counting every ride note by its sixteenth slot in the bar:

16th slot in barPlain noteheadGhost notehead
0, 4, 8, 12 (the quarter pulse)4072
every other sixteenth11,314

The ride is struck plainly on each quarter and ghosted on every sixteenth between. A ghosted ride note is alone in its beat 99.4% of the time; a plain one shares its beat with kick or snare 60.8% of the time. That is the notation telling a drummer to keep the ride quiet except where it lines up with the backbeat.

Verdict: all 1,317 are genuine and must be restored. The caveat is cleared.

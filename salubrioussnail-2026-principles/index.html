1. Why Situational Cards Belong in Your Commander Deck

Commander decks have pre-engine and post-engine phases.Once your card draw engine is online, drawing a few situational or 'bad' cards is less detrimental.By categorizing cards into buckets and using probability, you can safely include a limited number of janky or late-game cards without harming early consistency.
Early Game Priority
  • In Commander, decks have a pre-engine phase where you set up card advantage engines.
  • Once you are drawing multiple cards per turn, individual card quality matters less.
  • Cards like fast lands are excellent early because later filtering provides other land options.
Card Buckets
  • Separate nonland cards into three buckets: premium enablers, situational cards, and general utility.
  • Check the probability of drawing your premium enablers and your situational cards.
  • Hypergeometric calculators help assess how often you will draw specific cards.
Running Bad Cards
  • Protection spells, life gain spells, and combo pieces are examples of acceptable situational cards.
  • Running around five situational cards keeps the odds of drawing multiple early very low.
  • If situational cards exceed ten, you risk starting with multiple dead draws.
  • It is okay to include pet cards as long as you manage the numbers.

2. Two Rules for Hidden Commander Success

The video teaches two essential rules for building a hidden commander deck in Magic: The Gathering.First, include cards that serve as analogs to your hidden commander to provide redundancy.Second, minimize the footprint of synergy cards by ensuring they are useful even without the hidden commander.Using Sibsig Ceremony as a case study, the builder demonstrates how choosing a commander like Athreos, Shroud-Veiled reduces the need for dedicated support.
Hidden Commander Basics
  • A hidden commander is a card in the 99 that serves as the deck's main build-around.
  • You will not always have access to your hidden commander, so the deck must function without it.
  • Some hidden commanders like Sunforger are easier because the cards they work with are already good in the deck.
  • For tricky hidden commanders like Sibsig Ceremony, you need to carefully balance synergy and independence.
Find Analogs
  • Include cards that can perform a similar role to your hidden commander.
  • Analogs provide redundancy in case your hidden commander is removed or not drawn.
  • Astral Slide has an analog in another color, effectively giving you multiple copies.
Minimize Footprint
  • Use synergy pieces that are independently useful or serve multiple purposes.
  • Sunforger's targets are instants you would want to play anyway, so they don't eat up deck space.
  • Selecting a commander like Athreos that naturally combos with Sibsig Ceremony reduces the need for extra support cards.

The video argues that Magic's current all one-off sets prevent immersion and mechanical depth, while the old three-set block model had its own flaws like weak later sets.It proposes an alternative approach and highlights a missed opportunity from 2024 as a case study.
Current Model Issues
  • The current one-off set model feels rushed and prevents players from becoming immersed in a plane.
  • Storylines feel disconnected because the narrative never lingers on a single plane.
  • Mechanical themes cannot be iterated across multiple sets, losing depth.
  • Every plane must present all its ideas in one set, leaving no room to explore themes later.
Old Block Model Issues
  • Third sets in three-set blocks often felt cramped and poorly conveyed the lore, like Dragon's Maze.
  • Middle sets sometimes felt like filler with odd mechanics, such as Born of the Gods.
  • Later sets in a block consistently sold worse than the first set.
  • Designing satisfying limited environments across multiple sets was challenging.
Proposed Improvements
  • A specific missed opportunity in 2024 serves as a case study for better set design.
  • The video argues for an alternative set structure that avoids the pitfalls of both models.

5. Using Fulcrum Cards to Expand Deck Synergies

This video teaches how to add subthemes to Commander decks through fulcrum cards that bridge archetypes.It advises evaluating cards based on their floor and ceiling to avoid dead draws.Deck space and theme compatibility are crucial when combining different strategies.
Fulcrum Cards
  • Fulcrum cards tie together multiple subthemes by rewarding one theme with payoff from another.
  • Examples include Throne of Geth, Displacer Kitten, and Professional Facebreaker.
  • They create points of fusion between different archetypes.
Evaluating Card Floor and Ceiling
  • The floor is how a card performs in a subpar situation.
  • Low floor cards like Dross Scorpion or Astral Slide can be dead without support.
  • Use floor ceiling calculations to decide if a card fits, as shown with Ret to my mystic.
Deck Space and Theme Compatibility
  • Some fulcrum cards require more deck space to be effective, like Doorkeeper in Arcades.
  • Themes with many natural fulcrum cards, like artifacts and ETB, feel compact.
  • Combining themes with less shared DNA, like cast from exile and landfall, is harder but doable.
  • When combining unique themes, understand your goals and plan accordingly.

The Aggro Goal
  • Aggro decks aim to pressure life totals to influence opponent decisions.
  • Low life totals make opponents play conservatively and avoid using life as a resource.
  • The narrator wanted opponents to constantly think about their life total.
Failed Attempts
  • The initial Liesa deck felt more like a stax deck than an aggro deck.
  • Insufficient card draw made early versions boring to play.
  • Adding initiative cards complicated gameplay and distracted from life pressure.
  • Switching to Bruna added political value but still missed the original goal.
Lessons Learned
  • A clear deckbuilding vision prevents distraction by tangential mechanics.

Criteria for a Ban

Power and Tax Issues

10. Common Commander Deckbuilding Mistakes and Solutions

Many Commander decks suffer from unfocused goals, vulnerability to hate, and lack of proactivity.The video teaches how to increase focus by choosing specific payoffs and practicing restraint.It suggests improving durability through haste, flash, and ramp instead of relying only on protection spells.It also stresses the need for a clear proactive win condition.
Focus Your Goals
  • Center your deck on a specific synergy and define clear win conditions.
  • Avoid including cards just because they share a mechanic if they pull the deck in different directions.
  • Practice restraint and exclude cards with marginal synergy to maintain focus.
  • Increase specificity to transform a loose collection of payoffs into a coherent strategy.
Increase Durability
  • Reduce reliance on narrow protection spells and use versatile options like counterspells or flicker effects.
  • Equip key creatures with boots to grant hexproof or shroud plus haste.
  • Grant haste to important creatures to ensure they act before removal.
  • Use flash enablers to cast setup pieces on opponents' end steps, dodging sorcery speed removal.
  • Ramp to cast synergistic pieces together, minimizing the window for interaction.
Be Proactive
  • Build a deck that actively pursues a win condition rather than only controlling opponents.

11. A Framework for Evaluating Ramp in Your EDH Deck

The video argues that ramp serves both curve smoothing and total mana increase, and that decks vary in which they need.It provides methods to determine the right amount of ramp by analyzing mana values and using hypergeometric calculators.A benchmark of 18 ramp spells is suggested for consistency.
Ramp's Roles
  • Ramp serves two separate functions: curving out and increasing total mana quantity.
Curving Out
  • Plan your ramp to sequence spells optimally in the first five turns.
  • Match ramp mana costs to the mana values of your non-ramp cards.
  • Consider vulnerabilities: mana dorks die to board wipes, so weigh risk versus reward.
  • Tapped lands often fit into curves without penalty, especially on turn one.
Increasing Mana Total
  • Expensive commanders need ramp to overcome command tax and missed land drops.
  • Decks with high card draw but slow speed need extra mana to deploy all their cards.
  • Prioritize permanent ramp like land ramp or mana rocks for long term mana growth.
How Much Ramp
  • Use a hypergeometric calculator to see the chance of drawing ramp by a desired turn.
  • As a starting benchmark, 18 copies of a role gives high consistency.

Step 6. A correction protocol on the validated metric, built and refused

With pitch, ownership and attack-string all disqualified, the only surviving audio metric is attack support. A new tool, bar_support_audit.py, localises it: rather than judging a note, it asks which BARS have no acoustic support while their neighbours do. A single unmatched attack proves nothing, since an onset detector systematically misses attacks inside a held distorted chord, and a region carries information a note cannot.

It ships with a decoy control, the same tab shifted 3.7 seconds. Two constructions were tried and both were refused:

Cause: 718 onsets across 251 seconds is one every 0.35 seconds. The DTW map compresses any input into that distribution, so a tab shifted by nearly four seconds still falls beside an onset. Onset density this high leaves the measure no headroom, which is a property of dense distorted rhythm guitar rather than a coding error. The tool is kept, exits nonzero, and emits nothing whenever the decoy keeps up.

Terminal state for per-note audio correction on this song. Six measures were run against controls. Two are valid at song level and localise nothing: map alignment at 5.75x and attack recall at 5.70x, both of which separate the right tab from a wrong one across the whole song. Four cannot license a change: pitch separation is inverted, attack precision is inverted, ownership lost to its control at -0.0115, attack-string evidence lost at -0.215, and per-bar attack support lost at +4.7 against a +15.0 threshold. Nothing here says the tab is wrong. It says these tools cannot tell, on this material, and each of them says so itself.

Step 8. The mechanism, found, and the gate that catches it

Step 7 said the defect was in the measure's construction, which was as far as the evidence went at the time. It goes further. The cause is a degenerate DTW map, and it is measurable directly.

Mutiny vs its own guitar3273.5%0.940666.0%

A wrong pairing yields few anchors, so interpolation pins a quarter of the tab outside the anchor span and collapses up to 284 consecutive attacks onto a single audio time. Every one of those notes is then compared against the same frame. When that frame is a sustained chord, a large share of written pitches agree with it, while the shifted-time control falls on a different and sparser frame and collapses. That is the large spurious separation, appearing on the pairing that is most wrong.

beat_map.py accepted both wrong maps. Gain +11.24 and +44.1, over its +8.0 threshold, because it judges alignment gain against a random control and nothing else. audio_accuracy_audit.py then measured pitch through those maps. No stage asked whether the map was healthy enough to carry a per-note question.
Built and validated: map_health_gate.py. It reads a beat_map.py --json file and fails a map on clamped share above 12%, a collapse run over 120, fewer than 0.85 distinct audio times per attack, or under 85% span coverage. Thresholds sit between the two measured groups with margin. Validated 4 for 4: both correct pairings PASS, both wrong ones FAIL on all four criteria at once. A failing map still supports song-level alignment and attack recall, which is what those metrics were always measuring.

This changes the standing conclusion. Pitch agreement is not inherently inverted. It was being fed maps that could not carry it, on pairings nothing was screening. Whether it becomes usable on a map that passes the health gate is the next thing to measure, and it now has a gate in front of it.

All four continuous features fail, and the reason explains which class of signal survives. A DTW map warps tab time onto onset time, so both the real times and the shifted control times end up beside onsets. Sampling any continuous spectral feature at either set returns the same distribution, which is what the near-1.0 gains show. The searchable space narrows to discrete event matching rather than continuous spectral features. Onset strength, spectral flux, transient clarity and attack envelope sharpness are all ruled out on this material with proper controls, so a future attempt should start from event-level questions rather than from frame-level ones.

Step 11. The one hard fault is a false positive, and the tab is right

Playability needs no audio, so the audio blocker never applied to it. Dumping the tie state across bars 3 to 8 resolves the single hard fault on the file.

Bar / beatString 4, the moving lineString 3, the pedal
bar 3, beat 99s4f8 tie origin, p65s3f5 tie origin, p58
bar 3, beat 100s4f8 tie dests3f5 tie dest
bar 3, beat 101, the flagged beats4f10 tie origin, p67s3f5 tie origin, p58

bar 7 to 8, beats 107 to 110s4f10s3f5 held, with s2f5 and s1f3

String 3 fret 5 is a pedal held by an unbroken tie chain from bar 3 through bar 8 while the melody moves on string 4 through frets 8, 9 and 10. One finger holds, another reaches. That is index and pinky across adjacent strings at position 5, which every guitarist does. The gate models it as four fingers fitting inside four frets at once, which is the wrong model for a two-finger pedal and reach.

Every span-reducing alternative breaks a tie the music depends on. Legal placements in Drop C are pitch 58 at s0f22, s1f15, s2f10, s3f5, s4f1 and pitch 67 at s1f24, s2f19, s3f14, s4f10, s5f5. The gate's own suggestion s2f10 + s4f10 collides with the s2f5 tie held from bar 7, which is why applying it took the file from 1 fault to 5. s2f10 + s3f14 needs string 3, which carries the pedal. s4f1 + s5f5 moves the pedal onto string 4, the string carrying the moving line.
So The Mutiny has zero genuine playability faults. The correct action was to refute the flag rather than edit the file, and nothing was changed. The gate already prints "held span", so it knows the note is held; it just does not use that fact when choosing the limit. The fix it needs is a held-pedal exemption: when one note of a pair is inside a tie chain and the other is the only moving voice, judge the span against a two-finger reach rather than the flat four-fret limit.

2. Broken Satellites copy tracks, local only

The local file carries Rhythm Guitar Copy next to Rhythm Guitar, and a track literally named Copy next to the bass, plus a five-string standard B E A D G bass under a Drop C guitar. None of that is on Songsterr: the live revision returns five clean tracks with a Drop C bass. So the duplication happened locally. A part filled by duplicating another part is a duplication rather than a transcription, so run the octave-copy gate on the local file, and re-pull the live tab as the reference.

3. Asleep in the Trunk is in 3/4, and the book says 4/4

Songsterr's transcriber entered 3/4 explicitly. The MIDI metadata says 4/4 and so do the book cards. That is unresolved, and it changes every bar number in the song, so it has to be settled before bar-level correction on a 264-bar chart. The 2026-06-03 audit left the caveat that the song may open in 3/4 and move to 4/4.

4. Two songs cannot yield guitar evidence at all

Asleep in the Trunk and Jackie were separated to four stems, so their guitars are mixed into other. No amount of care on the tab will produce a per-attack guitar attribution from a stem that also contains keys and everything else. A pitch found in other does not prove the guitar played it. Both need re-separation to a six-stem model before they enter the queue.

Every difference from the prior gets classified as supported correction, unsupported change, or unresolved ambiguity.

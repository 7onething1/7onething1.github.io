## The four corrections that changed the work

1. **The greenhouse was missing from the entire package.** The August 6 draft described an outdoor
   bed garden and never used the word greenhouse, even though the July 22 proposal was built around
   upgrading one that already stands. It now carries its own irrigation zone across the budget,
   narrative, metrics, and procurement spec.

2. **The site address was contradicted between drafts.** July 22 placed the site in Raytown, Eastern
   Jackson County. August 6 used Kansas City 64127. Kansas City is correct, confirmed against the
   KVC Missouri location page and the SAM entity record for The Niles Home for Children. That puts
   the site inside KCMO city limits, which PLANT requires.

3. **The $30,000 anchor grant does not apply.** The Eastland Giving Circle covers Eastern Jackson
   County east of I-435, suburban Jackson County outside Kansas City, and Cass County. The site is
   inside Kansas City and west of I-435. It was carried only because of the Raytown error. Kept on
   the Funding Roadmap as a closed question rather than deleted.

4. **A required assessment was treated as an optional call.** The City requires sites working with
   Kansas City Community Gardens to complete a KCCG site assessment before applying and attach it to
   the application. That is the longest lead time in the package and is now a leadership decision
   due August 11.

Eight further corrections are recorded on the What Changed sheet, including a cited URL that returns
404, four missing City requirements added to the timeline, and a budget rebuilt from eight round
numbers into a component specification.

## Verified August 6, 2026

PLANT program rules read directly from the City of Kansas City PLANT page: three funding tiers
($5,000 for sites under two years, $5,000 for previously awarded projects, $10,000 for existing
farms three years or older), 50 percent paid upfront, nine-month spend window, KCMO city-limits
requirement, KCCG assessment rule, award obligations, and the scoring panel that includes the
Lincoln University Extension horticulture educator.

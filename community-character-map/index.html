The Good Place

24 / 25 — Highest rated
CommunityThe Good PlaceWhy it maps
Jeff WingerEleanor ShellstropReluctant lead arrived under false pretenses; treats moral self-improvement as a con for two seasons before the ensemble teaches them to mean it. Both close every group debate.
Britta PerryTahani Al-JamilStatus-anxious almost-leader getting humbled. Name-dropping is the moral-superiority tic Britta's PETA-activism is.
Annie EdisonChidi AnagonyeHigh-achieving rule-follower whose moral rigidity is the engine of half the comedy. Both freeze on decisions.
Abed NadirJanetThe non-human ensemble member whose brain runs on a different OS, narrates the structure from inside. Closest to Abed than any character on TV.
Troy BarnesJason MendozaSweet, dim, loyal. Jacksonville monologues are Troy's "shirts versus skins" in different costume. Friendship with Janet replicates Troy-Abed beat for beat.

Dean PeltonMichaelInstitution's chaos-engine authority figure: self-pity, costume changes, reluctant care.

Plot engine

The architect's deception (S1), the rebuild (S2), the human-test (S3), the new neighborhood (S4). Every season is a workplace assignment from Michael forcing the four humans plus Janet to stay in proximity. The Good Place's weekly engine: "what does Michael's plan require."

Best 5 episodes for a Community fan — in watch order

  • S01E01 Pilot — Eleanor arrives; the Jeff-hiding-his-credentials parallel is immediate.
  • S02E06 The Trolley Problem — Chidi's ethics class becomes the study-group table equivalent. Annie's season 2 pedagogy-as-power arc, one episode.
  • S02E10 Best Self — Four parallel confession-ritual-send-off beats. Structural DNA of Community's "Remedial Chaos Theory."
  • S03E10 Janet(s) — Every human inhabits Janet's void. Most literal mirrored-subplot move on TV.
  • S04E14 Whenever You're Ready — Four parallel goodbye arcs. The finale Community would have written if it had a clean ending.

Britta PerryLisa MillerThe driven sharp woman closest to Jeff's equal and the moral self-correcting voice.
Abed NadirMatthew Brock (Andy Dick)The weird ensemble outlier whose brain runs on a non-standard OS. Andy Dick's performance: "behave as if the script is delivered in real time to my surprise" — the closest live-action analogue to Abed's flat-affect reactions.

Chang/HickeyBill McNeal (Phil Hartman)Chaos antagonist inside the ensemble. Manic theatricality of Chang plus grumpy-veteran ego of Hickey.

Plot engine

News cycle plus Jimmy James's whim. Every episode: Jimmy walks in with an order ("we are buying a boat, we are sending Joe to Antarctica, take a personality test"). Structurally identical to Community's class assignment plus Dean Pelton's announcement. The newsroom is the study room with worse furniture.

Best 5 episodes for a Community fan — in watch order

  • S02E01 Airport — Bottle episode, entire cast stuck in one location. The "Cooperative Calligraphy" equivalent.
  • S03E02 Rose Bowl — Jimmy James subplot runs parallel to Dave-Lisa subplot on the same "pride is the problem" beat.
  • S03E17 Bill Moves On — Three reaction-to-Bill's-departure subplots rhyming on "we don't say goodbye well." "Mixology Certification" structure.
  • S04E16 Houses — Lisa moving in with Dave, Bill buying a car, Joe refurbishing a generator. Three parallel "I am building something that will fall apart" beats.
  • S04E20 Sinking Ship — Titanic-bottle parody. Every ensemble pairing has its own romantic-doomed subplot. Community's genre-episode in 1998.

Plot engine

Weekly parks-department mission: build the park, harvest festival, recall election, sewage merger. Structural identity with Community's Spanish-project engine is exact. Parks runs more goal-driven across seasons; Community runs more cyclical.

Best 5 episodes for a Community fan — in watch order

  • S03E02 Flu Season — Leslie sick, Andy sick-pretending, Tom sick-overreacting. Three-strand rhyme on "the show must go on."
  • S03E07 Harvest Festival — Season-long project pays off, every ensemble member contributes one moving piece. Community's paintball structure in small-town governance drag.
  • S03E13 The Fight — Leslie-Ann fight, Ron-mustache fight, Andy-test prep fight on the same "you have to stop hiding from a hard conversation" beat.
  • S05E05 Halloween Surprise — Ben's job offer, Ron-Diane meeting kids, Andy's audition: three "the thing you've been working toward arrives and now what" arcs.
  • S07E04 Leslie and Ron — Bottle episode, two characters, one room, three years of unspoken conflict. Community's "Cooperative Polygraphy" without the polygraph.

Cheers

22 / 25 — The Template
CommunityCheersWhy it maps
Jeff WingerSam MaloneThe template. Cocky ex-pro washed up below his stated ambition. Both close bits. Both date the smart woman and deflect through one-liners.
Britta PerryDiane Chambers → Rebecca HoweDiane is ideological-overcorrector Britta. Rebecca is status-anxious-overcorrector Britta. Sam-Diane-then-Rebecca is Jeff-Britta-then-Annie.
Abed NadirCliff ClavinNon-standard-OS ensemble member through a structural-recognition tic. Trivia-as-personality is Abed's media-literacy-as-personality.
Troy BarnesWoody BoydSame character. Sweet, dim, loyal handyman/bartender. Woody's "Mr. Peterson" greetings are Troy's "TROYYY" reactions.
Pierce HawthorneNorm Peterson (partial)Ensemble's oldest member, present every week as ambient moral weight. "Drunk-rich-on-bar-tab" energy partial match.
Shirley BennettCarla Tortelli (inverted)Dignified-strong-moral-framework slot. Carla's Catholic-Italian morals are Shirley's evangelical-Black morals. "I will hit you for that comment" energy identical.
Dean PeltonFrasier Crane (pre-spinoff)Pre-spinoff Frasier is the ensemble's pretentious-authority figure whose inappropriate fixations drive half the bits.

Plot engine

Bar bottle plus the dating-Sam triangle. Every episode opens with bar regulars and adds a guest with a problem. Community's study-room bottle plus a Spanish-class subplot. No school-wide engine to escalate, so the bottle is tighter and the ensemble generates plots from within. Cheers is Community's direct ancestor — the show that proved the ensemble bottle engine works at 22 minutes without a premise beyond "people who know each other."

Best 5 episodes for a Community fan — in watch order

  • S01E01 Pilot — Sam-Diane dynamic established cold; Jeff-Britta DNA is here in 1982.
  • S01E03 Sam's Women — Diane makes Sam feel guilty about his ex-girlfriends; the Jeff-Britta status-leverage move, original.
  • S05E09 Thanksgiving Orphans — Every ensemble member's "I have nowhere to go" beat rhymes against every other's. The closest Cheers gets to Community's bottle structure.
  • S05E21 The Bartender's Tale — Sam-flashback episode structured as an unreliable-narrator bit. Community's "Abed's Uncontrollable Christmas" narrative-form ancestor.
  • S08E25 An Old-Fashioned Wedding — Three weddings in one episode. Mirrored-subplot construction at its cleanest.

Stems for the whole set

The tape is mono and it carries most of its energy in the low end, which sits outside what Demucs was trained on. Separating it raw drops the guitar stem into bass register, measured at 93.9 percent below 250 Hz on song 04 in the earlier pass. So the mix gets the tilt first, then separation runs, and the inverse tilt goes back on every delivered stem so the tone matches the source.

Two models run on each song. htdemucs_6s carries the only guitar head, so guitar and other come from there. htdemucs holds more capacity per source across four heads, so bass, drums and vocals come from that one. Every stem sums to one channel, since the source channels are bit identical. Five stems per song at 44.1 kHz 24 bit.

The drum chair rotates through this band. A silent drums stem on a take means an empty chair, and it is not a separation failure. Songs 04, 05 and 06 already came through this pipeline last night and their numbers are on the stems page.

The set is 32 songs and 148 minutes, running as two parallel chains. Output lands in ~/Projects/_outputs/band-practice-stems/STEMS/, one folder per song.

How a cut gets found

Four signals vote on every second of a take. Chroma self similarity catches a new tonal center. MFCC self similarity catches a new arrangement. Local tempo catches a step in the pulse that holds. Short level dips catch the breath between pieces. A checkerboard kernel sized in minutes turns the four into one novelty curve, since a song boundary is a block change and a chorus is not. Peaks land at least 150 seconds apart, then snap to the quietest instant within ten seconds so the cut falls in a breath.

The number on each card is that combined score. Above 0.55 reads strong, above 0.40 reads likely, below that reads thin and wants your ear.
